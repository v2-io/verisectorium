# «decision»
A choice among alternatives, made at a branch point by a party accountable for it, on a stated basis; it stands by that party's backing while its basis holds, and no evidence makes it true.

## Intro
Two kinds of statement are adjudicated in a verisectorium, and everything else it says is built from them. A **truth claim** is right or wrong against something beyond the say-so of whoever states it, and it stands by evidence: «acts|def/act» of finding out, which accumulate, and which anyone with a sound method may add to or overturn. A decision is the other kind. Nothing found out can make it true or false; it is made, by someone who answers for it, and it stands by their backing. Accepting a piece, promising one, routing a chunk, leaving a known defect in place, choosing where effort goes, choosing a definition or a design: all are decisions.

## Definition
A decision is an «act» of making so. It records:

- **What was decided, and about what.** A piece (accepted into canon; kept as it was beside a change), a slot (promised), a chunk (where it goes), an owed item (waived), the work (where effort goes, and until when), the ontology (how this verisectorium works), or the subject itself (a modelling or design choice). When the subject itself is decided, the wording of the choice is content, a veritoid of a decision kind, and this act answers the «dimension» that asks who stands behind it.
- **Who decided.** The accountable party: one or more seats (the steward, the lead, the author of a piece, a council) with standing to decide it, each held at that moment by an «actor». Where the ontology asks for unity, all of them. A seat outlasts the actors that hold it, so a decision is sustained, reopened to, and changed by its seat.
- **Its basis.** What it rests on, recorded as what it read: pieces at their versions, the truth claims taken as grounds, each at the warrant it then had, the assumptions it names, and any condition it states for reopening. A basis element reopens the decision by computation only if it is a piece or a warrant; an assumption worth naming is written as a piece, a claim or a question, so that a check against it reaches the decision.
- **The importance it was decided at.** How much rested on it, and for what, when it was made.

It may also record the alternatives weighed, who was consulted, and who was told.

A decision **stands** while its basis holds and its party has not said otherwise. When something in the basis fails (a ground falls or is contradicted, a piece it read is rewritten, a named assumption is shown false, a reopening condition is met, or more comes to rest on it than it was decided at) the decision is **reopened**. A reopened decision backs nothing: what it backed is owed on the «frontier», addressed to its party, who sustains it on the new basis or decides again. Reopening is not reversal; what was decided stays written until a decision changes it.

A decision made in advance for a class of cases, and applied by an ‹instrument›, is a **rule**. The ontology holds the rules. Applying a rule is computed; the rule is a decision like any other, with a party and a basis, and an automated action is a decision someone made earlier.

### Invariants
- Who held each seat when it was made is read from the harness, never typed. A decision recorded in another's name is a false record of a person; where one mind records what another decided, it records its own ‹testimony› that they said so, with their words. Only its party corrects a decision, or someone shows it was never made.
- It is reopened exactly when its recorded basis fails. A change that leaves the basis standing reopens nothing, however large; a change to the basis reopens it, however small.
- It warrants nothing about whether what it concerns is true. A decision about a truth claim decides what to do in light of the evidence (count it, build on the claim, hold it out of canon); the evidence stays recorded as evidence.
- It is not a lock. Any actor may put a reasoned case for deciding otherwise to its party; a decision cited as a reason not to think is being misused.
- Who has standing to make it, and how much agreement it needs, are the ontology's to declare, and may rise with importance.

## Discussion
- **Why decisions are a second atom and not a kind of evidence.** The two are built differently. Certainty in a truth claim changes with accumulated evidence and needs nobody's permission. A decision is made at a branch point on some basis and needs an accountable party. Treating a decision as evidence produces "it was ratified, so it is true"; treating evidence as a decision produces "we agreed it holds". Both are the same error from two sides, and keeping two atoms is what makes it visible.
- **Each rests on the other.** A decision's grounds are truth claims, each with its own warrant. Every piece of evidence is at bottom a fact someone or something attested (that this was run, read or said, by whom, against what), and where the trail under such a fact stops is itself a decision not to carry it further. The division continues in principle and stops in practice where carrying it further costs more than it could change.
- **Every adjudication ends in a decision.** A tribunal on whether something is true gathers and weighs evidence, and then someone decides: that the evidence is enough, that the piece is accepted, that the question goes back. What it writes may be evidence on a truth claim; closing it is a decision. So every item on the «frontier» waits on a decider, and the model says only who has standing: an agent, a council, a person or a rule may fill the seat, and which is chosen later, per verisectorium.
- **Importance.** How much agreement a decision needs follows how much rests on it, and agreeing on that often has to come before agreeing on anything else. Deciding cheaply in order to learn is good practice: a pilot under one arbitrarily chosen regime is often the fastest way to gather the evidence. Recording the importance a decision was made at is what keeps that safe, because a cheap decision that later bears more than it was made to bear is reopened and not silently promoted to law.
- **What the record of a decision is for.** It shows the succession of choices as understanding changed, and it gives a later mind the path for revisiting one: work out why it was decided, check that reading with its party, and propose the change. It is not there to tell anyone who the decision-maker is so that thinking can be handed back to them.
- **Decisions about the subject and decisions inside the work.** A syntax choice in a specification is content with a decision behind it; keeping an assertion after a rewording is a decision with no content of its own. Where the verisectorium is the project, the two are sometimes one act, and nothing here requires telling them apart beyond recording what was decided.

## Examples
- Accepting a definition into canon in a verisectorium whose ontology asks for the steward and the lead in unity: one decision, two parties, basis the definition at its version and the definitions it uses being accepted. Reword the definition and the decision is reopened.
- A theory's rule that a derived claim is accepted once a mind other than its author has re-derived it and a second means has been tried: a decision made once, in the ontology, applied by an instrument to every claim.
- A steady state reached with three small findings still open: three waivers, each a decision with its reason, shown in the steady state's conditions.
- A chunk of an audit marked "already present in the result it bears on": a decision about where the chunk goes, with the result's current version in its basis.
- A specification's choice of one separator over two others: content stating the choice, and a decision recording who chose, on what grounds, which assumptions would reopen it, and that it was decided as a small matter.
- "Concentrate on the scope chapter for now": a decision about the work, with an expiry.
- "RESOLVED in canon, do not re-open", written in a note: not a decision. It has no party, no basis and no way back.

## Historical Context
### Etymology
From *decidere*, to cut off: the alternatives not taken are cut away. It shares its root sense with the *-sect-* of verisectorium, which is apt, since deciding where to cut is the first decision made about any influx.

### Where it came from
- The two atoms were the stated primitives of the plan this model began from (`msc/notes/02-2026-11-04-plan.md` §A0: truth claim, decision, attested fact as the floor, attribute; "Everything else is composed of these"). Joseph, 2026-11-04: "Does the plan do a good job of building from the primitives? (decisions w/ backing and truth-claims w/ certainty and evidences)?" His sort of the same day by what builds each: certainty "changed with and requires (accumulated) evidence"; decision "decided at an inflection/branch point with certain basis-- requires accountable parties"; and "after all, a warrant or basis is a truth-claim with various certainties". Through the first and second cores the pair survived only as two sorts of act. Joseph, 2026-11-10: "the two critical atomic components that everything else ends up reducing to... even attributes reduce to the machine making an attestation of a truth claim and automated actions are decisions".
- What backing consists of is his list of 2026-08-14 (quoted in `msc/notes/18`, "On what backing is"): who is accountable, who knows, who was given a voice, on what grounds and assumptions, what would require a revisit, and the path for revisiting; with his account the same day of agents "arguing historic authority instead of underlying grounding".
- That every adjudication ends in a decision, that the decider is a seat filled later, and the place of importance are from the councils work of 2026-11-03/04 (`~/src/arch/msc/councils/research/dialog-for-kernel-2026-11-04.md`, read to its line 1008 and Joseph's turns after): "There is always a decision to end, one way or another"; "treat each vsect transition-edge as a queue that is drained by a 'decision-maker'"; "There is different level of unity required for different levels of importance".
- In the estate's own verisectoria the decision is the most fully worked record there is (deciders, consulted, considered options, assumptions, reopen-when, in three unrelated shapes), while the model had it only as a word used in passing.
