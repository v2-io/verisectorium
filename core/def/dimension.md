# «dimension»
One question asked of a piece, declared with what it asks, of which kinds, against what, whether its answer is found out or decided, what produces the answer and by what means, how the warrant is read, and what passes along references; a dimension is a constraint.

## Definition
A dimension's answer for a piece is always one of two things. Where the dimension is **found out**, the answer is a truth claim about the piece ("it follows from its premises", "it is stale", "it says what its source says"), and it stands by evidence. Where the dimension is **decided**, the answer is a «decision» about the piece ("accepted", "kept as it was", "ratified by its owner"), and it stands by the backing of its party. Every dimension is one or the other; a question that seems to be both is two dimensions.

The ontology declares each dimension with these things:

1. **asks**: the question, in plain words.
2. **of**: which «kinds|def/kind» it is opened for; a dimension may be of an «actor» as well as of a piece.
3. **against**: what a piece is held to. Where that is the same for every piece (logic; the world; its own canon) it is named once. Where it varies per piece (the source this quotes; the segment this explains; the target this is important for) the dimension says how a piece names it, usually by a «reference» of a declared kind. An act answering the dimension reads what fills this, which is why a change in it lapses the answer.
4. **found out, or decided**.
5. **produced by**: which «acts|def/act» answer it and whose have standing, read from the «actor»'s record. For a found-out dimension it also names the **means** that count, each a different way of asking the question: for validity, following the written derivation step by step; deriving the statement afresh without it; setting the statement against its neighbours' results; building an instance; a formal proof. The first two differ in what they share: every mind that follows one written derivation has read the same thing. For a decided dimension it names the party with standing and how much agreement is needed.
6. **read as**: how the current answer for a piece, and its warrant, are read from the acts that still count and concern it. This is a rule, a decision made once in the ontology and applied by computation: the highest rung reached, a narrowing or contradiction winning until answered; a conjunction of named means; the latest outcome; a count of independent lines; a declared numeric projection; for a decided dimension, the standing decision of the party. Where no rule can weigh the evidence, the weighing is routed to a decider. What arrives is a decision about what the verisectorium holds for now (count this, hold that out, ask again); the evidence stays evidence, and the decision does not become the answer.

And optionally **passes along**: for each reference kind, what this dimension does to pieces that reference the piece, and which dimension of the target it reads when the target's kind does not open this one (a claim resting on an imported result reads the import's *source*). Validity *caps* along predicated-on: a piece's effective warrant is the least of its own warrant and the effective warrants of everything it is predicated on, a missing target counting as the lowest; freshness *stales* along narrates; most dimensions pass nothing.

For each piece and dimension, three things are kept apart. The **answer** is what is claimed, measured or decided: follows, stale, reproduces, reach of forty, accepted. Where the question is one the content itself speaks to, the piece's **assertion** is the answer its written content carries, with the conditions it carries it under ("exact", "conditional on (S1)–(S5)", "to leading order" are content: they say what is claimed); where it is not, the answer is the outcome of the acts. The **warrant** is how well the current answer stands, read from the record and never written: for a found-out dimension, what the evidence gives, by which means and along how many independent lines; for a decided one, whether a decision by a party with standing is on record with its basis holding. *Never asked* is a warrant.

A ‹determinant› is a dimension the ontology names as part of what being right consists of for this verisectorium; better is read over the determinants and nothing else, weighted as a projection declares over segment kind × determinant, the canonical projection's weighting being the default. A weight is never a property of a piece. The other dimensions are fitness: adjudicated as carefully, organizing the work, and silent on whether a piece is right. The mark is the ontology's, and the same question may be a determinant in one verisectorium and fitness in another. The mark cuts across found out and decided: freshness is found out and usually fitness; in a specification, who stands behind a rule is decided and is a determinant.

An attribute is a found-out answer of the simplest sort: an ‹instrument› attests a measurement (word count, age, reach, churn), anyone may re-run it, and no judgment enters. A rule may read attributes and raise a question on another dimension.

### Invariants
- Two things that can move independently never share a dimension. The tell for a fused one is a scale whose steps are combinations of two other scales' steps.
- No dimension's answer stands in for another's, and no decided answer stands in for a found-out one. Ruled does not make it true; a clear explanation is not support; a passing step-check does not deliver the statement; agreement is not evidence.
- A dimension has a producer, or the ontology checker refuses it. A question nothing will ever answer is a wish.
- What a piece says may not outrun the record. Its asserted answer may not be one that binding evidence contradicts; anything content says about how well a piece stands ("we prove", "verified", "ratified"), its own or another's, may not exceed that piece's warrant; a decision it reports must be on record with its basis holding. Each failure is owed on the «frontier».
- Evidence adds only along independent lines. Two confirmations that share a source (the same written derivation read, the same seed, a shared inheritance) are one line said twice, and confirmations are assumed to share a source until shown otherwise. Means are a separate matter: a *read as* may ask for several, and many independent lines by one means are still many lines.
- "Never asked" is a warrant, per dimension and per declared means, and a dimension not opened for a kind is a fact; neither is a gap.
- Nothing combines dimensions into one score on a piece.

## Discussion
- **Why found out and decided come first.** They are the two atoms: a truth claim with its evidence, and a «decision» with its backing. They differ in what builds them (accumulated evidence; an accountable party at a branch point), in who may move them (anyone with a sound method; the party), and in what undoes them (a change to what the evidence read; a failure in the basis). A design that gives every question one shape ends by treating a ruling as a rung of certainty, or a count of agreeing minds as a decision.
- **Why the answer is kept apart from its warrant.** "Novelty: high" and "we are fairly sure of that" are two statements, and so are "exact under (S1)–(S5)" and "re-derived twice". A status word that carried both, as "exact" did in the estate's theory files, could not say which had been judged. The body states the answer flatly, as the currently known truth; doubt goes beside it as named open checks; how well it stands is read.
- **Why means are declared, and lines counted.** A claim can be confirmed many times and be false, because every confirmation followed the same written proof: one means, and one line, since each of those checks read the same derivation. A record that says only "checked, passed" cannot show that. With means named by the dimension and recorded by each act, a reader sees "confirmed four times, always by following the proof, never against its neighbours, never instantiated", and a *read as* can require more than one.
- **Why a dimension is a constraint.** The frontier is the list of places where what is written outruns the record, with what passes along references beside it. A steady state is a feasible point; better is a direction within feasibility.
- **Why determinants are a mark and not a term.** A determinant is a dimension the ontology points at and says: right is measured here. It needs no machinery of its own.
- **The two regimes.** A theory claim's determinant dimensions read true or false, and its doubt is a list of named open checks. An empirical fact or a proposed model reads by degree. They join at the hypothesis: a flat if-then resting on a model whose adequacy carries the graded part. Building on a model of middling credence is a decision about the work, not a finding about the model.

## Historical Context
### Etymology
From *dimetiri*, to measure out: one independently measurable question. Joseph's word since August 2026 for the orthogonal questions asked of a piece ("the thing that is orthogonal as it is created"); *aspect*, *axis* and *facet* were candidates that each collapsed the declared question with one thing's answer to it. The geometric overtone is accepted because orthogonality is the invariant.

### Adjudication
- "Truth determinant" was a term of its own in the first core (2026-11-06/07; `msc/notes/17b-2026-11-07-first-core/`), carved as aspect × standard on 11-08 and found on the same day to be non-composable ("authenticity to logic" never arises; "self-consistency" has no standard). Joseph's lean that it is a synonym for or subset of dimensions, and his observation that "aspect" had itself been a candidate name for dimension, led to the role declaration here. The five-slot declaration came from his 11-04 "what builds/founds/sustains this axis" and the census of the estate's verisectoria (`msc/notes/18`).
- Adjudicated against msc/notes 02 A0–A2, 08/09 (RC1's dimension), 09 §2.2, 12, 18 families A–G and findings 1–8, 19 §3, and Joseph's 11-08 turns on aspect × standard, on dimensions as constraints, and on determinants as a subset.

## Working Notes
- From the second real root: a theory segment reports its own numerics ("numerical integration agrees to three decimals") as a matter of course, and the model offers three homes with three consequences: content speaking of standing (then overclaimed if no run is on record), an observation piece, or a claim on a reproduces dimension held against a script. Lean: the third. And a claim with three derivations under three premise sets is three claims, each with its own conditions; a document's one status word cannot be aliased to "the" claim until one is chosen.
- Open. Findings have no size: two small precisions block a rule for canon exactly as a narrowed derivation does. Lean: the dimension a finding is on, and what that dimension's rule asks, is where size belongs, not a severity field.
- Completeness, from the first core. Its AAT determinants included "completeness, against the questions the theory undertakes to settle", with the remark that "on validity alone, a store softened to tautologies would be perfect." Here that job is done by a clause in the sense of better ("or the content answers to a determinant more strongly in a sense the ontology declares"). If completeness were an ordinary found-out dimension, asked of a projection and held against a declared list of questions, a softened claim would lower its warrant and better could be read over determinant warrants alone. Lean: try it on the next real root; if it holds, the "more strongly" clause goes.
- A piece answers to a determinant when it is "in the service of it: it asserts something the determinant settles, or it asks, doubts, explains or records in aid of settling it" (Joseph's reading, 2026-11-08). Nothing here says how a question or an explanation stands to a determinant it is not itself judged on.
- Things held against something outside the verisectorium, and time. Joseph, 2026-11-09, as the one part of influx he sees as needing real decisions ("the literal influx parameters for external referents"): influx is like a turbulent mixing layer between two flows. The verisectorium has its velocity and each outside anchor has its own, "ideally something slower than the v's, or all you can hope for is a snapshot". So most veritoids will need "some dimension of expected obsolescence curve / half-life / shelf-life / temporal-triggers/anchors or something that gives epistemic effective-time for any fact that is temporally anchored", which is also "a claim on how often we hope to entrain the other source (that is, check it again for updates)", and, separately and where possible, a prediction of how often the source changes. He keeps the two apart: "how long can we afford having something stale if it is stale" against "what is the likelihood of this being stale by this time"; he calls these critical temporal truth determinants. Read through the two atoms, they are one of each: how likely the source is to have moved is found out, about the source, shared by everything that relies on it; how long staleness can be afforded is decided, per reliance, at an importance. A candidate that adds nothing: the outside thing is pinned by a veritoid whose content is "this source at this version, last looked at then"; looking again is an act; moving the pin is a write that lapses what read it; the affordable staleness is a decision on each reference to the pin, and a rule raises the question when it runs out. A source that moves faster than the verisectorium is scoped in time ("as of"), which changes what is claimed and not how well it stands. For a fact anchored in time, being current is part of being right, so for those kinds freshness is a ‹determinant›. Untested.
- Open. Whether *against* can carry every second argument, or whether some (importance for a target; comprehension by a reader) belong to the act. Lean: the dimension names how a piece fills it; the act reads what fills it.
- Open. What shows two lines of evidence independent when both actors are instances of one model family. The dimension can require different means and different actors; it cannot yet require a different failure mode. A gap row carries it.
- Open. Whether an orientation dimension (of an actor) is one dimension with a varying *against* or three. Lean: one. See «actor».
- Open. Whether at least one determinant must be held *against* something no write to the verisectorium can change (logic, the world, a source outside it). Every example so far has one; a verisectorium whose determinants are all against its own canon could improve by its own lights without limit.
- Open. Whether prerequisite, precedence and veto among dimensions are core machinery or only something an ontology may state. Lean: stated only, until a verisectorium needs the engine to enforce one.
- Untested on a declared ontology: the declaration as a whole. The next real root should be run with validity declared in full, means included.
