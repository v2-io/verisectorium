## Intro

Content comes in two regimes, by which «truth⸱determinants» it answers to and how it settles. A «THEORY⸱CLAIM» aims to be true or false: it is stated flatly, settled by derivation and broken by counterexample, and any doubt about it is a named thing to check, placed beside it and never folded into it. An «EMPIRICAL⸱FACT» or a «PROPOSED⸱MODEL» is not expected to become simply true: it is adequate by degree, can be falsified, and is built on by decision. The two regimes meet at the hypothesis. A theory claim that rests on a proposed model is still a flat if-then; the model's adequacy carries the graded part, and nothing graded leaks into the claim.

## Definition

A piece of content about the world, supported by runs or measurements, adequate by degree and never simply true.
- It carries what was run or measured, under what, and whether it reproduces.
- It can be falsified by a run; it cannot be derived.

## Discussion

- Why the regimes are core and not a store's choice. Which regime a store centres on is the store's (a theory store centres on claims; a spec store on decided rules). That two regimes exist, and that they meet at the hypothesis, is about what content can answer to at all.
- Why doubt lives beside a theory claim and not in it. A flatly stated claim is falsifiable; a hedged one is not. Keeping the doubt as named work makes it closable one item at a time, and keeps the claim honest in the only way a claim can be, by being refutable.
- The earlier designs put most of their machinery, graded weight of evidence above all, into the second regime and then tried to make theory claims fit it. For a theory store that is the edge case, not the centre.

## Examples

- A theorem of AAT, and the list of things not yet checked about it, kept beside it.
- A citation manager's key-to-PDF association: an empirical fact with graded belief from many small evidences, where a numeric credence is apt.
- A proposed mismatch-dynamics model in AAT, built on by several results that remain flat if-thens conditional on it.
