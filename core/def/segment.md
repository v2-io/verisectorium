# «segment»
A piece of the corpus: a «veritoid», which has content and nothing inside it, or an assembly of slots, each filled by a segment it holds or by a reference to one written elsewhere.

## Intro
Content divides. A result has a claim, a derivation, an explanation; the derivation has steps; a chapter gathers results; a theory gathers chapters. One thing does all of that: a segment is either a leaf with content, the «veritoid», or an **assembly**, whose «kind» declares slots and which fills each slot with a segment. So referring, composing, asking questions and accepting work the same way at every depth, from a single step to a volume. Most verisectoria stop at a few scales in practice: veritoids, the document-like assemblies they are written in, and the outlines that gather those.

## Definition
An assembly has **slots**. Its kind declares them: what kind of segment fills each, whether one, at most one or any number, in what order, and which are required. In a given assembly each slot is in one of these written states:

- **suggested**: empty, with a note of what might go there. Advice, often from outside the work; it commits nobody.
- **promised**: empty, with the kind and name of what will fill it. Written when someone with standing in the assembly decides, from inside the work, that it is needed. The promise is a «decision» and outlives the filling: it is kept when what fills the slot is accepted.
- **filled**: by a segment the assembly **holds**, or by a «reference» to a segment that lives elsewhere, freestanding or held by another assembly (**pointed at**).
- a **gap**: a slot, or a run of slots, whose kind is not yet known. It says something is missing and not what.

A held segment is written with its holder and is reached through it. A segment held by no other is **freestanding**: it has a name, and it, together with everything it holds to any depth, is what one ‹segment·changeset› writes. A pointed-at segment may fill slots in any number of assemblies.

Whether a segment is wanted in a place, and whether the segment is accepted, are separate. The first is the slot's state, written in the assembly that has the slot and changed by writing that assembly. The second is a decided «dimension» of the segment itself: a segment is **accepted**, in canon, once a decision by those with standing admits it. While that decision's basis holds the segment is settled; when the basis fails the decision is reopened and the segment is **unsettled**: still in canon, and owed, until it is accepted again or the acceptance is withdrawn. A segment never admitted, or withdrawn, is **provisional**: to canon it is only the claim that it has been suggested. Acceptance is per segment at every depth. Accepting an assembly is a decision about the assembly: that these are its parts, in this order, and that it holds together. Its basis is a read of the whole, which reads of each part what the kind says such a read relies on (for an outline, a part's headline and what it asserts; for a short document, everything).

Holding and pointing are not resting. One segment **rests on** another when it has a «reference» to it of a kind some «dimension» passes along (predicated-on, above all), or when a decision about it has the other in its basis. An assembly does not rest on its parts by having them: a whole that names its parts and says only what is true of the whole can be accepted before them and stays settled while one of them is unsettled, provisional or absent. It shows the state of each part, and it is reopened only when something its accepting read relied on changes.

A ‹projection› is an assembly whose slots mostly point: a chapter, a volume, the canonical outline, a reading path, a report. A ‹source› is an assembly holding chunks cut from material brought in. A segment's **inbox** is the chunks bearing on it that no decision has yet routed for good, with any note a mind has left there (a note is a source of one chunk, minted in the act of leaving it); it is computed, and it is not part of the segment.

### Invariants
- It has an id minted at creation and never reused. A freestanding segment has one current name, and old names keep resolving.
- It is of exactly one declared kind, and it is a veritoid or an assembly, never both.
- A freestanding segment changes only as a whole, in one ‹segment·changeset›.
- An assembly is not one fate. Each segment in it has its own warrants; the assembly has dimensions of its own, chiefly coherence (does it hold together read as one thing), answered by acts that concern the assembly.
- An assembly's version covers its slots, their states, and which segment fills each; it does not cover the versions of what fills them. So an edit deep inside does not change every assembly above it. What depends on an assembly's content (a review of it, its acceptance, a reference to it) reads what the kind or the reference kind says it reads of what the assembly holds, and lapses, reopens or goes stale on that.
- A promise is owed until what fills its slot is accepted, or the promise is withdrawn.
- Nothing inside a provisional segment is owed. An accepted segment that rests on a provisional or unsettled one is unsettled, unless all it relies on is that the suggestion exists. A piece once accepted stays in canon, settled or not, until its acceptance is withdrawn; so of two pieces in the same recorded condition, one that was once accepted owes and one that never was does not.
- A segment reached by no canonical projection and by no predicated-on reference in or out is an orphan; orphans are allowed and counted.
- A ‹source› is cut completely into chunks. Its text is never rewritten; its cut, and what each chunk bears on, may be refined as routing shows the grain. It is retired, computed, while every chunk has a routing decision that stands.
- Adding to or draining a segment's inbox never changes the segment.

## Discussion
- Why a segment is not one kind and not one fate. ASF's frontmatter made a whole segment look like one fate and one kind; its `status:` word was five questions' worth of assertions on heterogeneous veritoids, typed once. Many ASF segments are also coarser than they should be, holding independent claims that should each have their own name; promotion is the recut for that.
- Why Working Notes were three things. Inbox chunks (material bearing on the segment and not yet routed); standing content mis-shelved (regression guards, open questions with closers, dead ends with reasons, each a veritoid of its own kind in a slot of its own); and in-place fate markers written as prose, which are routing decisions that never became acts. The first is the inbox; the second is content; the third is the acta.
- What provisional claims, and what it owes. A provisional piece, written or not, claims one thing to canon: that the idea is suggested, sometimes with more about the idea. That is what gives a verisectorium a low-stakes way to hold low-certainty ideas; without one a mind must choose between cutting off creative and nonlinear thought and letting it clutter everything. Provisional pieces and suggested slots are mostly early attempts at an outline: to get the shape and scope of some run of segments, as a scaffold that almost always changes once a few segments near acceptance; or segments from elsewhere that might do as they are and have not been adjudicated; or guesses by someone new to the subject; or a place to sweep orphans so they can be drained. A provisional piece is not canon. So what it says inside itself is not owed: its assertions are part of the suggestion. And an accepted piece that rests on a provisional one is unsettled, which is to say on the frontier, unless all it relies on is that the suggestion exists (as an outline does of a row it has not yet filled). Suggestions are stock, and stock may sit in a steady state. Whether they must be drained is each verisectorium's to say, through a fitness dimension of the projection that balances exploration against noise (coherence, comprehension, conciseness); some stock, like gaps, is a needed truth about incompleteness. Accepting one piece is a large perturbation: it pulls in everything it rests on, which must be adjudicated before the next steady state. So it pays to bring a set of provisional, mutually dependent pieces to a self-consistent whole before any of them is put up for acceptance.
- Promised is not provisional. A suggested slot is advice, often from outside the work. A promised slot is a commitment made from inside it: someone qualified, in the middle of a changeset, knows this segment is needed and says so before it is written. The promise is canon; its segment is not there yet. That is what lets a mind arriving later tell what is tentative from what really must be written. A promised segment that is missing is owed, so it is normally worked off before the next steady state. A verisectorium may let one stand at a steady state as part of its current honest truth, by a waiver with a reason like any other owed item, and then whatever rests on it cannot be warranted above what an unwritten thing warrants: it could not, for example, be exact.
- One line of commitment. A note in a segment's inbox (no place, no shape, no standing), a suggested slot (a place and a rough kind), a promised slot, a filled slot, an accepted segment: each step commits more, and each is cheap to take. The inbox and the slots are the two places a segment says what it wants; draining a note is what moves an intention along the line or drops it. Anyone may add a note. Where the honest in-between has nowhere to go, a mind chooses between silence and overclaiming.
- Why wanted and accepted are kept apart. "Provisional" used to do both jobs. Kept apart they combine freely: suggested and empty (an idea in an early outline); filled and unaccepted (an offered draft); promised and empty (owed); promised, filled and unaccepted (work in progress); filled and accepted (canon). A mind arriving cold can tell the adviser's idea from the thing that must be written.
- Why small segments cost a reader nothing. A file is a rendering and not a segment, so segments can be as small as the truth requires while what a mind is shown is as wide as the work requires.
- Why holding and pointing are the only two ways to fill a slot. A held segment has one home and is written with it; any segment, held or freestanding, may also be pointed at from elsewhere by its id. Nothing else distinguishes a section of a document from a chapter of a volume.

## Examples
- A scope segment of a theory: a freestanding assembly whose slots hold an intro (explanations), a definition, a predicate, any number of consequence claims, non-claims, discussion paragraphs, questions and guards; each of those a veritoid.
- A derived result whose derivation slot holds an assembly of steps: three deep by holding, written as one.
- A volume's outline with nine gaps and six promised slots: a canonical projection, no less true for them.
- This verisectorium's own outline: a projection whose slots are filled either by pointing at a def or a prose segment, or by holding a one-line definition directly, which is then just a veritoid.
- An audit report cut into chunks, most bearing on one segment each: a source.

## Historical Context
### Etymology
English, from *segmentum*, a piece cut off (*secare* again, the same root as the *-sect-* in verisectorium): what the cutting produces. It has been the estate's everyday word for the unit a mind writes and reviews since ASF's first files, and the model keeps it with that sense sharpened: the unit of writing and of naming, not of fate.

### Provisional, from experience
Joseph's account of what provisional has been for across the estate's verisectoria, in his words (2026-11-09): "an early underlying driver is that there needs to be a low-stakes way to add low-certainty ideas or else you have to decide between cutting off all creativity and important nonlinear thought or have it clutter everything up where it shouldn't. Provisional rows are almost always early attempts at an *outline* -- to get the shape of some continuum of segments -- to get the scope-- and to be an initial scaffold that almost always changes as soon as a few segments are about ready to be accepted. It's also used for segments from another repo/project that *might* be acceptable as is but need to be adjudicated first. Finally, most provisional rows are ideated by someone from an unrelated project etc.-- someone coming at it from verisectorium, for example, asked to help set up a new one for a new instance, and guessing on an outline based on a set of questions to them. Sometimes it's just to sweep up orphans into a part of an outline so that they can be drained more effectively."

On whether a suggestion is also a promise: "Only if that verisectorium has some kind of workflow/drain that connect's to the outline (segment)'s determinants that are responsible for balancing exploration & explication vs noise. Or in other words-- from a system dynamics perspective, is there a need to drain claims of ideas? Depends on the verisectorium. Typically, my understanding is that 'stock' can be part of steady-state but may affect other fitness dimensions (coherence, comprehension, conciseness, ...) but they might also, like with gaps, be critical truths about the *incompleteness* of something."

On acceptance: "it behooves the agent to get a set of provisional mutually interdependent segments/veritoids into an independently self-consistent graph before things start to try to adjudicate acceptance-- at which point you're probably going to need to get them all fully adjudicated before you hit a steady-state again-- it's basically a big perturbation once a single one gets accepted". And, the same day: "I thought I'd been clear from the beginning that provisional == not-in-canon, over and over and over again."

On promised against provisional (2026-11-09): "there is a sharp difference between a provisional … segment and a promised segment that is missing. In practice the big difference is that it is usually someone well-qualified who turns a provisional into an accepted segment that is missing at the moment, or adding one as a perturbation (versus the provisional which is more of 'advice from a third party')-- this is someone in the middle of a changeset who knows they need this segment and happened to promise it before getting it written (so, for example, a subagent would know which ones are tentative vs which ones really do need to be written)-- and for *some* verisectorium (a minority probably)-- it would be acceptable in the steady-state to have a promised unwritten segment as part of your 'current honest truth' -- although that *is* the case where you couldn't have it 'exact'".

Three days earlier (2026-11-06) he had given the failure this prevents: without a provisional mark on the rows of a first outline, the agents who first work on a project "immediately and implicitly mistrust the outline and canon and verisectorium, and either adhere too strongly to that draft outline, or pick and choose and stop distinguishing between what is grounded and what is not".

### Slots, holding and the leaf
Joseph, 2026-11-09, wondering what "provisional" was doing: "I wonder if it's a 'slot' within an instance that gets these more flow-and-initial-standing related attributes/dimensions distinct from the acceptance of a newly minted segment itself". Then: "slot holds a segment, and segment = veritoid or assembly of slots. So an 'outline' is just a kind of segment that usually has an ordered listing of slots-- each of which can be filled with, in many cases, a non-veritoid segment that holds the veritoids, but in our case here locally where we allow definitions inline, for example, the slot you called a 'row' is just the veritoid-- its content + at least one dimension (accepted) and some inbound dependencies are all it is. So, there could just as easily be a promised 'Formal Claim' section (slot with a promise of a specific veritoid) within a segment, or a higher-level 'outline' segment promising or provisionally having an intro paragraph veritoid along with a promised chapter outline (segment)". And that suggested and promised are "attributes on slots, not dimensions on segments (including veritoids)". The same day, on the leaf: "it might make sense model/mathematically to define … veritoid as a segment as well-- just a segment that is a leaf and therefore doesn't reduce any further (other than splitting). That way whatever we use as referencing and composing is consistent from veritoid -> 'segment'(s) -> topics -> sections -> chapters -> parts -> volumes". An invented outline was run through this reading before it was written here, and it held.

On the inbox: "There should always be a way for agents (including humans) to get their intuitions and tentative results known so they can be adjudicated." And: "the inbox -- either as an undisected collection or via working-notes -- is the other main place for a segment to declare what kinds of things it wants and intends". On grain and what a mind is shown, 2026-11-05: the tension is between "what to get in front of the agent that is the holistic-enough picture" and the independence of the parts.

## Working Notes
- That a whole does not rest on its parts was a lean in «reference»'s notes, from Joseph's 2026-11-09 remark that definitions run bottom-up while comprehension and adjudication run top-down. It moved into the body when a trial instance, built from these defs with the opposite sentence in them, found that every disturbance then unsettled its document, then the outline, so that any two changesets met at the outline and scoped steadiness collapsed to the whole.
- Open. For a required slot that takes many fillers, whether the kind may ask that at least one, or all, be accepted before the assembly can be. Lean: the kind's to declare; core says only that holding is not resting.
- Open. What an acceptance reads. As written, the accepting decision's basis is the segment at its version and what it rests on being accepted, so a rewording reopens it and re-accepting a small change is a small decision. The risk is that small fixes go unmade or re-acceptance becomes a stamp. A carrying check by a mind other than the editor ("this edit left unchanged what the acceptance relied on") lets the acts under an acceptance bind at the new version, which is how the estate's one recorded sprint handled it; whether the acceptance itself is then sustained by rule or put up again is each verisectorium's.
- Open. Who has standing to promise a slot. "Someone with standing in the assembly" is all this says; a kind would declare it, as a decided dimension of the assembly.
- Open. Whether a gap must sometimes be defined well enough for something downstream to rest on it knowingly, and what such a reference is warranted at. Lean: it holds and warrants the lowest, so what rests on it shows as capped.
- Open. Nothing says a projection's order is held to anything. A canonical outline claims dependence order; where it deliberately disagrees, that is a decision with a reason. Lean: an ordering dimension a projection kind may open, against predicated-on order.
- Open. Whether "source" and "projection" are subtypes or kinds each ontology declares. Lean: subtypes, because every verisectorium has both and the engine treats them specially.
- Churn is a second grain detector beside adjudication: a segment whose history is long and whose changes are about different things is carrying two claims. Cheap, and computed.
- A restates reference means agreement with the piece that owns the claim; two pieces restating a third that disagree are repaired by ceasing to restate, never by reconciling the copies.
- Untested: a slot whose kind fixes it (a result's required claim) being promised; a gap resolved into slots; the same segment accepted in one verisectorium and pointed at from another.
