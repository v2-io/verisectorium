# «segment»
Veritoids written and judged together under a name: the unit of writing, of naming, and of whole review.

## Intro
A «veritoid» almost never makes sense alone. Its fate is intertwined with its neighbours', usually so that if a core one fails the others go down with it, and adjudicating a change to any one of them means looking at the rest. A segment is the set of veritoids kept together for that reason. Its ‹segment·kind› declares its slots, the inner dependencies between them, and how the segment's own «dimensions|def/dimension» are read from its veritoids. Two subtypes are needed from the start: a ‹projection› is a segment whose slots hold «references|def/reference» to other segments in a chosen order, for a purpose (a chapter, a part, a volume, the canonical outline, a reading path, a report); a ‹source› is a segment whose veritoids are chunks cut from material brought in.

## Definition
### Invariants
- It has a minted id and one current name; old names keep resolving.
- It is the unit of writing: its veritoids change only together, in one ‹segment·changeset›.
- It is not one fate. Each veritoid has its own warrants; the segment has its own dimensions, chiefly coherence (does it hold together read as one thing), read from a review of the whole.
- A ‹projection› may declare, over segment kind × determinant, the weight each pair carries for its purpose, with reach within it as a multiplier; that declaration is what makes the projection's sense of better, and the canonical projection's is the verisectorium's default.
- A ‹projection›'s rows carry two states kept apart, commitment (provisional, or promised by a decision) and existence (absent, or present), and a projection is true as stated at every «steady·state» because a provisional row claims only that the suggestion exists; its coherence lapses only on what its kind says a review of it reads of each member (headline and assertion, not every proof step), so one edit does not lapse the whole theory.
- It is of exactly one declared ‹segment·kind›.
- A segment reached by no canonical projection and by no predicated-on reference in or out is an orphan; orphans are allowed and counted.
- A ‹source› is cut completely into chunks; it is retired, computed, when every chunk has a final crossing.
- Its inbox (the chunks bearing on it with no final crossing) is computed and is not part of its written state. Adding to or draining it never changes the segment.

## Discussion
- Why a segment is not one kind and not one fate. ASF's frontmatter made a whole segment look like one fate and one kind; its `status:` word was five questions' worth of assertions on heterogeneous veritoids, typed once. Many ASF segments are also coarser than they should be, holding independent claims that should each have their own name; promotion is the recut for that.
- Why Working Notes were three things. Inbox chunks (uncrossed material bearing on the segment); standing content mis-shelved (regression guards, open questions with closers, dead ends with reasons, each a veritoid of its own kind in a slot of its own); and in-place fate markers written as prose, which are crossings that never became acts. The first is the inbox; the second is content; the third is the acta.
- Four places a record can go, and which the segment owns. The acta (what was done; the verisectorium's, not the segment's), the history of renderings (outside the verisectorium), the inbox (influx bearing on the segment, outside its written state), and history (written prose kept with the segment about how it came to be as it is: a slot of account veritoids, revisable, faithful to the acta, and the one place a segment may point outward as breadcrumbs). A body states present truth and stands alone; history says how it got there; the inbox holds what is not yet adjudicated; the acta hold what was done. A sentence of history inside a claim is a claim that has stopped standing on its own.
- What provisional claims, and what it owes. A provisional piece, written or not, claims one thing to canon: that the idea is suggested, sometimes with more about the idea. That is what gives a verisectorium a low-stakes way to hold low-certainty ideas; without one a mind must choose between cutting off creative and nonlinear thought and letting it clutter everything. Provisional rows are mostly early attempts at an outline: to get the shape and scope of some run of segments, as a scaffold that almost always changes once a few segments near acceptance; or segments from elsewhere that might do as they are and have not been adjudicated; or guesses by someone new to the subject; or a place to sweep orphans so they can be drained. A provisional piece is not canon. So what it says inside itself is not owed: its assertions are part of the suggestion. And an accepted piece that rests on a provisional one is unsettled, which is to say on the frontier, unless all it relies on is that the suggestion exists (as an outline does of a row it has not yet filled). Suggestions are stock, and stock may sit in a steady state. Whether they must be drained is each verisectorium's to say, through a fitness dimension of the projection that balances exploration against noise (coherence, comprehension, conciseness); some stock, like gaps, is a needed truth about incompleteness. Accepting one piece is a large perturbation: it pulls in everything it rests on, which must be adjudicated before the next steady state. So it pays to bring a set of provisional, mutually dependent pieces to a self-consistent whole before any of them is put up for acceptance.
- Promised is not provisional. A provisional row is advice, often from outside the work. A promised row is a commitment made from inside it: someone qualified, in the middle of a changeset, knows this segment is needed and says so before it is written. The row is canon; its segment is not there yet. That is what lets a mind arriving later tell what is tentative from what really must be written. A promised segment that is missing is owed, so it is normally worked off before the next steady state. A verisectorium may let one stand at a steady state as part of its current honest truth, by acceptance with a reason like any other owed item, and then whatever rests on it cannot be warranted above what an unwritten thing warrants: it could not, for example, be exact.
- Why projections nest without a third level. A chapter is a segment whose slots hold references to segments; a part holds chapters; the theory holds parts. That is gathering, not content inside content, so the two-level rule stands.

## Historical Context
### Etymology
English, from *segmentum*, a piece cut off (*secare* again, the same root as the *-sect-* in verisectorium): what the cutting produces. It has been the estate's everyday word for the unit a mind writes and reviews since ASF's first files, and the model keeps it with that sense sharpened: the unit of writing and of naming, not of fate.

### Provisional, from experience
Joseph's account of what provisional has been for across the estate's verisectoria, in his words (2026-11-09): "an early underlying driver is that there needs to be a low-stakes way to add low-certainty ideas or else you have to decide between cutting off all creativity and important nonlinear thought or have it clutter everything up where it shouldn't. Provisional rows are almost always early attempts at an *outline* -- to get the shape of some continuum of segments -- to get the scope-- and to be an initial scaffold that almost always changes as soon as a few segments are about ready to be accepted. It's also used for segments from another repo/project that *might* be acceptable as is but need to be adjudicated first. Finally, most provisional rows are ideated by someone from an unrelated project etc.-- someone coming at it from verisectorium, for example, asked to help set up a new one for a new instance, and guessing on an outline based on a set of questions to them. Sometimes it's just to sweep up orphans into a part of an outline so that they can be drained more effectively."

On whether a suggestion is also a promise: "Only if that verisectorium has some kind of workflow/drain that connect's to the outline (segment)'s determinants that are responsible for balancing exploration & explication vs noise. Or in other words-- from a system dynamics perspective, is there a need to drain claims of ideas? Depends on the verisectorium. Typically, my understanding is that 'stock' can be part of steady-state but may affect other fitness dimensions (coherence, comprehension, conciseness, ...) but they might also, like with gaps, be critical truths about the *incompleteness* of something."

On acceptance: "it behooves the agent to get a set of provisional mutually interdependent segments/veritoids into an independently self-consistent graph before things start to try to adjudicate acceptance-- at which point you're probably going to need to get them all fully adjudicated before you hit a steady-state again-- it's basically a big perturbation once a single one gets accepted". And, the same day: "I thought I'd been clear from the beginning that provisional == not-in-canon, over and over and over again."

On promised against provisional (2026-11-09): "there is a sharp difference between a provisional … segment and a promised segment that is missing. In practice the big difference is that it is usually someone well-qualified who turns a provisional into an accepted segment that is missing at the moment, or adding one as a perturbation (versus the provisional which is more of 'advice from a third party')-- this is someone in the middle of a changeset who knows they need this segment and happened to promise it before getting it written (so, for example, a subagent would know which ones are tentative vs which ones really do need to be written)-- and for *some* verisectorium (a minority probably)-- it would be acceptable in the steady-state to have a promised unwritten segment as part of your 'current honest truth' -- although that *is* the case where you couldn't have it 'exact'".

Three days earlier (2026-11-06) he had given the failure this prevents: without a provisional mark on the rows of a first outline, the agents who first work on a project "immediately and implicitly mistrust the outline and canon and verisectorium, and either adhere too strongly to that draft outline, or pick and choose and stop distinguishing between what is grounded and what is not".

## Working Notes
- Open. Whether a projection's members may also be veritoids (a chapter preface written inline) or only references to segments. Lean: a projection kind may declare content slots of its own beside its member slot; the preface is then its veritoid.
- A ‹source› and its chunks are written once and never changed; a ‹crossing› concerns a chunk and writes nothing in it; so the changeset that lands a source has no inner dependents to affirm, and a changeset touching a source's chunks is refused. Stated here so the first real intake does not meet it as a surprise.
- Churn is a second grain detector beside adjudication: a segment whose history is long and whose change notes are about different things is carrying two claims. Cheap, and computed.
- A restates reference means agreement with the piece that owns the claim; two pieces restating a third that disagree with each other are repaired by ceasing to restate (a reference to the owner), never by reconciling the two copies.
- On "What provisional claims, and what it owes" in the Discussion. Two things follow that are not yet written into the defs they belong to. (1) «reference» and «frontier» do not yet say that a reference from an accepted piece to a provisional one is owed unless it needs only that the suggestion exists; I had first written that such a reference "holds and warrants the lowest", which Joseph corrected: it "is the very definition of unsettled state or frontier, unless the only thing the suggested one is claiming is that there is an idea (provisional) that exists". (2) Provisional is a state of any piece, not only of a projection's row, and the `row` inline word's commitment states (provisional, promised) want restating from it. Promised is now said in the Discussion; what is still open there is who has standing to promise (the Discussion says "someone qualified"; a dimension's *produced by* would have to say it), and how a *gap* differs from a promised row in the model (a gap does not know what will replace it).
- **Whether a veritoid is a leaf segment** (Joseph, 2026-11-09: "it might make sense model/mathematically to define … veritoid as a segment as well-- just a segment that is a leaf and therefore doesn't reduce any further (other than splitting). That way whatever we use as referencing and composing is consistent from veritoid -> 'segment'(s) -> topics -> sections -> chapters -> parts -> volumes"). Undecided. The case for it: «verisectorium» says content divides without end in principle and stops in practice at a few scales, while this def and «veritoid» fix two levels ("it contains no veritoid"), and both cannot be the machinery. What differs between the two today is not leaf versus not, but three things that would become declarations of a kind: whether a slot *owns* its pieces or *points at* them (a segment's veritoids against a projection's members); which piece is *written whole*, changed-or-affirmed at one instant; and whether a piece has a name. One kind of piece, whose kind's slots hold owned pieces or references and whose leaf has content and no slots, would let an act concern a segment (today it cannot, so nothing answers a segment's own dimensions), would dissolve the question below of whether a projection's members may be parts of segments, and would allow owned pieces under one name (a canon part, an inbox, a history, viewable together by that name), which is how this repository's own files already behave. "Veritoid" would remain the word for a leaf, "segment" for a named piece written whole, and two levels a fact about most ontologies. The cost: the changeset rule has to be restated for "the piece written whole" at whatever depth, and an earlier attempt at one recursive type (2026-11-05) was withdrawn because every rule then in hand was stated at exactly two levels and no example needed a third. Lean: yes as the machinery, after one worked move through a three-deep case.
- The inbox as the cheap place for the tentative, which the defs do not yet say. Joseph: "There should always be a way for agents (including humans) to get their intuitions and tentative results known so they can be adjudicated." Where the honest in-between has no place to go, a mind chooses between silence and overclaiming. So anyone may add to a segment's inbox, and a note written straight into it is its own small source with one chunk; it costs one sentence and owes nothing. An open item only does its job if it sits where the mind that should act on it will pass, which is why the inbox is per segment. Lean: say this in the Definition; it is what makes "recording too little is as dishonest as claiming too much" bearable.
- Why small segments cost a reader nothing, also unsaid: the tension is between "what to get in front of the agent that is the holistic-enough picture" and the independence of the parts (Joseph, 2026-11-05). Because a file is a rendering and not the segment, segments can be as small as the truth requires and what a mind is shown can be as wide as the work requires; the two no longer constrain each other.
- Open, and the outline itself depends on it: "gap" has no meaning in the model. This verisectorium's outline marks rows *gap*, but a row here has only commitment (provisional, promised) and existence (absent, present). A gap is neither a promised row nor a planned segment: it does not know how many segments or what kinds will replace it, it must sometimes be defined well enough for something downstream to rest on, knowingly, and it ends by vanishing or by being replaced (Joseph, 2026-11-06). Lean: a gap is a segment of a gap kind whose one veritoid says what is missing; a reference to it holds and warrants the lowest, so what rests on it shows as capped and not as broken.
- Open. Nothing here says a projection's order is held to anything. A canonical outline claims dependence order; where it deliberately disagrees (a term introduced before what it rests on, for a reader's sake) that is a decision with a reason. Lean: an ordering dimension a projection kind may open, against predicated-on order, with exceptions as acceptances.
- Open. Whether "source" and "projection" are subtypes of segment or kinds declared by every ontology. Lean: subtypes, because every verisectorium has both and the engine treats them specially (the inbox; the canonical projection).
