# Two moves at once

*An invented example. It illustrates the terms; it is evidence for nothing.*

**What this shows.** With several minds at work something is nearly always open somewhere, and if the whole verisectorium had to be steady at once, every move would close by waiving whatever was unrelated to it. Here two findings land in unrelated places. Each opens its own ‹atomic·changeset› with its own scope, one closes while the other is still open, and the verisectorium is steady everywhere outside the open scope without a single waiver. Then the two are made to meet, and they become one.

## The material

Steady state S0. T holds a claim c; U holds u, predicated on c. V holds a claim x; W holds w, predicated on x. No reference runs between T or U and V or W. All accepted.

## The run

| when | what happens | open changesets, and what is owed |
|---|---|---|
| Mon 09:00 | A finding lands on c. | **M1** opens. Its items: found (c); reopened (c, T, u, U). Its scope is every segment it has written or that has an item it caused: T, U. |
| Mon 09:10 | A finding lands on x. | **M2** opens. Scope: V, W. The two scopes share nothing. |
| Mon 09:30–14:00 | M1 is worked: c gains a hypothesis H, the finding is answered, c is re-derived, u is narrowed and re-derived, T and U are read whole. | M1's items are all cleared. **M1 closes.** The verisectorium is steady outside M2's scope: T and U are settled canon at their new versions. V and W are unsettled, and shown so. |
| Tue | The mind repairing x sees that x also needs H, and writes a predicated-on reference from x to c. | c is settled, so the reference holds. M2's scope is still V, W. M2 came after M1: it read what M1 wrote. |
| Wed | M2's items are cleared. | **M2 closes.** Nothing is open: a steady state of the whole. |

Run again with one change. On Monday at 11:00, before M1 has closed, the mind repairing x writes the reference from x to c.

| when | what happens | open changesets, and what is owed |
|---|---|---|
| Mon 11:00 | x now rests on c, and c is unsettled. | x has an item whose cause is in M1. The scopes have met: **M1 and M2 are one changeset** from here, with scope T, U, V, W. |
| Mon 14:00 | Everything that was M1's is cleared. | Nothing closes. x's repair is not finished, and c's new form is part of what x is being repaired against. |
| Wed | The rest is cleared. | The one changeset closes. On the steady record c gained H and x came to rest on it at the same instant. |

## What this says about the model

- **A scope is computed, by cause.** Every frontier item names the write or act that made it owed, and reopening spreads along what rests on what, so the segments a disturbance reaches are found by following causes. Nobody declares a scope.
- **Steady is said of a place.** "Steady outside M2" is a complete and honest statement on Monday afternoon. A reader of T builds on settled canon; a reader of W is told it is unsettled and why.
- **Closed changesets are ordered only where one read the other.** M2 came after M1 in the first run because x came to rest on c. Had it not, the two would have had no order, and none would have been needed.
- **Meeting is merging.** In the second run neither could close alone, which is right: there was never a verisectorium in which c had H and x both did and did not rest on it.
- **Waivers went back to being rare.** Nothing was waived in either run. A waiver is for a known defect knowingly left, not for work that is merely elsewhere.

**What it did not exercise, and what it cannot see.** A change that touches everything (a notation used throughout) meets every open scope and is one changeset with all of them; that is the case where work stops and the whole is looked at. And two open changesets can contradict each other in substance while no reference connects them. Their scopes never meet, both close, and the verisectorium is steady and inconsistent until someone notices. That is the unwritten-reference problem, and scopes do not solve it.
