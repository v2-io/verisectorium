# An outline fills in

*An invented example. It illustrates the terms; it is evidence for nothing.*

**What this shows.** Whether a thing is wanted in a place, and whether the thing is accepted, are kept apart at every step without strain: the first moves by writes to the assembly that has the slot, the second by «decisions|def/decision» about whatever fills it. And a decision is reopened by a failure in its basis, here twice: once because what it read was reworded, once because an assumption it named turned out false.

## The material

In this verisectorium a segment enters canon by a decision of the steward and the lead together. Its basis is the segment at its version, the checks they asked for, and what it rests on being accepted.

An outline O, which is canonical. It is an assembly whose slots are an ordered list:

1. filled, **holding** a veritoid D: a one-line definition. D is accepted.
2. filled, **pointing at** a segment R1: a result, with a claim c1 that is predicated on D, and an explanation. R1 is accepted.
3. **suggested**, empty: "a result about X is probably wanted here", left by an adviser who is not working on this.
4. a **gap**: "something is missing between this and whatever follows; not known what".

A reading path P, not canonical, with two slots: one pointing at R1, one pointing at D.

D is held by O and changes only when O is written. R1 is written on its own. At S0 nothing is owed. The suggested slot and the gap are there, and S0 is still a steady state.

## The run

| step | what happens | what kind of thing it is | the frontier afterwards |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agent A, at work on what follows R1, sees that a result R2 is needed and says so: slot 3 goes from suggested to **promised**, with the kind and name it will have. | a decision by A, who has standing in O; a write to O | **promised**: slot 3 has nothing accepted in it. The last whole read of O read its slots, which changed: lapsed, and O's acceptance is **reopened**. |
| 2 | A writes R2 (a claim c2, predicated on c1 and on D; an explanation) and fills slot 3 by pointing at it. | a new segment; a write to O | The slot is filled. R2 is provisional, so nothing it says inside itself is owed. The promise is still owed. |
| 3 | B, who did not write it, re-derives c2. | a check concerning c2 | c2 has a warrant. It is still provisional: a piece is checked before it is accepted, not after. |
| 4 | The steward and the lead accept R2. They note one assumption: that X is finite, which c2 uses and nobody has shown. | a decision, two parties, basis recorded | R2 is canon; the promise is kept. Owed: a whole read of O. |
| 5 | A rewords D. | a write to O, since O holds D | D's acceptance read the old D: **reopened**. c1 and c2 were accepted on D being accepted: both **reopened**. B's re-derivation of c2 read D: lapsed. Their references to D are **stale**. O's whole read read D: lapsed, and O's acceptance is **reopened**. P's slot pointing at D is stale too, and P is not canon, so that is shown and owed by nobody. |
| 6 | The steward and the lead accept the new D. B re-derives c2; someone other than its author re-reads c1 against the new D. The authors affirm their references. R1 and R2 are accepted again. O is read whole and accepted. | decisions and checks | Empty. **S1.** |
| 7 | Later, a check elsewhere shows X is not finite in one case. | a check, contradicting the named assumption | R2's acceptance is **reopened**, though not a word of R2 or of anything it references has changed. The item names the failed assumption and is addressed to the steward and the lead. |
| 8 | They decide again: c2 is narrowed to finite X in a changeset on R2, B re-derives it, and they accept R2 on the new basis. | a write, a check, a decision | Empty. **S2.** |

At S2 the gap in slot 4 is still a gap. It is shown with the steady state as a known truth about what is missing, and it owes nothing.

## What this says about the model

- **Wanted and accepted never had to be the same question.** Suggested to promised to filled was writes to O. Unchecked to checked to accepted was acts about R2. A reader of O at any step could tell the adviser's idea (slot 3 before step 1) from the thing that must be written (slot 3 after it).
- **A promise is kept by acceptance, not by writing.** After step 2 the promised slot held a draft nobody had accepted, and it was still owed.
- **An accepted piece cannot be touched up without being put up again.** Step 5 is a small rewording and it reopened three acceptances. That is the "big perturbation" in small, and the alternative is an accepted mark that survives changes to what was accepted.
- **An assembly's version is its own slots and what fills them, not the versions of the fillers.** O's whole read lapsed at step 1, when its slots changed, and at step 5, when a veritoid it holds changed; not at steps 3 or 4, when nothing in O changed.
- **Held or pointed at was the whole difference** between D and R1: a held piece is written with its holder, a pointed-at one on its own.
- **A decision is reopened by its basis, not by an edit.** At step 7 nothing was rewritten. A named assumption failed and the decision that rested on it stopped backing anything. Had the assumption not been named, nothing would have connected the two.

**What it did not exercise.** The inbox, which is the other main place a segment says what it wants: here every intention arrived already shaped as a slot, where in practice most arrive as a note, and the adviser's vague idea at S0 was more honestly a note on O than a suggested slot. A piece three deep by holding; a slot whose kind fixes it being promised; a gap resolved into slots; a decision reopened because more came to rest on it than it was decided at.
