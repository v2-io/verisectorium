# The stopped bound through the model

**What this run shows.** **The model would not have caught the stopped bound. It would have shown how thin its support was, and only if an act records what kind of question it asked.** Prop A.1S(i) was false for six months. During that time at least three minds who were not its author affirmed the statement itself, in so many words, and the model would have recorded each as a confirming «act» concerning the claim: its validity warranted, no overclaim, nothing owed. Nothing in the record shows that any of those passes asked by a second means: most verified the written proof's steps, and for two of them no means was recorded at all. What broke the claim was a different question, put by a mind checking something else: does this statement survive collision with its neighbour? The record that could have shown the gap is not "who checked" but "by what means", and that is a declaration the «dimension» has to make.

## 1. The case

Prop A.1S(i), in AAT's sector-condition derivation, said the stopped second moment $\mathbb E[\lVert\delta(t\wedge\tau_R)\rVert^2]$ decays to $n\sigma_w^2/(2\alpha)$. Under the segment's own condition (ii) that is false by three lines from the segment's own Corollary A.1S.1: on exit the stopped path sits on the sphere, the mass frozen there does not decay, and the corollary says exit happens. The proof's steps (Itô correction, Grönwall algebra, optional stopping) are each legitimate. The false step is an unstated identification of the killed moment with the stopped one, between them.

## 3. What the model shows, and what it does not

**It does not show (i) as owed.** From the third day (i) has a confirming check by a mind other than its author, and by late May it has several, across model families. Its asserted answer stands confirmed, and a rule that accepts a claim on one independent confirmation accepts it. A steady state. The spike author who relied on (i) on 10-30 would have been told, correctly by the record, that it stood.

**Grain and concerns-within-reads are not what was missing.** They make the record more legible: the audits that only praised the localization step would be seen to concern the step. But the statement itself was affirmed, more than once, and no rule about what an act concerns turns "the stopped bound holds ✓" into a check of something else.

**What was missing is the means.** The record cannot show that any confirmation asked by a second means. Most verified steps of the written proof; for two of them ("plausible … can remain", and the adjudication's "are fine") no means is recorded at all, and that it cannot now be recovered is the point. The break put the statement against the segment's other results and built an instance. Two things in the model bear on this, and both were stated only after this case was read:

- A dimension names the means that count, and its *read as* may require several of them. If AAT's validity read as fully confirmed only when following the derivation, a collision against the segment's own results, and a constructed instance each bind, (i) would have been owed until someone ran the collision, and the collision is what broke it. That is the ontology's declaration to make, and it would have had to be made before anyone knew which means mattered.
- Evidence adds only along independent lines. Several minds following one written proof are one line of evidence said several times, and a confirmation that records no means cannot be shown to be a second. A reading that counted lines and not acts would have shown (i) with one line of support it could vouch for, however many confirmations.

So a ‹check› says not only which dimension it answers and what it concerns, but by what means, in words the dimension declares; the criterion fixed first is where that is written. With that, a later reader sees for each confirmation which means it used, and sees at a glance that none was a collision and none an instance. Without it they see: confirmed four times.

**Also not shown.** That the audits which broke (iii) named (i) as the fallback, and the repair then leaned on it: a refutation of one claim acted as support for its neighbour and nothing records the leaning. That an act which confirmed two claims and was contradicted on one of them the same day still counts in full for the other. And whether any confirming check was right; the model makes what it asked countable and no more.

**What the model does change.** "RESOLVED — do not re-open" would be inert, and the file would not have told later auditors to stop. The 10-30 break is on the record before any repair. The dependent left unchecked stays visible. The step checks are kept as the steps' warrants and survive the rewrite, where binding at the file lapsed them all.

## 4. What this says about the model

- The honesty constraint is necessary and was not sufficient here. (i) did not outrun its record; its record could vouch for one line of evidence and read as four.
- "Which of the several questions under *valid* did this check ask" has to be something the ontology declares and an act records. One dimension called validity, read as "highest confirmed rung", reproduces this failure exactly as it happened.
- Independence of acts is not a side gap. Minds of three model families agreed, none recording a means other than the written proof, and nothing shows they were more than one line.
- The cheapest thing that would have found it was never scheduled: nobody was asked to check (i). It was found as a side effect of verifying a new claim placed beside it. A dimension whose producer never fires on a piece is visible only if never-asked-by-this-means is.
