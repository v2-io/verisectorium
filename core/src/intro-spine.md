# The whole thing, first

A «verisectorium» is a place for cutting truth. Every one in the estate began as a pile of influx (quotes gathered on a subject, spikes, drafts, results, an older theory's files) and was dissected until each piece could contribute on its own, adjudicated by recorded acts, refined toward agreement with what it is held to, and reassembled into coherent results that others read and build on. That production is the purpose; results built on adjudicated results are how discovery and decision proceed, and how work compounds under turnover instead of being redone. Concretely a verisectorium is an ontology, a corpus of what has been written, acta of what has been done, a history, and praxes for the minds that work in it.

**Two kinds of statement are adjudicated, and everything else reduces to them.** A *truth claim* is right or wrong against something beyond anyone's say-so, and it stands by evidence, which accumulates and which anyone with a sound method may add to or overturn. A «decision» is a choice made at a branch point by a party who answers for it, and it stands by that party's backing while its basis holds. Each rests on the other: a decision's grounds are truth claims, and every piece of evidence is at bottom a fact someone attested, with a decision about where to stop asking. A measurement is an instrument attesting a truth claim; an automated action is a decision someone made in advance.

The five sentences that follow are the **machinery** that keeps the production trustworthy. The **practical model** a mind at work meets (an outline with marked slots, document-like segments as markdown, an inbox, a frontier, a steady state) is built on this machinery and is not the same thing as it; the «verisectorium» def says more.

1. **The corpus holds what was written and the acta what was done. Everything said about how well a piece stands is read from that record, never typed from outside it.** The corpus holds many true sentences, but it holds no verdict on them except what the acts give: a true claim nobody has checked and a false one everyone has passed look the same to it until an act says otherwise. A claim believed for good reasons that later fails is not a malfunction.
2. **A dimension is a constraint.** Each question asked of a piece is answered either by finding out or by deciding, and what canon says may not outrun the record: it may not assert what evidence on record contradicts, say of anything that it stands better than it does, or report a decision that is not on record with its basis holding. A violation is owed; only what has been accepted can owe.
3. **A steady state is one where nothing is owed, except by recorded waiver.** A disturbance opens a changeset whose scope is what it reaches; inside that scope the verisectorium is in work time, and the frontier says exactly what is still owed and who can clear it; outside every open scope it is steady. The waivers are a steady state's stated conditions.
4. **Writing happens in whole segments, at one logical instant; warrant moves one act at a time.** A segment is a veritoid or an assembly of slots, to any depth; whether a thing is wanted in a place is written in the assembly, and whether it is accepted is a decision about the thing.
5. **Better is a direction along the dimensions the ontology names as determinants.** It is a partial order, not a score and not a queue. Softening is the cheapest way back to a steady state; strengthen-first is what the direction adds and is the daily work. What to work on next is a decision.

And one rule under all five: **identity is minted and outlives content.** Acts bind to versions; versions are of something; that something has an id minted for it and never reused.
