# What "better" is

*Moved from the first-iteration overview (`MODEL.md`, Fable 5.1, 2026-11-08) into a segment of its own on Joseph's suggestion, so that it narrates the defs it rests on and lapses when they change. Provisional. Where a thing is Joseph's it says so; [M] marks the least tested.*

A checkpoint is *feasible*: every constraint holds or is accepted. **Better** is a direction within feasibility: a later checkpoint is better than an earlier one when, on the determinant dimensions, no warrant has fallen and some has risen, or the content answers to a determinant more strongly in a sense the ontology declares (AAT: a claim that entails another is stronger; weaker hypotheses, stronger conclusion). Two checkpoints can each be better on a different determinant with neither better overall; choosing is a decision, and so is where effort goes next. No composite score lives on a piece. The one place the estate combines scores openly is when choosing between candidates in a decision, and that is where it should stay.

This is why softening is honest and still not enough: lowering an assertion is the cheapest way back to feasibility, and a verisectorium softened to tautologies would be perfectly steady and worth nothing. Strengthen-first is what the direction adds and the engine does not enforce.

Your two regimes need nothing of their own. A theory claim asserts flatly and its determinant dimensions read true or false; its doubt is a list of named open checks beside it, not a number. An empirical fact or a proposed model is read by degree. They join at the hypothesis: a flat if-then resting on a model whose adequacy carries the graded part. A verisectorium that wants numeric credence declares a dimension whose *read as* computes one from the acts, as relata does.
