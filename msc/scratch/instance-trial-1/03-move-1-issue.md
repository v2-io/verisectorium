## What I had to compute by hand, and whether it was determinate

Every frontier cell came out the same way when I recomputed it. The exceptions are where I had to choose an ontology rule the model left open: what an unanswered finding does to a warrant (step 2), what an act reads of an assembly (step 3), and whether "all fillers of a required slot" or "at least one" must be accepted (step 6, for G with p3 and p4 in it; I took *all*). With those fixed, the move was mechanical. That is the strongest positive result of the trial.

## What the model did *not* raise

- x1. The rationale's reason became wrong at step 5, when p1 changed, but x1 narrates r3, and r3 never changed. Nothing went stale. A2 caught it by reading. The thing x1 actually explains is the *decision* a3, and a reference cannot point at an act (finding 1).
- The new ground at step 7 ("both separators make `a:b=c` ambiguous") is a truth claim about the rules, the kind of thing *consistent* checks. Written only in a decision's basis, as I did, it is unchecked and invisible. Written as a piece, it would need a kind: a premise is "about the world", and this is about the rules (finding 13).
