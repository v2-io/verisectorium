# Account: the v2 kernel in Rust (Opus 5.5)

*Spike on branch `spike/kernel-rust-opus`, from base `58dd5c1`. Everything here was written 2026-11-07 by one Opus 5.5 instance. Tiers: "tested" means property-tested or unit-tested as stated; "compiler-checked" means a `compile_fail` doctest shows the violation is rejected; "proved" would mean discharged by a tool for all inputs within a stated bound. **Nothing in this spike reached "proved"**: every Kani run was stopped before it finished (the last batch at the coordinator's request, for CPU). See the proof section.*

## Proof

**Headline: tested thoroughly, proved nothing.** Every property P1–P8 is property-tested over 1024 generated logs per property. Those logs are shaped to exercise each case (a coverage test enforces this), checked against an independent oracle, and the suite was mutation-checked (20 seeded faults, all caught). Kani harnesses exist for P3–P7 over the kernel's own code. No Kani run finished: two were stopped by me after 201 s and ~6.5 min, and eight were stopped after ~10–18 min at the coordinator's request because they were starving the other spikes of CPU. A run that didn't finish is evidence neither way. "Result unknown" below means exactly that.

### P1–P8

| | status | how | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** Determinism | tested | `p1_determinism`: folding the same records twice gives equal `State`s; the incremental head equals a fresh fold; `state_at(i)` equals the fold of exactly the first i records; positions/ceilings/frontier agree. Plus the oracle test on every prefix. | Argued, not proved: `State` holds only `BTreeMap`/`Vec`, with no interior mutability, IO, time, randomness or hash-seeded containers, and the computed functions take `&self`. No Rust tool states "is a function of its inputs" as a contract (see below). |
| **P2** Append-only | compiler-checked + tested | Four `compile_fail` doctests on `Log` (private record vector; no `&mut` to records or head state; `State::apply` crate-private), each paired with a compiling twin and confirmed rejected for the intended reason (E0616, E0308 ×2, E0624). `p2_append_only`: after each append, every earlier prefix of records and every earlier `state_at` is unchanged. | The compiler checks the violations I named, not "no operation removes a record" in general. That isn't a quantifiable contract in Rust (see below). |
| **P3** Lapse is exact | tested; Kani result unknown | `p3_lapse_is_exact`, both directions: an act that bound stops binding iff it read an (id, version) the changeset changed or removed; an act reading nothing in the segment keeps its binding; a refused changeset changes nothing. Mutation "binds uses any instead of all" is caught. | Harnesses `p3_lapse_exact` (log ≤ 2 records, never run) and `p3_step` (any acyclic pre-state, 2 ids, any act, any changeset): bounded check started, aborted for CPU; result unknown. |
| **P4** Monotone under acts | tested (strong form); Kani result unknown | `p4_monotone_under_acts`: appending any act lowers no position, changes only positions of what it concerns, and **adds nothing to the frontier**. | `p4_step`: bounded check started, aborted for CPU; result unknown. |
| **P5** Acyclicity | tested; Kani result unknown | `p5_acyclicity`: every prefix state is acyclic, and every cycle refusal names a real cycle of the graph the changeset would have made (edge by edge). `refusals_are_warranted` checks the other refusals' stated reasons. | `p5_acyclic` (3-record fold, 3 ids): stopped by me at 201 s. `p5_step` (an induction step; acyclicity checked by a brute-force test independent of the kernel's `find_cycle`): at 3 ids stopped by me at ~6.5 min; at 2 ids aborted for CPU, twice (B-tree and small-map variants). Result unknown. Recorded choice: refusals are *recorded* (the attempt is in the log) with the verdict *computed* at fold, never stored. |
| **P6** Ceiling bound | tested, including tightness | `p6_ceiling_bound`: ceiling(v) ≤ position(v), ≤ ceiling(t) for every held target, `sketch` for a missing one, and *equals* the min (so it is the latent level, not just a bound). The oracle computes ceilings by fixpoint iteration instead of recursion and agrees. | `p6_p7_any_state`: bounded check started, aborted for CPU; result unknown. |
| **P7** Steady means honest | tested | `p7_steady_means_honest`, through the never-vacuous form "overclaim(v) is on the frontier iff claimed > ceiling"; `p7_repairable_by_acts`: from any generated state, acts alone discharge everything except what a dangling reference causes, and the resulting steady states are honest. | Same harness as P6; result unknown. P7 is close to definitional once the frontier is right; the substance is in the frontier, which the oracle test covers. |
| **P8** Replay | tested | `p8_replay`: text round trip gives identical records, an identical `Log`, and an identical state at every index; a file round trip with spaces, `%`, newlines, empty and non-ASCII strings. | No harness: the codec is string work, which is where Kani was weakest. |

Further tested properties, beyond the spec's eight: `kernel_matches_oracle` (differential, every prefix), `changeset_scope` (a changeset writes exactly its segment), `version_covers_references` (a version names one written content across the whole log; this is what found the hole in finding 4), `refusals_are_warranted`, `revert_revives`, `unmet_need_is_subsumed_unless_needed_exceeds_claim`, and `generator_coverage` (non-vacuity: fifteen behaviours each at least 20 per 1000 logs, rarest observed about 80).

**Mutation check of the test suite.** I seeded 20 faults into the kernel one at a time, against the kernel as of commit `31c55f2` (before the `Map`/`Set` alias, which changes no logic), and ran the suite against each. All 20 were caught: binding by any instead of all; position ignoring binding; position counting reads instead of concerns; ceiling ignoring references; a missing target counting as rederived; an affirmation not needing to bind, or to concern v; checks also reconciling; no cycle check (caught by a crash: ceiling recursion overflowed the stack on a cycle, so it was not an assertion that caught it); a changeset keeping old members; overclaim using ≥; unmet-need using position; no version-reuse check; version reuse checked only against the held version (the pre-strengthening kernel, caught only by `version_covers_references`); foreign ids allowed; concerns ⊄ reads allowed; refused acts still counted; codec dropping concerns or empty strings; `state_at` off by one. The harness is `mutate.py` in my scratchpad and is not committed. The mutants and the tests that killed each are listed here.

### What I believe Rust could prove that I did not reach, and its cost

- **The Kani harnesses as written, finished.** They're the bounded result the toolchain offers on the shipped code: P3, P4, P5 and P6/P7, from *any* acyclic pre-state, at 2 ids, 2 segments, versions {1, 2}, ≤ 2 refs, ≤ 2 reads, ≤ 3 recorded acts. Starting from an arbitrary pre-state makes them independent of log length, and p5_step is a genuine induction step. CBMC also checks every panic, index and overflow on the way, so a pass would have proved the absence of panics in `apply`, `positions`, `ceilings` and `frontier` within the bounds. **Cost unknown**: none finished within 10–18 min on a contended machine, and I cannot say whether they would have in 30 min or 30 hours. The ids-as-`u8` swap and the sorted-`Vec` map swap (`smallmap.rs`, property-tested to agree with std operation by operation) are the two concessions already made for the prover. The 3-act bound is complete for 2-id states by an argument (`any_acts` doc) that is mine, not machine-checked.
- **Unbounded proofs** would need Verus, Creusot or Prusti. I didn't attempt any of them, so these are estimates, marked as such. P3 and the position half of P4 are nearly definitional (binding is "all reads current"; a changeset changes versions only in its segment) and should be easy in Verus over spec-level maps. P6 and the frontier half of P4 need induction over the acyclic graph, so acyclicity would have to be stated as the existence of a rank function, and `find_cycle` proved to establish it. That is the expensive part. Each would mean rewriting the kernel's containers against vstd's verified ones, or trusting a spec layer over std's `BTreeMap`. My guess: P3/P4-positions in hours; P5 + P6 + the frontier half of P4 in days; and the shipped kernel would no longer be idiomatic Rust.

### What cannot be stated as a contract here, and why

- **P1** ("a function of the log prefix"): Rust has no effect system and no purity contract. Determinism follows from the language semantics of this code (no interior mutability, no global state, ordered containers), which a reader can audit but no tool states. Kani cannot state it usefully either, because it executes deterministically, so `fold(r) == fold(r)` is vacuous there. *The language.*
- **P2** ("no operation removes or alters a record"): "operation" ranges over every API a caller could use, including dropping or replacing the whole `Log`, and over the log on disk, which no library type governs. What is statable is per-violation rejection (the `compile_fail` checks) plus the privacy argument. *The spec is imprecise* (what is an operation, and is the file on disk in scope?); *the language* can only show specific violations rejected.
- **"Equal version means identical content"**, on which P3's honesty rests: content is not in the model, so nothing can check it. The kernel enforces the part it can see (references, log-wide). *The spec*: it should require content hashes.
- **P3–P7 unboundedly**: statable in Kani only as bounded harnesses. Function and loop contracts in Kani are unstable features I didn't try. *The toolchain*, given the choice to stay on shipped Rust.
- **P8 under Kani**: statable, but the codec's string handling is the worst case for CBMC. *The toolchain*, in practice.

### What Rust's verification story buys the model, judged from one side

What Rust gave cheaply and *always on*: invariants carried by types, checked on every build at no proof cost. An affirmation that cannot carry a rung; an exhaustive match over every owed-item and refusal kind; a log that cannot be written except by `append`; a state that cannot exist except as a fold. Property testing with an oracle and shrinking was strong and fast. The spec findings came from working the example by hand and then pinning each with a test against the kernel; the kernel's own soundness hole (version reuse) came from a property test. None came from a prover.

What it did not give me, on this machine and this afternoon, was a single completed proof. The mainstream Rust path to proof is either bounded model checking, which choked on std's B-tree before reaching the kernel's own logic, or a verification dialect, which means rewriting the kernel. From the Rust side I can't judge what gnatprove discharges on the Ada spikes. But the Ada spec's contracts would sit on the shipped code and be unbounded, which is precisely the gap I couldn't close. Whether that wins depends on what those spikes report about the cost of loop invariants and containers. I have no basis for guessing.

### The one property whose proof would most change my confidence

**P3, lapse exactness, together with P4's strong form ("only writing adds an owed item").** Everything the store says about a piece is computed from acts that still bind. If lapse is exact, no status outlives what it rested on and nothing lapses that shouldn't. If acts can only remove owed items, the frontier is honest about where debt comes from. I did not get a proof of either. I got both tested over generated logs with a coverage floor and a mutation check, which is strong evidence and not a proof.

## Where the spec is ill-formed, and what I built against

These are the findings I would most want the model to take back. Findings 1–5 are each pinned by a named test, so they are mechanically checked facts about the spec, not my reading of it. Finding 6 records decisions, and 7 records observations, two of them tested.

**1. The §6 expected frontier contradicts §3.** After the perturbation, §6 lists seven owed items. Computed from §3's own definitions, using values §6 itself states (ceiling(e) = sketch, ceiling(c) = sketch):
- three items are missing from §6: `overclaim(e)` (claimed drafted > ceiling sketch), `unmet-need(d, c)` and `unmet-need(e, c)` (both need c at drafted, and ceiling(c) = sketch);
- one listed item cannot hold: `stale-reference(d, c)`. A rewrote d to d@2 *because* c changed, so d@2 was written against c@2.

Test: `the_spec_expected_list_is_inconsistent_with_its_own_contracts`. The corrected list is in `after_perturbation_the_frontier_is_what_the_contracts_compute`. My guess at the cause: the list was written by asking "who points at c?" (giving stale for d, e, u) and "who claims more than c can give?" (giving c, d, u), without running each definition over each veritoid. That is exactly the error the computed frontier exists to prevent.

**2. With an inert affirmation, the narrative cannot reach S1.** The spec says an affirmation "never counts toward a rung" and gives it no other effect. But e is never rewritten, so its reference still says "written against c@1". Nothing in the narrative reconciles it with c@2, so `stale-reference(e, c)` survives to the end and S1 is not steady. Test: `without_the_affirmation_s1_is_unreachable`. The model's def of segment changeset says what an affirmation is *for*: "every inner dependent either changed or carried with the author's affirmation that it is still right". **Built:** an affirmation's one computed effect is to reconcile references. `stale-reference(v, t)` is discharged when a *binding* affirmation concerning v read t at t's current version. It never touches position, so it never touches ceiling. Because it binds like any act, it lapses the moment e or c changes again. With that, the narrative reaches S1 exactly as the spec says, and the log still holds B's first re-derivation of d@2, binding to nothing.

**3. The act tuple lacks `concerns`, and without it acts must under-record what they read.** The spec's position rule raises every veritoid an act *read*. Its prose says "the rung the act attests for the veritoid(s) it **concerns**". The two come apart as soon as an act reads context. B's re-derivation of u must read c, because u rests on c. Recorded honestly, under the spec's rule it raises c to rederived, though nobody checked c. So the spec's own example records B's check as reading only `u@1`. The consequence is the quiet part: since the check didn't read c, c's change doesn't lapse it. Through the whole perturbation, position(u) stays `rederived` on a re-derivation done against a premise that no longer exists. Only the ceiling and the stale reference carry the change. The model's def of act says "recording too little is as dishonest as claiming too much", and the spec's tuple forces recording too little. **Built:** `Act.concerns ⊆ keys(Act.reads)`, checked at fold. Position counts only concerned veritoids, and binding uses all reads. Test: `reading_as_context_does_not_attest`. Every act in §6 is recorded with concerns = reads, so §6's numbers are unchanged.

**4. A version must name one content across the whole log, or revival lies.** `binds` is a function of the current versions only, so a revert *revives* every act that read the reverted-to version (`revert_revives`). With content hashes this is right and even nice: the act's whole input is back, byte for byte. P3 speaks only of lapse and is silent on revival. But versions here may be counters, and the kernel cannot see content. My first kernel checked "same version, different references" only against the version currently held. So remove-then-rewrite, or c@2 then c@1 again, could give c@1 new references and resurrect checks of the old c@1. The property test I wrote to state "a version names one written content" was what exposed it. **Built:** `Refusal::VersionReused` checks every earlier writing of `(id, version)` in the log. Mutation-checked: the earlier kernel fails `version_covers_references`. What the kernel still cannot check is the *content* behind a version. That stays the writer's promise, and the model should probably require content hashes so that it becomes a fact instead.

**5. P5 asks "recorded or reported"; I chose recorded, with the verdict computed.** `Log::append` records every submitted record, whether or not it takes effect. Whether it takes effect, and why not, is computed by the fold from the prefix state and is never stored as a verdict. Replay recomputes refusals (`a_cycle_is_refused_and_the_attempt_is_recorded`). This is the model's own rule ("computed from recorded acts, never typed") applied to refusals. A refused attempt is something that happened, and a stored verdict could be edited. The other refusals: an act that concerns nothing or concerns something it did not read; a changeset naming a veritoid another segment holds (`ForeignVeritoid`); `VersionReused`.

**6. Smaller decisions the spec left open.**
- A reference to a veritoid that does not exist contributes `sketch` to the ceiling. Nothing can rest on what isn't there. It produces `stale-reference`, plus `unmet-need` when anything above sketch is needed. Acts cannot repair it; only a changeset can (`p7_repairable_by_acts`).
- Ids are unique store-wide. A changeset may not write another segment's veritoid. Moving one is remove-in-U then add-in-T, two changesets, and acts reading `x@v` revive across the move. That is the model's open gap "veritoid identity under recut", met in practice.
- `claimed` is not covered by the version: softening at the same version is allowed and lapses nothing. References *are* covered.
- An act may read versions that do not (yet) exist. It is recorded and binds if and when they appear. That allows checks of staged work-time content. It also allows a check to be pre-recorded against a version not yet written, which, with counters, is a guess dressed as a check. A model question, not decided here.

**7. Observations for the model, not departures.**
- **Only writing can add an owed item.** A strengthening of P4, tested: appending any act (check or affirmation) never adds anything to the frontier, it only removes (`p4_monotone_under_acts`). The frontier grows only through changesets. Every owed item traces back to a write.
- **`needed` mostly duplicates the ceiling.** If `needed ≤ claimed(v)`, then `unmet-need(v, t)` implies `overclaim(v)`: ceiling(v) ≤ ceiling(t) < needed ≤ claimed(v). Tested: `unmet_need_is_subsumed_unless_needed_exceeds_claim`. The rung `needed` therefore says something of its own only when it exceeds v's own claim. Meanwhile the ceiling ignores `needed` entirely: d "needs c only at drafted", yet d can never be above c. Either `needed` is a different thing from the latent level (a lower bound the author asserts, distinct from the cap the premise imposes), or the ceiling should consult it. The spec's example happens never to separate the two.
- **Multi-read checks lapse as a unit.** B's re-derivation of (c@2, d@2) lapses for *c* when only d changes to d@3, so "B re-derives again" must re-read c. In this domain that is right (d is c's derivation). In general it means an act's granularity is a modelling choice with real cost.

## What the language made easy

- **Sum types carried the model's distinctions at no cost.** `Record`, `ActKind`, `Owed`, `Refusal` and `Outcome` are enums, and every `match` on them is checked for exhaustiveness. The spec's act tuple gives an affirmation a rung that means nothing. `ActKind::Affirmation` has no rung field, so the meaningless value cannot be written. When I renamed the refusal for version reuse, the compiler found every place that had to learn about it, including the coverage test's tally.
- **Derives did half of P1 and P8.** `#[derive(PartialOrd, Ord)]` on `Rung` *is* the ladder (declaration order). `#[derive(PartialEq, Eq)]` on `State` and `Log` makes "the same state" and "the same log" one-line assertions. `BTreeMap` everywhere makes iteration order a function of contents, so nothing printed or compared depends on a hash seed.
- **Privacy is how P2 is held, and the compiler checks the obvious violations.** `Log.records` is private; the only mutator is `append`; `State::apply` is crate-private, so every `State` a caller can hold is the fold of some log prefix. Four `compile_fail` doctests on `Log` (each paired with a compiling twin) check that writing to a recorded log, getting `&mut` to its records or head state, or advancing a state without a record are rejected. I checked each was rejected for the intended reason: E0616, E0308 twice, E0624.
- **proptest plus a naive oracle was the most productive test design.** One property compares the kernel against a second implementation written straight from §3 by a different route (positions by scanning, ceilings by fixpoint iteration), on every prefix of 1024 generated logs. Shrinking turned failures into four-record counterexamples. The failing seeds are kept in `tests/properties.proptest-regressions`.
- **Kani is a cargo subcommand.** Harnesses sit in the crate under `#[cfg(kani)]` and target the crate's own code, with no translation step. That was the easy part; finishing a run was not (next section).
- **Edition 2024's `let … else` and let-chains** kept the fold short and flat.
- **The borrow checker never objected.** That includes the iterative DFS, which holds `stack.last_mut()` while pushing. This kernel is values all the way down (no shared mutable graph, no lifetimes in the API), which is the shape Rust handles best. I chose that shape partly because it is honest to the spec (state = fold of records), not to dodge the borrow checker. But a kernel with an incremental index or a persistent store would meet the borrow checker in earnest, and this spike says nothing about that.

## Where I fought the tool

- **Kani against `String` and `BTreeMap`, and the result: no completed proof.** The most trivial harness (one changeset, at most two veritoids with one reference each, check acyclicity) had not finished after about 6 minutes at 3 GB resident. The machine was shared with other spikes' CBMC runs, so the wall-clock time is inflated by an unknown factor. I stopped it. CBMC's trace shows the time going into `alloc::collections::btree::node` internals and byte-wise string comparison, not into the kernel's logic. The fix was `model::Name`: `String` normally, `u8` under `cfg(kani)`. `state.rs` and `log.rs` use ids only through `Eq`/`Ord`/`Clone`, so the verified source is the shipped source. But what is verified is that source *instantiated at `u8` ids*, A generic `State<I: Ord + Clone>` would make the instantiation explicit in the types. I didn't do it, because it would have threaded a type parameter through every signature for the prover's sake. Even at `u8` ids and 2 of them, the step harnesses were still in symbolic execution after 18 min, with the trace in `alloc::collections::btree::node`. So I swapped std's B-trees for a sorted-`Vec` map under `cfg(kani)` (`smallmap.rs`, tested against std). That cut memory to about a third, but those runs too were still in symbolic execution at ~10 min when all runs were stopped for CPU. Two concessions to the prover, both confined to `cfg(kani)`, and still no verdict. That is the most important language-and-toolchain fact this spike produced. I also count a cost to other people here: my four-way parallel runs, together with the other Rust spike's, put eight CBMC processes on the shared machine and starved the other agents. I should have asked before going parallel on a shared box.
- **Vacuity, the real fight, and not the language's fault.** My first generator produced logs in which an affirmation discharged anything in 2 of 1000 cases, and about half of all acts were malformed. Every property "passed", and P4's affirmation half and the reconciliation path were barely exercised. A coverage test (`generator_coverage`) now fails if any of fifteen behaviours is rarer than 20 per 1000 logs. Reshaping the generator to read current versions, carry veritoids and act on held ones raised the rarest to about 80. Then the richer generator surfaced the version-reuse hole (finding 4).
- **Small papercuts.** `prop_assert!` stringifies its expression into a format string, so `Owed::Overclaim { v }` inside it fails to compile (bind it to a variable first). `prop_assume!` in a property whose precondition got rarer as the generator improved hit proptest's global reject limit and failed depending on the seed. Replacing the assumption with a stronger, unconditional property was better anyway (see `p7_repairable_by_acts`). An external SIGTERM killed one Kani run three seconds in, during compilation. I didn't send it, and a re-run was clean.

Split: the language itself cost close to nothing; the kernel and its tests were most of the thinking; the verification toolchain was most of the waiting.
