## In brief

A little over a hundred separately named questions turned up, sorted below into eleven families by what each one asks. The number means little, since some rows are one question in two stores. Six things came out of laying them side by side that I did not find said plainly anywhere, though parts of each are.

1. **Almost no question is about the piece alone.** Nearly every one has a hidden second argument: faithful *to* what, current *against* what, understood *by* whom, important *for* what, permitted *to* whom. Joseph said this of importance ("always needs a target subject"). It looks true of nearly all of them, including the ones with nothing to do with truth. What makes an answer lapse is a change in either argument. (Finding 1)
2. **"Ladder, unordered set, or number" covers only a few of the answer shapes in actual use.** The others include conjunctions of named checks, per-span marks where the lowest wins, a position with a separate credibility column, open lists, sets of per-judge verdicts, weighted factors with vetoes, and comparisons that exist only between candidates. (Finding 2)
3. **The questions are not a flat set; the working stores order them, in three different ways.** One answer can be a prerequisite for the meaning of another, can take precedence over another in a conflict, or can veto outright. Stores that do this most deliberately are vivarium, ai-risk-model and udon's spec. The current «truth·determinant» text says each is asked about separately, which is true, and says nothing about their order. (Finding 3)
4. **"Is it valid?" is the most heavily used question and the most fused one.** The recorded errors separate into at least eight different questions, and the checks that passed the stopped bound were answering a different one from the check that broke it. Of 500 recorded check outcomes in ASF, 84 are neither "confirmed" nor "broken" but "narrowed". (Finding 4)
5. **Four families get little or no place in the earlier lists:** how much latitude remains (forced, chosen, tuned, arbitrary), what has been promised, whether it may be used or shown, and questions about the minds rather than the content. (Finding 5)
6. **The field usually called "the epistemic status" has repeatedly held several questions at once** (five for ASF's; see Second pass), and one current term, «claimed·level», still reads as singular although its own Part II neighbours have begun to take it apart. (Finding 6)

**The largest limit on this census.** I read `core/def/truth-determinant.md` and its cold audit in my first pass, before most of the material. The brief's request not to be told the recent sort therefore did not fully hold: I had seen it. I named the column "relative to" and not "held against" to keep some distance, but the reader should treat my sort as not independent of that term.

### What laying the reports together adds

Six things, in my words. They are readings over the reports, not measured, and the same-lineage caution applies.

**A. Standing fields that depend on someone returning stop.** ASF `stage`: 205 of 247 at `draft`, and FORMAT says "currently ignored in practice". Vivarium: 115 of 115 at `draft`, and no other value in git history. Comproprium: 57 of 57. Udon spec-lite: `verification-level` on 0 records, `awaiting-second` true on 236 of 236. Fields a tool computes, or a mechanism forces, held. Standing that gets used lives in clause-level prose. Vivarium's reader adds that its strongest answers are refusals, not records (a read-only handle, a compile-included ledger). *What it puts to the model:* per question, is this a record or a refusal, and who or what will ever write it?

**B. Attestations of acts are different from claims about the artifact.** Who decided, who read it, what was predicted beforehand can only be recorded; claims about the artifact can be convicted by running something. It is the vivarium reader's distinction, and the udon and source-store reports independently rank attribution highest. Vivarium: "THIS IS THE ONE FIELD WHERE AN ERROR IS NOT MERELY WRONG." *What it puts to the model:* whether one «value» covers both.

**C. Much of standing is an obligation on the next mind.** The udon reader's point, and reader A's by a different route: flags say who moves next, `strength` says how revisitable, the sections are addressed to someone about to use the claim. Joseph on leans and rulings (udon 3.2) is about tone at the moment of reopening.

**D. Attribution can displace the question it serves.** Joseph, as the udon reader quotes him: "they cared so much about the provenance correctness that they forgot for a moment to even care whether it was a good decision or not." That reader's note is the caution to keep: each field that makes authority more legible increases this risk.

**E. Absence has at least seven kinds.** Not asked; asked and unanswered; not applicable; looked and not found, with a named search; deliberately left open; decided to be empty; hidden from this reader on purpose (tools B2). ai-risk-model's draft has seven more for sources. Nearly every store reinvented this.

**F. There is a real cost to all this.** Neurips records that the register carrying its discipline "reads to outside reviewers as jargon-padding". limen runs on tables of four or five columns and no tier ladder. The tools reader's caution stands: this is a catalogue of what has been useful somewhere, not a schema for every piece.

### 3. The stores order the questions, in three different ways

**As a prerequisite for meaning.** The ai-risk-model layers are stated by Joseph as "each depending on the ones before it being true". RC1 says "Fidelity underlies everything". ASF's stages are "ordered: a segment at `claims-verified` has also passed `deps-verified`".

**As precedence in a conflict.** Vivarium: "B OVER A (Joseph, 2026-07-03): the same laws admit many histories", and "Lawful first, Earth-shaped second". Its worked warning is "VIBE-MODELING = HIGH D RIDING ON LOW B/C", a rule about which answers are allowed to run ahead of which. N06 has validity gating and coherence following: "Validity gates the change; coherence goes on the frontier."

**As a veto or a weighted trade.** udon's CTQ table has vetoes and weights, and treats "impact radius" as "a cost, not a quality". Relata's pass is a conjunction. In councils, what an "object" does depends on importance.

«truth·determinant» says that a piece may answer to more than one and "each is asked about separately", and its working notes lean toward never combining them into one score. The field evidence is mixed in a useful way: the one place the estate openly combines (udon CTQ) scores **candidates in a decision**, not pieces in a store. My reading is that combination belongs to decisions between alternatives, and that this may be the principled line. It is one specimen.

*What it puts to the model:* whether an ontology declares an order among its determinants or dimensions, of which of the three kinds, and whether a roll-up may read across them at all.

### 9. What gets adjudicated without anyone saying so

Each of these shows something being settled by an arrangement.

- **Being in the body.** ASF's discipline makes the body's flat sentence itself a verdict: "ASF has worked very hard to *state the currently known truth*" (N12 addendum). The verdict has no mark, because being there is the mark.
- **A note saying it is settled.** "RESOLVED in canon — do not re-open" sat above a false result for five months, and one later auditor reported "this segment pre-answered my strongest planned attack". That is Disposition exerting inhibitory influence, with no record that anyone decided to.
- **Where a note sits.** RC1/07's placement-for-influence is what the Working Notes convention was doing without the term.
- **An unmarked outline row.** Without a provisional mark, agents "immediately and implicitly mistrust the outline and canon and verisectorium, and either adhere too strongly to that draft outline, or pick and choose and stop distinguishing between what is grounded and what is not" (N14).
- **Which file a thing is in.** Slug prefixes carry kind; comproprium's three directories carry failure mode by design.
- **A vestigial field.** ASF's `stage:` was still present while "Joseph ignores `stage:`" (system-review §2), so a declared answer had stopped being an answer.
- **The audit that recommended a fallback.** Audits that broke (iii) "recommended (i) as the safe fallback", adjudicating (i) without checking it.
- **Mixed.** Epistemic Status sections are prose that holds explicit verdicts, hand-run projections and records of checks done together (15 kinds of thing in the 62 sections read, prep/`research/asf-inline-labels-2026-11-06.md`).

## Where the material disagrees with itself or the current model

1. **Fidelity is three things.** RC1's Fidelity is about an account of what happened, and "nothing after capture" moves it. Carriage and the ai-risk quote levels are about a copy of a source, and they rise with checking (level 1 to 3). A third sense is about a restatement of the store's own body (A12). All have been called fidelity.
2. **Is a usage license earned?** RC1/03 calls it a bound, "declared, not secured by degrees", and in the same cell "projected from Evidence outcomes + Backing declaration". `«bound»` says "There are no degrees of a bound." "Usable as a hypothesis" looks earned; "do not revive" looks declared.
3. **Weakest link.** Vivarium's design has a memo's state as "a weakest-link fold over its dependency cone"; PLAN A2 deliberately replaced Joseph's weakest-link phrasing with a conjunction and an interval; his 08-14 remark names max and gcd as two different useful aggregates. No document reconciles these. They may be answers to different questions.
4. **Every kind answers to a determinant.** `«truth·determinant»`'s invariant meets a licence record, an orientation gate, an open question and a queue item. The audit raised some of this; the census adds that these are not edge cases in the working stores.
5. **A kind "chooses among them and adds none of its own"** («truth·determinant»). udon's convention has "a ladder each kind declares", and the working note there, recorded at Joseph's request, says that in a spec it "is mostly authorization" and in a theory "derivation and evidence". That is per-kind verification, at the level below determinants. Whether they conflict depends on what a determinant is taken to fix.
6. **The body of a segment is both.** `«veritoid»` content is ‹written›; Epistemic Status prose "is written content about the claim" (N15), and yet some of what it says is what has been done (items 8 and 12 of the 62-section reading, checks in prose). Those parts have no home once the record exists.
7. **Stage.** ASF's stage is "Orthogonal to epistemic status"; vivarium's is "a current-state marker, not a gate"; udon's doc-state is "not a ladder" and reports a fact. The outline has no term for process state as distinct from `«value»`.

## What I could not tell

- Whether the eleven families are the right cut. Joseph's own sort (by what builds it) and RC1's (by what falsifies it) cut across mine and I have not shown any of the three to be better. Family C and family A could be one family under a sort by mover.
- How the shapes in Finding 2 would look after "what is a question" is settled. Some may be the same shape read twice.
- Whether the "relative to" filler is one thing. A standard, a reader and a target may behave differently enough that one slot is the wrong abstraction.
- Whether what I counted as implicit in Finding 9 was implicit to the people doing it. Some of it may have been deliberate and just undocumented.
- Any count here beyond the ones marked as measured is indicative. The 500 outcome lines are from a `grep` and are not deduplicated against vsect's event store (which the sprint counted as 488 checks).

The most useful next step this census suggests is N16's first question, still unanswered: put one real root through whatever is decided, with a written guess beforehand of which of these questions it will strain.
