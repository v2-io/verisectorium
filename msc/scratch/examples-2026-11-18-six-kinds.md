# Six kinds of verisectorium, run through quickly

*Fable 5.2 session, 2026-11-18, at Joseph's request: "Run through several examples in scratch … and then see if you can formulate a better '1-paragraph overview'". All six are invented, at the size of a thought experiment. They are evidence for nothing; they are a way of finding out what a one-paragraph statement has to cover. Findings are recorded and not answered with mechanisms. The kinds are from his list of 11-06 (theory, empirical work, a formal spec, concept engineering or essays, world-building) plus intake, which every one of them starts with.*

*For each: the pieces; what is asked of each kind of piece, and whether it is found out (F) or decided (D); one disturbance; what is then out of line and how one would know; what noise is there; and what did not fit.*

## 1. A mathematical theory

- **Pieces.** Claim, hypothesis, proof step, explanation; a result gathers them; an outline gathers results.
- **Asked.** Of a claim: does it follow (F, held against logic under what it assumes). Of an explanation: does it say what the claim says (F). Of a result: has it been read whole (F).
- **Disturbance.** Someone builds a case where a claim fails.
- **Out of line.** The claim (evidence says it fails). Everything predicated on it. After the repair: the claim is unchecked; explanations that narrate it; the whole-read.
- **How one knows.** Follow what relied on the claim: references to it, checks that read it.
- **Noise.** A claim restated in three places. Explanations that have drifted from what they explain. Old findings left beside repaired text.
- **Did not fit.** Nothing new. This is the case the model was grown on.

## 2. A formal specification

- **Pieces.** Rule (wording), decision record (alternatives weighed, grounds, what would reopen it), fixture.
- **Asked.** Of a rule: who stands behind it, on what grounds (D). Is it consistent with every other rule (F). Do its fixtures pass (F, by an instrument).
- **Disturbance.** A usage report shows one ground is false ("nobody nests deeper than two").
- **Out of line.** Not a word has changed. The decision's condition is untrue, so the decision supports nothing, so the rule has no answer to "who stands behind this".
- **How one knows.** The decision recorded what it relied on. The ground is a piece; evidence against it is on record.
- **Noise.** Rejected alternatives kept in the rule's body. "Decided, do not reopen" in a note. Fixtures for rules that no longer exist.
- **Did not fit.** "Consistent with every other rule" relies on the whole set. Add any rule and every such check has relied on something that moved. Known, and still unplaced.

## 3. An empirical result

- **Pieces.** Claim ("A beats B on X by 3 points"), run configuration, result table, interpretation.
- **Asked.** Does it reproduce (F, by a run; held against code, data and library at versions). Does the interpretation follow from the table (F). May we build on it (D, at an importance, on the condition that the effect is at least so large).
- **Disturbance.** Five more seeds give a mean of 1 point; or a library is upgraded.
- **Out of line.** The first is plain evidence, and the decision to build on it may have lost its condition. The second: every run relied on the old library, so none says anything about the present.
- **How one knows.** For the seeds, the record. For the library, only if something looks. Nothing inside the verisectorium moved.
- **Noise.** Tables for configurations nobody uses. Run logs kept as if they were results.
- **Did not fit.** The answer never becomes "true". It is a size with a spread. How well it stands has a different shape here than in example 1, and the model needs no single shape so long as each kind says its own. The thing outside that moves, with nobody watching, is the open question about time.

## 4. An essay or paper built from sources

- **Pieces.** Quotation (a span of a source), argument step, section; the paper orders the sections.
- **Asked.** Of a quotation: does it match its source at that place (F, by an instrument). Of a step: is it faithful to what it quotes, and does it follow from the steps before it (F, by a read). Of the paper: does it read in order to someone who does not know it (F, by a cold read). Is the argument the one the author means to make (D).
- **Disturbance.** The author moves section 4 ahead of section 2 for flow.
- **Out of line.** No piece changed. The paper's order changed, so the cold read relied on something that moved. And a step in the old section 4 uses a term the old section 2 introduced.
- **How one knows.** The first, from the record. The second, only by reading it cold in order, because nobody writes a reference for "introduced before used".
- **Noise.** Quotations gathered and never used. The same point made in two sections. Sentences that defend a claim the paper no longer makes.
- **Did not fit.** Here the work gets better by getting shorter. Note 27 says later is better when it "says more and stands as well". For this kind the improvement is the same truth in fewer words, and that sentence has no way to say so.

## 5. Intake, with this repository as the case

- **Pieces.** A source, cut into chunks.
- **Asked.** Of a chunk: where did it go (D). Of a source: has it been read, by whom, how far cut (F, from the record).
- **Disturbance.** A note arrives. `msc/notes/` has twenty-seven of them.
- **Out of line.** Nothing. No piece in canon relied on it and nothing was asked of canon. A verisectorium with a thousand unplaced chunks has an empty list of what is out of line.
- **How one knows that anything is wrong.** One does not, from consistency. One knows from a different reading of the same record: what was brought in and never placed.
- **Noise.** This is the kind where it is plainest. Each new lead here has re-read the pile and written another whole-model note on top of it, this session included. Joseph, 11-08, called it the march of notes.
- **Did not fit.** The October sprint's third failure (no source ever retired) cannot be seen by "nothing is out of line". It needs the other view, and that view is where "raise the truth-to-noise ratio" does work that "be consistent" does not.

## 6. The world of a novel

- **Pieces.** Fact about the world, scene.
- **Asked.** Of a fact: does the author intend it (D). Is it consistent with the other facts (F). Of a scene: is it faithful to the facts it uses (F).
- **Disturbance.** The author decides a character was born ten years earlier.
- **Out of line.** Every fact and scene that relied on the old date.
- **How one knows.** From what relied on it, where that was written down.
- **Noise.** Facts no scene uses. Two versions of one fact.
- **Did not fit.** Nothing. Worth noticing: nothing here is held against anything outside. The floor is the author's decisions, and everything found out is found out relative to them. The two kinds of answer are enough even where "truth" means only agreement with what was decided.

## What held in all six

- **Every question sorted into found out or decided**, and the floor differed: logic in 1, the world in 3, a source in 4, decisions in 2 and 6.
- **In every disturbance, what went out of line was found by one question: what relied on this?** A reference relies on its target. Evidence relies on what it read. A decision relies on its conditions. A whole-read relies on the order and membership of what it read. The disturbances were of seven sorts (a rewrite, a failing check, a ground shown false, something outside moving, a decision changed, a reordering, an arrival), and the same question served for six of them.
- **What differed was only what each kind of piece has to have answered**, and what shape "how well" takes: a level reached (1), who stands behind it (2), a size with a spread (3), match or no match (4), and so on.

So, as an observation from six invented cases: the part that is the same for every kind looks like two things and not three. *Nothing relied on has moved unseen*, and *nothing this kind must have answered is unanswered*. "Grounded", the third idea in note 27, is the first of these followed along what a piece rests on, plus a remark about prose.

## What did not fit

1. **Unplaced influx is invisible to consistency** (5). It is visible to the same record read another way.
2. **Reliance nobody writes down** (4: used before introduced; and the theory case's unwritten reference). Found only by a cold read in order.
3. **Something outside moving with nobody watching** (3).
4. **A check that relied on a whole set** (2, 6).
5. **Better as shorter** (4). "Says more" is one kind's sense of improvement.

Items 1, 2 and 5 are the same gap from three sides: the model as written has a numerator and no denominator.

## The paragraph, after the examples

A verisectorium is a body of work in which many minds, each arriving cold, raise the proportion of what is true and built-upon to what a reader has to get through. Material brought in is cut into pieces small enough to be right or wrong on their own, and each piece is of a kind that says what must be asked of it: of a theorem, that it follows; of a design rule, who stands behind it and on what grounds; of a quotation, that it matches its source; of a measurement, that it reproduces. Every answer is either found out, and stands by evidence, or decided, and stands by whoever is behind it. The verisectorium holds only two things as fact: what was written, and what was done, and with each thing done, what it relied on. From that record it computes, and never types, the one thing that is the same for every kind of work: where something relied on has since moved, and where something that must be asked has no answer. That list is the work. It is done off to the side, where an attempt can be abandoned without losing what it found out, and it rejoins the whole as one step when the list is empty. So what a reader builds on is always a state in which nothing is known to be out of line, with whatever was knowingly left open shown beside it. And the same record shows what was brought in and never placed, and what is kept that nothing relies on, so that leaving something out can be as deliberate as putting it in.

**What changed from the earlier paragraph, and why.** The purpose is stated as a ratio (examples 4 and 5). "What it relied on" is in the record by name, because one question did nearly all the work in every case. What must be asked is said to belong to the kind, with four kinds named, so that "how well anything stands" no longer has to pretend to one shape. The last sentence is new and is the least tested: only the first half of it (what was never placed) is something the model as written can compute.
