*For the lead session that asked, and for Joseph. Written 2026-11-10 by a cold agent (Fable 5.2, harness key `0a83fb87…/a3db6e09…`) that had read nothing of this repository before the brief. Nothing under `core/` or in asf was changed.*

The segment is `02-tst-core/src/der-change-expectation-baseline.md` (Volume 2, TST): `type: derived`, `status: exact`, three derivations in it, three segments that `depends:` on it and five more that use it in prose, and 39 recorded acts, among them an unanswered narrowing of one of its derivations. Three files beside this one:

- `ontology.md`: the kinds, dimensions (each declared in full), reference kinds, rules and seats the segment needs.
- `cut.md`: the segment as it is at asf HEAD, cut into 65 veritoids and 6 inbox notes, with what it writes set against what its pieces rely on.
- `record.md`: the 39 acts under the model; what binds at HEAD; warrants; what is accepted and owed under three acceptance rules; what a steady state would state.

## The result in brief

1. **Every piece of the file found a place.** It took three things core does not have and an ontology may add (a premise kind with an `assumes` reference; a kind for a reported number; a guard that is a decision) and one it has and under-describes (a choice).
2. **The frontier could not be computed until I chose what "accepted" means for asf, and asf does not say.** Read as asf declares itself, nothing is accepted and the frontier is empty. Read by the rule core's own examples use, nine of 65 pieces are in canon, and what owes is fixed by which sentences an independent check happened to concern during one half-hour. Read as asf's prose uses the word "canon", everything is, and the frontier comes out close to what the tool already shows. The three give 0, about 6 and about 17 owed pieces for one record. (§1, §2)
3. **Of the bad news in this record, none is a contradiction in core's sense.** Every non-confirming check here is, or was amended to, *narrowed*, which core files as a question about splitting that the author may clear, and which by core's wording lapses on a rewrite. The practice treats each as a finding that must be answered by another mind. (§3)
4. **Four of the twelve acts about acts fit core's three.** The other eight are a checker amending its own outcome, and seven repairs to the address of the state a check was made against. (§8)
5. **What the model shows that the tool and the file do not:** the claim in the title, $\hat n_{\text{future}} = n_{\text{past}}$, which every dependent uses, has never been the subject of any check; all sixteen concern the lifetime mathematics behind it, its paraphrases or its citations. And five checks the tool lapsed are, read at the grain of what they read, still binding. (below, "What it showed")
6. **It fits, with one joint that does not bear weight yet.** The two atoms, the written/done/computed line, reads against concerns, means, answering and carrying all did real work on real material. Canon did not.

## Where the model strained

Ordered by how much turns on each. "Core says" is my reading of the files named; where I had to guess, `ontology.md` marks the guess.

### 1. Nothing in asf is accepted, and only canon owes
asf has a `stage:` ladder whose gates would be acceptance decisions. `FORMAT.md` calls it "currently ignored in practice"; this segment is `draft`; of the 34 rows of its volume's outline, 28 are draft, 5 missing and 1 deps-verified; and the ladder's top rung is "ready for external challenge". No rung means accepted, and the record holds no decision of any sort about this segment. So by «segment» ("a segment never admitted … is provisional") and «frontier» ("only canon owes"), a ten-day-old unanswered narrowing of a derivation, restated in the segment's first paragraph and in a second segment, with three declared dependents, is not owed by anyone. The model's answer is in «steady·state»: it appears among the stated conditions, as something recorded against a provisional piece. That is true and it is a list. Cause, who can clear it, scope, and a changeset that has or has not closed are what the frontier adds, and they apply to nothing here.

asf's own word "canon" (in its reviews, its Working Notes and `FORMAT.md`) means the segment set on `main`. That is core's *filled slot in the canonical projection*, which core deliberately separates from accepted. asf also has a word for core's provisional: `stage: exploratory`, "deliberately held under discussion". A `draft` segment is neither: it is asserted by the work, built on by other segments, and vouched for by no party. Core has two states where asf has three.

`core/` itself is in the same position: every row provisional, nothing accepted, so by its own «frontier» nothing in it is owed; yet its praxis sends for cold reads and treats what they find as things to fix, which is what owing is.

Lean, for what a cold mind's is worth: owing follows being asserted by the work (a filled, unmarked slot the canonical projection reaches), provisional stays the explicit low-stakes mark, and acceptance becomes a decided dimension whose absence is a stated condition and not the gate of owing. The question under it is Joseph's: is an asf segment at `stage: draft` provisional in his sense?

### 2. Under an evidence rule, what owes is an accident, and rests on an identity nobody recorded
`record.md` §5, rule E. Core's invariant says it plainly ("of two pieces in the same recorded condition, one that was once accepted owes and one that never was does not"); on real material it means the frontier holds the last paragraph of the hazard subsection and not the summary sentence that says the same thing, and holds two Lineage sentences whose text never changed because the check that accepted them was one act with a third that did.

It also means that whether a piece owes depends on whether a rewrite kept its id. Two integrators replaced this segment's main claim twice in under four hours, moving the earlier claim to a subsection and back. Core says a rewrite keeps the id and a split or merge mints new ones, and has no word for *replaced*, nor for who says which it was. Under E, recording a rewrite as "retired, and a new piece minted" sheds everything owed on the old one. That is a fourth cheap exit beside the three «frontier» lists.

### 3. Narrowed
Four checks here did not confirm. Three came back *narrowed*; the fourth came back *broken* and its own author changed it to narrowed four minutes later. None found a textual part right and another part wrong. They found: a paraphrase wider than what it paraphrases (check 3); a sentence true on one reading and false on another (9); a hypothesis the result does not need and cannot have (10); a hypothesis the result needs and does not state (15). Each wants a rewrite, not a split.

Core says: a check's outcome is confirmed, narrowed or contradicted («act»); "narrowed is a split signal" («dimension»); on the frontier a narrowed check is *raised*, "does it want splitting?", cleared by "the dimension's producer, or the author" («frontier»); and what outlasts an edit is "a contradicting check or a finding" («act»), which a narrowing is not. So the cheap exit core closed for contradictions is open for the only kind of bad news this record has: reword the piece and the narrowing lapses.

The practice is otherwise, and consistent: vsect opens a finding for every broken or narrowed check, and it stays open across rewrites until a mind other than the one that found it records it answered. (Core excludes the repairer and vsect the finder; in the three answered here the answerer was neither.) `impl-a-small-move` itself records "false as stated: it needs a further hypothesis H" as a finding that unsettles at once. The same event arriving as a check's outcome only raises a question.

### 4. Hypotheses are shared pieces, and predicated-on is the wrong reference to them
Core's example claim holds "a statement with its hypotheses and conclusion". Here the hypotheses are blocks of their own ("**Observation.**", "**Assumption.**", "conditional on a constant birth rate"), each used by several claims, and the open defect is exactly that one claim stopped saying which of them it uses. So they are veritoids, and a claim points at them.

With what? Predicated-on caps validity, and a conditional claim is not less valid for a doubtful hypothesis. «dimension» has the right slot, almost in passing: where *against* varies per piece, "the dimension says how a piece names it, usually by a reference of a declared kind". So I declared `assumes` as how a claim fills validity's *against*, capping only holds-of. Three things then went unsaid by core: whether a claim *rests on* what fills its *against* (the definition of rests-on names only kinds "some dimension passes along" and bases of decisions); what accepting a premise means, since a premise is stated and not asserted; and that `reference.md`'s own example (c) uses predicated-on for a definition's commitment, which is this case.

The two regimes of «dimension»'s discussion ("they join at the hypothesis") are right and are what this segment is: asf's own item calls it "exact as a computation and empirical as a prediction about software". The model says so and gives no shape for the join. `assumes` plus a second determinant, holds-of, was enough.

### 5. Inheritance is fixed at the start; here contamination came by reading
«actor» makes independence a matter of what an actor *began from*. Every TST checker's record lists the integrator's account and the integrator's own review among its sources, and checker 4's write-up says: "I read the round-4 account and the integrator's record first, then re-derived". By the letter that actor inherited nothing about the piece (I have not seen its brief); by the argument «actor» gives, it carried the author's reading before it fixed a criterion. All nine independent checks here are by actors whose records name the author's account as a source; only for checker 4's three can I say it was read first. Either inheritance covers what was read before the criterion was fixed, or an act's reads must include accounts of the piece as well as the piece. Core's own carrying act needs the opposite of the bare brief ("needs to know which act is being carried and its criterion"), so some reading of the editor's material is unavoidable for some acts, and the record should say which.

### 6. Dependence inside one segment: inner dependency, or reference?
Core has both and they behave differently. An inner dependency is declared by the kind, slot to slot, and a write that leaves a dependent neither changed nor affirmed is *refused*. A reference is written by the author, piece to piece, and a moved target makes it *stale*, owed later. This segment has about sixty dependences between its own pieces ("the tail of item 1 above", "which is the likelihood above", every sentence of the summary). Core's examples use inner dependencies only for explanation → claim and never show a claim resting on a sibling claim. I wrote them as references. `record.md` §6 shows what turns on it: the defect was made by a premise edit in one unit that a claim in another unit silently relied on, and neither mechanism, as I could declare it, catches that without either the reference somebody did not write or thirty affirmations per premise edit.

### 7. A cap across kinds
"Validity caps along predicated-on: … the least of its own warrant and the effective warrants of everything it is predicated on." z2 is predicated on Watson's lemma, an import; imports open *source*, not validity. c3 uses terms from a definition, which opens neither. Core says a dimension not opened for a kind "is a fact", and that "no dimension's answer stands in for another's", and gives no rule for the cap at such a target. I guessed a mapping from source's rungs onto validity's. A theory is full of claims that rest on borrowed results, so this is not a corner.

### 8. Acts that have no place, or half of one
- **Amending one's own act.** Check 9's author recorded "outcome 'broken' overstates … Read the outcome as narrowed". Core's three acts about acts all "ask a mind other than the one with an interest"; this is the same mind, and it changes what is owed. «act»'s Working Notes mention such "corrections" from the first real record and leave them unplaced. It needs to be an act: *amending*, by the act's own actor, weakening only.
- **The state an act was performed against.** Seven of the 39 acts exist to say that the commit a check named is on no branch, and which commit on `main` is "the same tree for this record"; two of the seven correct the other two. Lanes worked in separate worktrees and were rebased. Core has every act record "the state it was performed against" and defines a state as a point in the acta, which presumes one line. An act already binds to the versions of what it read; for binding the state is redundant, and here it was the most fragile field in the record.
- **One act, two dimensions.** Check 2 re-derives and reproduces numbers; check 6 follows a derivation and collides it with another unit; check 12 derives and simulates. Core leans "record two acts". Two of the sixteen are also typed on the wrong line for the model (a fidelity question recorded as "derivation").
- **What a non-confirming act confirms.** Check 15's free text says the hazard mathematics holds under survival-only conditioning, with numbers. Check 9's says "the survival-conditioning derivation itself stands". One outcome per act means these count for nothing; z2 and z3 have been re-derived three times and read *never asked*.
- **Criterion after.** The record types this honestly: 31 of the TST lane's 88 checks say `committed: after`, among them all thirteen of checker 1's and the only independent reproduction of this segment's numbers. Core: such a check "adds no weight". That is a third of a lane's checking read as nothing. It may be right; it should be decided knowing the size.
- **Remarks.** Check 16 confirmed "faithful", with a remark the checker also typed as finding F8. Check 11 confirmed, with an untyped remark that drove the next rewrite. A review record and its write-up are sources, and the model's intake would cut them whole; today only the typed items are in the store.

### 9. A guard is a decision
`kind.md`: "a regression guard opens *still false* (a check)". The guard this segment keeps is "don't re-promote [the corollary] without deriving the threshold": a prohibition on a future edit, with a reason and a condition that lifts it. The others the keep-list names from this volume are "do not re-add X to `depends:`" and "tier: empirical, not exact". These guard a label or a dependency, not a refuted statement, and each has exactly the parts of core's decision record (what, grounds, what would reopen it) and no party. «decision»'s own example of a non-decision, "RESOLVED in canon, do not re-open", is what they are today.

### 10. Work owed by someone
asf's largest ledger is "items: work owed, with owners" (632 open), each naming what it concerns and a key result as owner. Three concern this segment: run the cheapest falsifier; pass old reader questions against the text; carry out the Working Notes dispositions. None is a violated constraint, so none is on core's frontier. Nearest homes: an inbox note (not owed, no owner), a promised slot (promises a segment, not a check or a pass), a decision about the work (not on the frontier), a *raised* question (raised by a rule or a narrowing, cleared by the dimension's producer). I think they are raised questions put by a party's decision and assigned to a seat, and core has seats, but nothing lets a decision raise a question or lets an owed item be assigned. The owner is a key-result id because, as `kinds.yaml` says, "agent names go stale": a seat, in asf's own practice.

The other side of the same gap: «frontier» addresses a repair to "the piece's last author first". Four actors held that seat here in six hours and the last ended ten days ago.

### 11. "The" claim, and the status word
Core's example segment kind has one formal-claim slot and aliases the segment's truth to it. This segment has three derivations of one conclusion under three premise sets, each with its own tag and conditions (exact at every age; conditional on a constant birth rate; asymptotic), and a fourth tag on the conclusion itself: "Conditional on uniform feature rate". Its frontmatter says `status: exact`. asf's own label audit names "labeling a conditional result as exact without flagging the condition" as a common error. To alias anything I had to pick the claim, and picked the one the title and the dependents use (c3), under which the honest printed word is *conditional*. The hazard unit wants to be a segment of its own; the day's churn says so too.

### 12. Smaller

Core offers three homes with three different consequences: content saying how well a piece stands (then overclaimed, since no counting run is on record); testimony brought in as an observation piece; a claim of its own on a reproduces dimension with a script as its *against* (then a missing reference). I took the third. Theory segments report their own numerics as a matter of course.

- **Findings have no size.** F8 ("two small precisions") blocks acceptance exactly as the narrowed derivation does.
- **Better's first clause.** v5 had two sentences shown false and one hypothesis shown superfluous; v6 has one claim shown to lack a hypothesis, in three places. ## What it showed that the tool and the file do not

- **c3 has never been checked.** The tool shows this segment with ten re-derivations. Cut, they concern the lifetime posterior, the birth-rate reading and the hazard tail. The step to feature count, tagged conditional and carrying the result's name, is in no criterion. So are the summary's unsupported half-sentence (h3) and the mean-undefined claim that three dependents repeat.
- **Five checks bind that the tool lapsed** (`record.md` §3). The cause is the tool's rule that anything upstream through `depends:` lapses a check; the checks read nothing upstream. The same rule makes one changeset of a whole sprint. Per-veritoid binding saved nothing older than the last rewrite here, which is less than the first real root hoped for.
- **Means.** The record already carries them in each criterion and `recheck:` line. Laid out, the hazard unit after v3 had been followed and never collided until the check that narrowed it. This is the stopped-bound lesson found a second time, in a record that was writing its criteria down.
- **Decisions, by their absence.** A modelling choice, a guard and six routings are in this segment's file as prose with no party; the model names each as a decision nobody made.
- **Intake.** Checker 4's write-up contains a stronger statement that unifies the two routes (under the uniform-age likelihood the baseline needs Jeffreys' prior on the hazard) with replacement text. It is in asf's `msc/`, pointed at by a review's `sources:` and cut by nobody. Core's source, cut whole, with a routing per chunk, is precisely what would keep it.
- **Answering and carrying matched the practice exactly**: three answers, all by a mind that wrote no repair; one carry, by a mind other than the editor. They were written from this same sprint's record in another volume, so this is agreement and not independent confirmation.

## The cost

For one 115-line file: 65 veritoids; about 60 references among them, about 25 outward, of which the file writes 1 declared and some 20 in prose; 16 checks whose criteria concern from 1 to 8 pieces each and read more. The record's own criteria already say which sentences they are about, in quotation. What structured recording would add to an act is ids for what it now quotes; what it would add to a write is forty-odd references nobody writes today, most of them from a summary to what it summarizes. I did the mapping by hand and it was most of this trial.

## Does it fit?

The file, yes. The record's acts, mostly: the gaps in §3 and §8 are places where the practice has already settled something and core has not caught up. The frontier and the steady state, not yet: on the estate's main theory, as it is today, they are either empty or decided by a rule that has to be invented, and the invented rule that core's examples point to gives a frontier I would not show anyone as what asf owes. I would settle §1 before running a third root.

## On the model as a whole

The two atoms held without strain, and their best showing was negative: every act in this record is a finding-out, and the model made the missing decisions visible as missing. The written/done/computed line held too; what asf types that the model would compute is a short list (`status:`, the role on each act, the author's *redefined* or *reworded*).

The vocabulary did not need to grow much. I used about fifty-five of core's words. I never needed suggested, promised or gap (asf's outline has them; this segment does not), nor lineage. I was short of: a word for a hypothesis and its reference; a state between provisional and accepted; an amending act; a place for narrowed.

The size is real. Holding enough of core to do this meant reading all of it, about 1,600 lines; what I went back to while working was five things: «dimension»'s six-part declaration, «act», the frontier table, the three paragraphs of «segment» on wanted against accepted, and «reference». The worked runs taught me more per line than the defs.

One caution about my own result. `impl-scope-adaptive-system` is in core's reading order, so I met a sketch ontology for an asf segment before I made mine, and mine resembles it. The departures (premise and `assumes`, holds-of, the reported number, guard as decision, three acceptance rules) came from this segment; the resemblance did not get an independent test.
