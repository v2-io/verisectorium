# Audit of KERNEL-SPEC draft 1 against `core/`

*2026-11-10. By the Ada/SPARK agent (Fable 5.2), before any code. For the lead, and for Joseph later. Nothing under `core/` and nothing in the spec was changed. Nothing here has been run: every "counterexample" below is a trace I derived by reading, and is marked **predicted**. The build is where they get confirmed or die.*

## What I read

Whole, myself: `KERNEL-SPEC.md`; the root `README.md`; `core/README.md`; `core/model.outline.md`; `core/def/README.md` and all twelve defs with their Working Notes; `src/intro-spine.md`; the five invented moves (`impl-a-small-move`, `impl-an-outline-fills-in`, `impl-a-finding-is-answered`, `impl-two-moves-at-once`, `impl-intake`); `disc-written-done-computed`, `disc-better`, `disc-what-would-show-this-wrong`; `instance-trial-1/05-findings.md`; `instance-trial-2/README.md`; the last dozen entries of `core/log.kdl`; `kernel-spikes/README.md` (toolchain only). Not read: `disc-actor`, `disc-record-surfaces`, the two real-root runs (`impl-scope-adaptive-system`, `impl-stopped-bound`), the four de novo reads, the trials' ontology, state and record files, the earlier Ada spikes' code, anything in `ocaml/`.

## The short version

The spec is faithful to the defs sentence by sentence; I found only a handful of places where it says something a def does not. Its trouble is of another sort, and there are three parts to it.

1. **The fold in §5 does not use two of its own parameters.** `rests_on` and `review_reads` are declared in §2 and never appear in a §5 definition. So unsettledness does not spread, and a whole read of an assembly can never lapse. Most of what the invented moves show happening cannot happen in the kernel as written.
2. **Several things the §6 exit column and the K-properties speak of have no entry in §4 that does them**: withdrawing, sustaining, retiring with descent, raising a question, moving a held piece. And three things they depend on have no definition: what an item *is* (its identity across states), what its *cause* is, and which entries *belong to* a changeset.
3. **About half of K1–K15 are true by unfolding a definition** (K3's first sentence, K7's first, K14, K1, K2) or are true of a predicate that is not the one that matters (K5 is about `open`, and an item can leave the frontier while its bad news stays `open`). They would prove and say nothing. I give the non-trivial statement I think was meant for each.

Behind those, the reading turned up places where I think **core itself** says two things or has not chosen, and where the choice decides whether a K-property holds. The five that matter most: whether a reopened decision stands again by itself when its basis is restored (M1); what becomes of canon membership across a recut (M2); whether an answer is a check that lapses (M3); that withdrawal is an exit for every item and under the placement policy a *write* can do it (M4); and that a canonical outline is drawn into every scope through its stale pointing references and its whole read, which is trial 1's collapse arriving by a different road (M5).

I would rather you revise with these in hand than have me build on guesses. Where I propose a repair it is a guess at what you meant, for you to take or replace.

## A. What the kernel cannot run without

**A1. `rests_on` is never used; `settled` does not propagate.** §5: "`settled(p, n)`: it stands." `def/segment.md`, Invariants: "An accepted segment that rests on a provisional or unsettled one is unsettled, unless all it relies on is that the suggestion exists." `def/frontier.md`: "Unsettledness spreads only along what rests on what." As written, the finding on c at 08:30 in the small move reopens nothing on u. Needed: `settled` as a recursive predicate over `rests_on`, the "relies only on existence" exemption as a flag on the resting edge, and a word on cycles. `def/reference.md` allows cycles in kinds nothing caps along, and a kind can be passed along (narrates stales) without capping, so `rests_on` may be cyclic. Least fixed point of "unsettled" is well defined there; I would take it and say so. It has a consequence at M1.

**A2. `review_reads` is never used; a read of an assembly cannot lapse on a filler.** §3 Read is "an id at a version", and an assembly's version by §3 "does not cover the versions of what fills them". So G's whole read of T never lapses when c changes, against every move ("T's whole read has lapsed"). `def/segment.md`: what depends on an assembly's content "reads what the kind or the reference kind says it reads of what the assembly holds, and lapses, reopens or goes stale on that." Needed: a second read form, *an assembly as reviewed* (its version together with the `review_reads(k)` projection of each filler), and the same for a reference to an assembly, whose default per `def/reference.md` is "anything the assembly holds". That second default matters at M5.

**A3. No entry withdraws or sustains.** §5 `in_canon` says "has not been withdrawn"; §6 lists "its party sustaining, deciding again, or withdrawing" and "withdrawal of the promise". §4's decision row has accept, waive, affirm, route, promise, other. Needed: a decision that concerns an earlier decision (withdraw), and a statement that sustaining is a fresh decision of the same sort on the same subject with new reads, the latest unwithdrawn one being current. Also who counts as "its party" once seats are out of scope (§1): I would add `same_party(a, b)` beside `standing`.

**A4. No entry retires an id or records descent, or moves a held piece.** K5 and `open` speak of descendants; §7 speaks of "moving"; a Write is only "a whole new written state". `def/changeset.md`: a segment changeset "may … recut (split, merge); move held segments in or out (promote, fold back); and mint new segments." Needed in a Write: the ids it retires with the ids each descends to; ids promoted out to freestanding; freestanding ids folded in. And one thing core does not say: whether a write may drop a held id with **no** descendant (deletion). See M2.

**A5. Nothing raises a question.** §6 "raised: a question put to a piece has no answer", and no entry in §4 puts one. `def/frontier.md` gives four origins: a rule; a narrowed premise; a decision about the work that assigns it to a seat; and (`def/act.md`) a finding relayed from an unattested source. Needed: a relayed flag on a finding (it raises, and does not unsettle); a decision "raise/assign"; and the computed case, for which `def/reference.md` gives the rule: "A narrowed premise puts a question to every dependent … the dependent's author answers it by changing or affirming." That last also means §6's exit for raised ("an act by the dimension's producer or the assigned seat") omits a **write** by the dependent's author and an **affirmation**, which is how the small move clears it at 13:00.

**A6. Items have no identity, causes no definition, changesets no membership.** K6 compares the frontier at n and n+1; a waiver "covers an item"; K12 speaks of "entries belonging to one" changeset and `steady` of an item's cause. None is defined. Three things are needed.
- *Item identity*: I would take (item kind, piece, and the act, reference, slot or decision it is about).
- *Cause*: `steady(R, n)` says "no unwaived item's piece or cause lies in the set of segments R", and a cause is an entry, not a segment. More seriously, cause cannot be "the entry at which the item appeared". In `impl-two-moves-at-once`, second run, Monday 11:00, the reference from x to unsettled c is written by M2's mind, and core says "x has an item whose cause is in M1". So cause is *why* the item holds, traced through what it depends on, back to root disturbances: provenance, not arrival time. An item may then have several causes.
- *Membership*: which open changeset an entry falls in. `def/act.md` has `belongs-to changeset {1,1}`, and nothing says how it is determined for an entry that causes no item (a check inside an open scope).  
Without these K12 and K13 cannot be stated. I can propose definitions and say which K-results depend on them, but they would be mine.

**A7. Slots have no identity, and the promise lives in two places.** A promise decision "concerns a slot"; a Read may be "which ids fill one slot"; `unfilled` is about a slot. §3's Slot is an anonymous sum with promised and filled exclusive. `def/segment.md`: "The promise is a «decision» and outlives the filling: it is kept when what fills the slot is accepted", and its Discussion lists "promised, filled and unaccepted (work in progress)". With the sum type, filling the slot with an unaccepted draft erases "promised" and the item goes, against `impl-an-outline-fills-in` step 2 ("The promise is still owed"). Needed: slot identity (I would use the kind's slot name plus a position among its fillers), slots that take several fillers (§3's Read already says "ids"), and a decision about which of the written state and the recorded promise the `unfilled` item is computed from. See M6.

**A8. Parameters §5–§7 use that §2 does not list.** What caps along which reference kind, and which dimension of the target is read when its kind does not open this one (`def/dimension.md`, passes along); the kind declarations (slots, required or not, inner dependencies), without which §7's first refusal and `unfilled` cannot be computed; whether a decided dimension allows carrying, and any cap on carries; the order on warrants ("falls", "least", "below what is needed" all need one, and "least" needs meets if it is partial); the canonical projection, which the placement policy needs.

**A9. Content the kernel must see.** `overclaimed` and `unbacked` are about what content *says*. With opaque content they cannot be computed. Needed: a veritoid's written state carries, beside its opaque content, a list of standing claims (piece, dimension, level) and of reported decisions. They are part of what is hashed.

**A10. Acts can be ill-formed, and core has refusals only for writes.** An act reading a version that never existed; answering a confirmation; contesting a later act; amending another actor's act; concerns outside its reads. The outline carries "refusals" as a gap row. The kernel has to do something. I would refuse them as ill-typed, keep that list separate from §7, and report it as a demand core has not met rather than a fact about the model. One case I would *allow*, following `def/act.md`'s Working Note that an act "binds by the versions of what it read and records no state": an act that read a version no longer current.

## B. Where the spec says what a def does not

**B1. `open`: who may answer.** Spec: answered "by an actor that does not share an inheritance with the author of any write to the piece since b". `def/act.md`: "Asked by someone other than the author of the repair." Two differences. The strengthening from "other than" to "does not share" follows `def/actor.md` and I think it right. But "any write since b" is empty when nobody has written, and then *anyone* may answer, including the piece's author. `impl-a-finding-is-answered` step 4 says the opposite: "A cannot clear it: there is no repair to answer, and A is the author." **Predicted trace** (3 entries): A writes p; D records a finding b against p; A records answering(b, answered). By §5, b is closed. Repair offered: b is answerable only at a version of the piece other than any version b read, by an actor not sharing with the author of any write that took the piece from what b read to what the answer reads. That also handles a finding recorded against a version already superseded.

**B2. "As a check" for the three acts about acts contradicts §5.** §4's table says answering, carrying and contesting count "as a check", which is to say while every read is unchanged. §5's `open` is an existential over the record ("has no answering act …"), which never lapses. These are different models; see M3 for answering. For carrying the literal reading regresses: a carrying act reads the piece at two versions, the piece moves to a third, the carry's read has "changed", so it lapses unless it too is carried, and so on. I think a carry must be a judgment about two *versions*, which are immutable ("One version names one written state for the life of the verisectorium"), so that it never lapses and can only be contested. Then `binds` is: there is a chain of uncontested carries, each for this act and this read, from the version read to the current one. That answers §9 question 5 by construction (carries compose, by chaining; a cap is a policy number on chain length), and K3 does not need the cap. For contesting: `def/act.md` has a contested act "stops counting", and a contest is itself a check that may be contested, so whether an act counts is a recursion on later acts, not "no contesting act that overturned it". It is well founded because an act concerns only earlier acts.

**B3. `steady` omits a clause.** `def/steady-state.md`: outside the open scopes "nothing there is owed, and nothing there rests on anything that is." The spec has only the first half.

**B4. `stands` hard-codes what core gives to `read_as`.** "No open bad news against a ground" is in `stands`. In `def/dimension.md` bad news lowers a warrant through the dimension's *read as* ("a narrowing or contradiction winning until answered"), and the decision reopens because the warrant it read fell. The spec's clause reopens a decision on a finding even where the ontology's reading would not, which is trial 1's finding 4 ("I invented *disputed*") decided silently.

**B5. `warrant` does not apply `standing` or the criterion.** `def/act.md`: acts by an actor without standing "are recorded and warrant nothing on that dimension"; a check whose criterion came after "adds no weight". Neither filter is in §5. And whether bad news from an actor without standing is `open` is unsaid in the spec (core: a relayed finding only raises).

**B6. `shares` is binary; core's is per piece and per act.** `def/actor.md`: "Two actors are one line of evidence to the extent of what they both inherited" about the piece, and what is read before the criterion is fixed "counts, for that act, as inherited". A binary relation is a fair first cut. What the spec must say is whether it is an equivalence, because it decides K3 and K4; see C3.

**B7. `in_canon` is written for one of the three `accepts` policies.** "An accepting decision for p exists and has not been withdrawn" is the party policy. Under a rule, `def/decision.md`: "Applying a rule is computed" and such a decision "is withdrawn only as the ontology says, never merely by failing", so `in_canon` is "the rule held at some earlier prefix, and no withdrawal since": a latch over the whole log, with no entry marking when it latched. Under placement, the acceptance is a write, and what withdraws it (a write removing the slot? marking it?) and what its basis is are unsaid in core. §9 question 2 cannot be run until each of the three says: what admits, what the basis is, what withdraws.

**B8. Smaller.** A Reference in §3 has one target; `def/reference.md` has several and an expected count ("Zero against exactly-one is a defect", for which no item is listed). §3's Read drops "only its assertion on a dimension". `unbacked` in §6 is "a decision not on record"; `def/dimension.md` says "on record with its basis holding". `unmet` does not say whether the target's own warrant or its effective one is compared; `def/frontier.md` treats own-above-effective as information, which suggests own, and I doubt that is wanted.

## C. Properties that prove and say nothing, or cannot be what was meant

**C1. Definitional.** K3's first sentence restates `binds`. K7's first sentence restates `stands`. K14 restates "retired". K1 and K2 hold of any pure function over an append-only log of two disjoint entry types. They are worth stating as what the types guarantee. Non-trivial versions: for K1, that an *incremental* state updated entry by entry equals the fold from scratch (the only form in which replay can fail, and the form an engine will have); for K2, that no decision moves a found-out warrant and no act of finding out makes, withdraws or sustains a decision (the two atoms, kept apart: `def/decision.md` "It warrants nothing about whether what it concerns is true", `def/dimension.md` "no decided answer stands in for a found-out one"). The spec has no property for the two atoms at all, and I would add this one.

**C2. K5 is about the wrong predicate.** "b is open against p or its descendants" survives writes by the definition of `open`, in which writes do not occur. What matters is whether the *item* survives, and §6's item needs the piece in canon. So K5 can hold while the item is gone (M2, M4). The property meant: the item (bad news b, against p or each descendant) is on the frontier at every later state reached by writes alone.

**C3. K3's second sentence and K4 need the same condition, and "monotone" is not enough.** K3 is marked (all), but "appending a confirming act lapses nothing" depends on `read_as`, exactly as K4 does. And two things break both under a monotone `read_as`:
- *Line counting.* If `shares` is not transitive and lines are counted as connected groups, a new confirmation by an actor who shares with two previously independent checkers joins their lines. **Predicted trace**: x and y confirm p, not sharing: two lines. z, who shares with each, confirms p: one line. The warrant falls, a decision that read it reopens. Good news undid a review. It holds if `shares` is an equivalence, or if lines are counted as the largest set of pairwise non-sharing acts.
- *Acceptance by rule.* **Predicted trace**, using the small move's own rule ("a claim is accepted while a mind that inherited nothing from its author has checked it…"): a provisional claim q is predicated on an accepted claim c, which has been rewritten and accepted again since q was written, so q's reference is stale (and not owed: provisional pieces owe nothing). B, who inherited nothing, checks q and confirms it. Every clause of the rule now holds of q (checked, nothing unanswered, resting only on accepted pieces), so the rule admits it. q is in canon, and its stale reference is owed. A confirmed check added a frontier item. Any latent item that is not itself a clause of the rule does the same (an overclaim in q's content; an unmet need). This is against `def/steady-state.md`'s invariant ("A confirming check on a steady state reaches a new steady state at once, since nothing becomes owed") and not only against the spec.

**C4. K6 cannot hold as stated wherever an item depends on another item.** An acceptance reopened because what it rests on is unsettled leaves when the *upstream* piece is settled, by whatever cleared the upstream item: an answering act, say, which is not in the reopened row's column. So the exit column has to be closed under dependence ("or an exit of an item this one holds because of"), or K6 is false at the first propagation. Further exits missing from the column that follow from the spec's own §5, each of which I expect a search to return: for **stale**, a write that changes the referencing piece (the small move, 13:00: "its reference pointing at c as before, now seen at c's new version"), a write that drops the reference, and the target being restored to the version seen (K9); for **reopened**, any entry that restores the basis (M1); for **every** row, withdrawal of the piece's acceptance (M4). Rather than patch the column by hand a fourth time, I would have the build *derive* it: enumerate, at small sizes, every (item kind, entry kind) pair at which an item leaves, under each policy, and hand you that table to set beside the one you intend. The differences are the cheap exits.

**C5. K11 holds only if the accepting decision read no more than `review_reads` says.** Under the party policy a decision's basis is whatever the act recorded as read. K11 then needs it as a well-formedness rule that a read of an assembly *is* the `review_reads` projection (A2), not a property of the fold.

**C6. K12's first half is true by definition or false, depending on A6.** If an item's cause is M1's entry, its segment is in M1's scope, so "entries belonging to one neither add … an item of the other" cannot be violated without the scopes having met. The content I think is meant is non-interference: for a region R closed under what its pieces read, reference and rest on, the frontier restricted to R is a function of the entries that touch R. That is provable, it says what "local" means, and its hypothesis (closure) is exactly where the policy enters. K13 under merging-by-definition is likewise empty; its content is the converse, that when the closures do not meet, each closes alone.

**C7. §9 question 4 is outside what a kernel with opaque content can say.** Two accepted veritoids whose contents are X and not-X, with no reference between them, owe nothing: two writes and two acceptances. "Contradict in substance" needs a relation on content the kernel does not have. I can exhibit the witness given such an oracle, and it will be that trivial.

## M. Where I think core has not chosen, or says two things

These are about the model. Each is stated against the def clause, with what turns on it.

**M1. Does a reopened decision stand again by itself when its basis is restored?** `def/decision.md`: "A reopened decision backs nothing: what it backed is owed on the «frontier», addressed to its party, who sustains it on the new basis, decides again, or withdraws it." That reads as a latch: once reopened, only the party. `def/veritoid.md`: "restoring earlier content restores the earlier version and every act that read it", and a decision is an act. And for a rule, `def/decision.md`: "reopened whenever the rule would not now make it", which is no latch (the small move, 11:00: "the rule accepts c again"). The spec takes no latch in §5 (`stands` is a function of the current state) and a latch in §6 (the exit column). What turns on it: without a latch, a reopened item leaves by any write that reverts, any check that restores a warrant, any answer to bad news against a ground, and any upstream acceptance, none of them the party's doing. With a latch, a party decision and a rule decision behave differently under the same history, mutual resting among party-accepted pieces needs an order of acceptance that A1's fixed point does not give (accept A while B is provisional: reopened at birth, and stays so after B is accepted until A's party acts again), and K9's "what bound to it" has to exclude decisions. I can build both and report K6, K7 and K9 under each.

**M2. Canon across a recut, and deletion.** `def/veritoid.md`: "What stood against the retired piece unanswered stands against its descendants; replacing a piece wholesale is a recut like any other and sheds nothing." `def/segment.md`: "Acceptance is per segment at every depth", and a piece is in canon "once a decision by those with standing admits it". Descendants have new ids and no accepting decision, so they are provisional, and "Nothing inside a provisional segment is owed". **Predicted trace**: p accepted; finding b against p; a write splits p into p1 and p2 with recorded descent. b is `open` against p1 and p2, neither is in canon, and the item is gone. This is trial 2's "fourth cheap exit" surviving the sentence written to close it, because the sentence is about what stands against a piece and the frontier asks also whether the piece is in canon. Either descendants inherit canon membership (unsettled, since the acceptance read the old version), or the item stays against the retired id. And core does not say whether a held id may be dropped with no descendant. If it may, either the dropped piece stays in canon for ever with an item nothing can answer, or deletion sheds everything.

**M3. Is an answer a check, which lapses?** `def/act.md` calls answering an act of finding out ("does the piece as it now reads still have what the finding … found?"), and a confirmation "binds while every one of its reads is unchanged". Taken so, an answer lapses at the next edit of the piece and the finding is owed again until the answer is carried or given afresh: every old finding returns at every rewording. Taken as permanent (the spec's `open`), this happens. **Predicted trace**: finding b on p at v1; A writes v2; B answers; A writes v1 again. By K9 the content b was found against is back, in canon, with b closed. Another way out, and K5 does not see it because it excludes answering acts. The lapsing reading is safe against the revert and costly; the permanent reading is cheap and has the hole. A middle one: an answer holds at the version it read and at any version reached from there by carries, and never at a version b read. I can build the choice as a switch and show both.

**M4. Withdrawal clears every item on the piece, and under placement a write can do it.** "Only canon owes", so the moment a piece's acceptance is withdrawn, everything owed on it and on its references is gone: contradicted, found, overclaimed, stale, all of it. That is what the model says (a provisional piece owes nothing), and `def/steady-state.md` keeps it visible in the stated conditions, and anything resting on the piece becomes unsettled. But §6 lists it for two rows only, and K5 and K6 as written are false of it. More to the open question that is Joseph's: under the lead's lean in `def/verisectorium.md` ("placing a segment unmarked in the canonical projection … *is* an acceptance"), un-placing or marking is a **write**, by whoever may write the outline. Then "bad news survives writes" is false in item form for that policy as it stands: a write to the outline takes the piece out of canon and its items with it. Under the party policy it takes a decision by the party; under a rule, whatever "as the ontology says" is. I expect this to be the sharpest difference among the three `accepts` policies, and it does not depend on any proof: it follows from what admits being a write. A placement policy would need un-placing to be something other than a bare write (a decision, or a write that leaves the items standing against the piece) for K5 to hold under it.

**M5. A canonical outline is in every scope after all.** `impl-two-moves-at-once`: "An outline pointing at T, U, V and W would not have joined the two scopes: holding and pointing are not resting." Resting is not the only road. (a) `def/reference.md` makes "the pointing of an assembly's slot" a reference kind, and a reference to an assembly holds only "with nothing changed in what the reference's kind says it relies on, by default anything the assembly holds". `impl-an-outline-fills-in` step 5 has exactly this: "P's slot pointing at D is stale too." So when M1 rewrites c inside T, the outline's pointing at T is stale: an owed item, on a reference written in the outline, caused by M1. `def/changeset.md`: a scope is "every segment it has written or that has a «frontier» item it caused". The outline is in M1's scope, and by the same step in M2's. (b) `def/segment.md` says an outline's accepting read reads of each part "a part's headline and what it asserts". A repair that adds a hypothesis changes what the part asserts, so the outline's whole read lapses and its acceptance is reopened, by M1 and again by M2. Either way the two scopes share the outline, and "several may be open at once while their scopes share nothing" fails. **Predicted**, and the first thing I would run. It is trial 1's finding 12 with resting removed and the same result. The condition under which K12 would hold is then about `review_reads` and the pointing kind's reads, not about `rests_on`: a hub's reads must be untouched by the writes in both changesets. For a substantive repair and an outline that reads what its parts assert, they are not. Either a pointing slot relies only on its target's identity and an outline's read only on headlines that repairs seldom touch, or sharing a hub is not meeting, and core would have to say which items on a shared segment join scopes.

**M6. The promise has two homes.** The slot's state "promised" is written and "changed by writing that assembly"; the promise is also a decision that only its party withdraws. A write that sets the slot back to suggested, or removes it, either clears `unfilled` (a quiet exit by any author of the assembly) or leaves a standing promise about a slot whose written state denies it. Core's own item for the second case would be `unbacked` turned around: a decision on record that content no longer reports. Nothing lists that.

**M7. Reopened routing: owed or not?** `impl-intake` step 7 shows the routing of 4a "**reopened**" in the type the moves use for frontier items. `def/frontier.md`'s row for reopened is any decision whose basis failed. Its invariants say "A source not yet retired … [is] not owed", and K10 needs every item's piece in canon. The spec's "a decision canon rests on" picks a reading and does not define the phrase. Which decisions put an item on the frontier when reopened: acceptances only? promises? waivers (a reopened waiver un-covers its item; is it also an item)? affirmations? routings?

**M8. Amending as an exit.** `def/act.md`: the act's own actor "may only weaken its own act". If a finder may amend a contradiction to narrowed, the item changes kind; if a finding may be amended away entirely, that is an exit the finder holds alone, and §6 does not list it. And "weaken" points opposite ways for good news and bad. The order needs stating, with its bottom.

**M9. Small inconsistencies in core, for the record.** The outline's row for *version* says a veritoid's covers "the ids its references point at"; `def/veritoid.md` says "its references as written (kind, target id, what each needs)". The outline's row for *acceptance* defines its basis by "what it rests on being accepted" and the row for *rests on* includes "a decision about it has the other in its basis": circular as written, harmless as a least fixed point, worth one sentence. `def/frontier.md`'s invariant "The same recorded things read in any order give the same frontier" is false if taken of the acta's order (a write then a check differs from the check then the write); I take it to mean the order of evaluation. `def/reference.md` gives a raised question's clearer as the dependent's author, `def/frontier.md` as the dimension's producer or an assigned seat (A5).

## Is anything in the spec not kernel?

Nothing I would cut. I would layer it, so that a proof about the lower layer does not wait on a guess in the upper:

- **Layer 1**: the record; ids and versions; reads, `binds`, carrying; bad news, answering, contesting, amending; decisions, bases, `stands`; canon; references and the cap; the mechanical items (contradicted, narrowed, found, reopened, stale, dangling, unmet, unfilled). K1–K5, K7–K10, K15, and the two-atoms property.
