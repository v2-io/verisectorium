# Debrief: what four formal languages, and five older fields, see in the model

*To Joseph, from the spike agent (Fable 5.2), 2026-11-11. You asked for exemplars in Lean 4, Datalog and one of Alloy or TLA+, and said you were "even more interested in each language's perspective on the model we have"; then widened it to anything else worth finding.*

***Corrected after an independent read.** The first version of this file (in git at `4fa250e`) was read cold by another agent, whose report is `de-novo-feedback-1.md`. I read that report whole and re-ran the probes I lean on below; it was right in nearly everything, and its chief finding was that this debrief claimed more than the perspective files behind it. Items 1, 3, 5 and 6 have been rewritten and the others amended. `08-corrections-after-the-independent-read.md` lists every change, and the two small places where I do not agree with the report. The lead relayed some of the first version's claims to you before the report came back; where what you were told differs from this, this is the one to hold.*

*What was done. I read notes 22 and 23, the kernel spec (drafts 3 and 4), both builders' accounts and nearly all of `core/`, and cut the model down to the smallest thing that can run the example in note 22 §4.3. Then the same small model and the same story were written four times: as rules (Datalog, by me), as types and proofs (Lean 4), as relations (Alloy 6) and as states and steps (TLA+), the last three by forks of me working in parallel. I also read into five fields that met parts of this problem long ago.*

*What standing any of it has. The small model is much smaller than the kernel spec: no warrant levels, no descent, one root, nobody's standing, and six of note 22's nine conditions. The four renderings of it agree on the story and are not the same function; the report lists six ways they differ. The three forks began with my reading and my guesses, so where two languages agree it is one expectation surviving two tests, not two minds. And where all four "found" something in your example, that is one reading of mine, written down before anything ran, encoded four times.*

## What I would put in front of you first

**1. Nothing owed should be read from where an event sits in a line that was merged from two.** Your sentence was: "there should be causal ordering, fwiw, definitely a clock and an addative base". The kernel spec took the clock as the order. On a single line that is harmless: each write replaces the one before it, so the clock's order and the order of replacement are the same order. They come apart only where a line is assembled from two forks, and there three faults have been found.

- A rewording credited with having been written against a version its writer never saw, because in the merged line it comes later by the clock. Both builders found this and mended it the same way: the write records what it was written against.
- Two forks each rewriting the same piece, each stationary alone, the merged line stationary, the later clock silently winning. The TLA+ fork found this. (The Alloy fork's reproduction is a degenerate one, a piece rewritten to the version it already had.)
- A guard answered by the wrong fork. A defect is found in a claim. Fork A fixes it and the defect is looked for in A's version and not found. Fork B, from the same checkpoint, rewrites the claim differently and nobody looks. B cannot close alone. Merged, nothing is owed: A's pass sits between by the clock and answers for a version it was never about. This case is the independent reader's, not mine, and it is the best of the three.

All three need a fork to be allowed to close *because the merged line is stationary*. The spec's own rule does not allow that: forks that meet must be reworked, and two writes of one piece meet. The OCaml builder left the choice between the two rules as the model's to make, and note 23 §8 repeats it. So these are three holes in a rule the spec does not have, found while that rule is still being weighed. They are not evidence that the spec's order is wrong.

I rewrote the small model so that no rule compares the times of two events: each write says which write it replaces, as well as what it was written against. On that footing the story's frontier is identical at every row; the third fault shows as owed with nothing written for it; the second *can be said* (one piece, two versions, neither replacing the other) and needs a clause to say it, which I wrote; the first cannot be stated at all.

What I had not reported, and the reader did:

- Two further faults I credited to this (a defect shed by putting the bad version back and editing; a check that does not count because it was made early) come right on the clock with only the guard reworded. They were faults of wording, as note 23 §8 already suspected of one of them.
- The footing has costs. It cannot say "answered since" unless an answer names the question it answers, so I had dropped that condition. What a write says it replaces is testimony that nothing checks, and a false one can make another piece vanish from canon. And a pass that read something the line never had will answer a guard.

So: a hypothesis about merged lines, with one good case, one case that needs a clause, three costs, and nothing yet tried on anything a builder built. The first version of this item said "the clock is for the record" and called causal ordering the load-bearing half of your sentence. That was more than I had shown.

**2. Consistency can be said without a fixed point, and saying it that way makes a choice visible; it does not make it.** Both builders report that consistency as the spec defines it has more than one fixed point and that taking the greatest is a choice. Said as *fault* (a piece is at fault when it owes something itself or rests on a piece at fault), consistent is "nothing it rests on, however far down, owes anything", and two pieces resting on each other with nothing wrong are fine. Lean proved, for any number of pieces and for an abstract "owes" and "rests", that this equals the greatest fixed point when what a piece owes of its own does not depend on what is consistent. It did not prove it of the small model's own function, nor of the spec's. Core says it this way in «frontier» ("Unsettledness spreads only along what rests on what"); note 22's condition 5 says it the other way round, and transcribed literally into a logic engine that sentence has two answers.

What this does not do is settle which the model wants. "Fault needs a reason" is one default. "Consistent needs a reason" is the other, is just as sayable, and would reject the ring. See the end of item 3.

**3. Three cases that the rule "a question, promise or waiver is unconditional" is near, and they are different.**

- A question or promise that stands only while its piece is consistent has no answer at all. That is the builders' counterexample, and no tool gives it one.
- A *waiver* that stands only while its piece is consistent does have an answer under the spec's definition. Lean accepts it. What comes out is a piece with a defect, counted consistent, on a waiver that holds because the piece is consistent.
- An *acceptance* conditioned on a piece upstream of what it supports is harmless, and is what note 22 §3.4 describes. Built into the small model it behaves correctly: the named assumption is found false and the result owes a good acceptance, with not a word rewritten. **The rule does not forbid this**, and I was wrong to say it did: draft 4 gives acceptances conditions. What stopped the builders was narrower. The Ada builder took a reading (an acceptance's conditions are plain ones) to avoid a circle between warrants and consistency; the OCaml builder left it untested. The useful part is that the circle is real only when a condition leads back to where it started.

The second bullet and item 2 pull against each other, as the reader saw and I had not. Two pieces resting on each other, and a waiver that stands because its piece stands, are the same shape: something supported by a circle and by nothing else. The greatest fixed point accepts both. If a circular waiver is to be refused because "nothing should stand on a circle", the ring wants the same answer or a reason why not. I do not have one. It is a decision about the model.

**4. With "who" set aside, none of the four languages can tell your two atoms apart.** To Lean a check and a decision are both records that someone attested something. To Datalog both are stored facts, and an affirmation or a waiver reads like evidence that holds. Nothing was run for this, and the reader is right that the first half is note 22 §3.2 handed back by encodings that were given it. The half I would keep is the reading: what distinguishes the atoms is who may add one and who may undo it, and that is the part the kernel has set aside. A kernel in which every actor counts cannot test what tells them apart.

**5. Stationary is where a close lands. It is not something the verisectorium is.** Written as "the main line is always stationary", TLA+ refutes it in two states: one finding arrives. Written as "stationary with the record as it stood at its last close", it holds, and it holds by construction: only a close changes either thing, and a close is only allowed onto a stationary state. The first version of this item set two and a half million checked states beside the two as if that were a measurement. It could not have come out otherwise. What stands is the clarification, reached by having to write the state down. The verisectorium's visible state is its latest checkpoint's line and the record; its only visible steps are that something is learned and that a fork closes; "the checkpoint" names two objects, the state rested at (frozen with its record, stationary for ever) and that line read with today's record (which may not be); and a check made on a fork is on the record at once, though nothing the fork wrote is the verisectorium. One more reading from the same view, not run: the model promises nothing will ever *happen*. That stabilizing gets done is praxis.

**6. Your example, read against note 22's condition 6 and against core's rule, gives two answers.** The eli8 depends on the description. At 10:30 the eli8 is rewritten and the description is kept. Nothing says anyone looked at the new eli8 beside the description.

- Read as note 22 words condition 6 ("looked at together as they now are", a condition on a state with no memory of what changed), that is owed, and S1 is not stationary.
- Read as core's «changeset» words it (every piece that depends on one that *changed* is changed too or affirmed), nothing is owed: the description did not change, so nothing is asked of the eli8, and S1 is reached exactly as you tell it.

The first version of this item gave only the first reading and said S1 "is not stationary by the letter". Three of the four perspective files pointed at core's rule and I dropped it. I also held the two ends of the story to different standards: I supplied the looks at S0 that the story does not mention and then demanded them at 10:30. What I think is actually here is that note 22 and core word this condition differently, and the example is where it shows.

The same goes for a related observation of the TLA+ fork. Asked for every way the fork could close, its shortest route keeps the *old* eli8: the claim gained a hypothesis, the description was affirmed unchanged, so nothing asks anyone to look at the eli8. That is core's rule working as written, an affirmed middle shielding the end, not a gap. Whether it should is a fair question. (The route also relies on a whole read that the fork invented as possible.)

Two smaller things. "c1′ is fine" is true of the question B asked and not of the auditor's, if the auditor's is a separate criterion, which the 11:00 row suggests and the story does not say. And the summary's "B passed a derivation with an error in step 3" is not on record as tabled, since A's discovery is not an event; that one is the TLA+ fork's and I think it stands.

## What each way of seeing showed

**Rules and facts** (Datalog, and two richer relatives). Six of note 22's nine conditions went down as rules, about fifty lines, and ran first time. The three left out are the two about *how well* something stands and the one about promises, and the first two are where this family is least at home; nobody tried them. A frontier item's cause and its remedy are the two kinds of leaf of its derivation, so "each naming what failed and what it would take" needs no design. I claimed that which news is good can be read off the rules without running a state. What was read off is narrower: given that a piece of evidence is already described, marking it as recorded and holding can only remove items. For the whole event the same reading says "either". I think the property is true of the small model, and Lean proves it for its own rendering, but that is by proof and argument and not by the reading. I expected these tools to enforce "computed, never typed", and they do not: both accept a status typed by hand beside the rules that compute it. This view cannot say "better", who, or a refused write. The reader also found a defect in my rules (a canon piece resting on a piece outside canon was marked and not owed), now corrected; no run changed.

**Types and proofs** (Lean 4). A checkpoint can be a type that cannot be filled in without a proof that nothing is owed; asking for one at 13:00 is rejected by the compiler. One qualification from the reader: the rendering bounds how far canon is followed by a number someone fills in, and with too small a number a checkpoint can be had for a state that owes. Asked what S1 rests on, Lean answers "the definitions"; B's checks are data the proof is *about*. A typed core would certify the record-keeping and never a claim. What kind of piece fills a slot, and how many, can be typing; "required" cannot, and nothing in the nine conditions can, because a fork has to pass through states that fail them. What makes even the first part possible is your slot states. On "the same, for this evidence": if sameness is equality of what was seen of a version, then chaining and running both ways are free, and a read that relies on existence only, normalization and an attested sameness are one idea. That is a tidy construction. It does not show that a mind's "these do not differ in what matters" is of that kind, and it does not answer core's worry about carry upon carry, which I had said it did: five such judgments can each be right and the first and last still differ. Lean gave nothing on forks.

**Relations, and a solver that hunts for counterexamples** (Alloy 6). Alloy has no recursion, so it had to find the model's computed side without any, and did: canon, sameness and fault are three reachabilities. Its real contribution was on two forks. It tried thirteen times to say in a sentence when two forks "do not meet", and each time the solver produced a pair that satisfied the sentence and still could not both close; a fourteenth was not decided. Most of the early ones are canon moving: a path with one step from each fork exists on neither. Two late ones are worth keeping, with the reader's care about each. One is a piece supported twice, each fork removing a different support; in its plain form, a claim checked once by way of one piece and once by way of another, and each fork rewrites one of them. The other is a finding about a *combination* of versions that no line has yet, which applies nowhere until two forks close together. I presented that as the model's answer to your "unconnected contradiction". It exists only because that rendering lets evidence be recorded about a combination no line holds; the TLA+ rendering does not. Whether a mind may check a combination that exists on no line is a question about the model that neither file asks, and I think it is a real one. The fork's reading of all its rounds: every frontier item is a conjunction, and two forks meet when some item takes one conjunct from each.

**States and steps** (TLA+). Item 5 and most of item 1. Its fork is plain that it was no help with the conditions themselves.

**Older fields.** The builders both wrote that fork-meeting "is the read-set and write-set condition of transaction processing" and that neither had checked a text. I read one: the nearer notion is *invariant confluence* (Bailis and others, 2015), and the reader confirmed my quotations of its definition and theorem. What follows from the theorem is less than I said. It follows that nobody can know from each fork alone that both may close; something at the close must look at the other. It does not follow that "reworked on the new checkpoint" is unavoidable in any engine. The lost rewrite has its own name there, the *lost update*, which I should have given. Assumption-based truth maintenance (1986) holds "going back loses work, not knowledge"; asked its question, the record of your example says that at each moment I tried between 08:30 and 14:00 no combination of versions ever written could have been rested at, and at 14:00 exactly one could. Build systems have evidence-with-reads and "same" under other names, and no guard; a guard is a regression test for a world where checks are not free.

## On "as types instead of terms"

`06-languages-as-homes.md` is the fuller answer and supersedes the paragraph that stood here. In one sentence: what may be stored has a shape that types or declarations carry; what a state owes is a function, best stated as rules as far as anyone tried, which is six conditions of nine; each further language is another hand-kept copy of that function; and finding out whether a general sentence about the model is true is a fourth job that none of those is.

## What none of this establishes

That any of it holds for the kernel spec. That the causal footing survives anything a builder built. That rules stay the right form for the conditions about how well something stands. Nothing here touches intake, "better", delivery, or who.
