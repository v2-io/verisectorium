# The model in a nutshell

*A self-contained statement of what a verisectorium is and how it works, meant to be held in the head. It assumes nothing from earlier notes. Written 2026-11-18 by a Fable 5.2 session at Joseph Wecker's request. It is a proposal: nothing in it has been built or run, and the last two sections say what it leaves out and what it rests on.*

---

## The whole thing in one paragraph

A verisectorium is a body of work in which many minds, each arriving cold, raise the proportion of what is true and built-upon to what a reader has to get through. It advances by one pattern: something is made off to the side on its own merits; a second mind checks it; a third reflects it into the whole, replacing what it supersedes, until the whole is at rest again. The whole is made of pieces small enough to be right or wrong on their own, each of a kind that says what must be asked of it. Every answer is either found out, and stands by evidence, or decided, and stands by whoever is behind it. The verisectorium holds as fact only what was written and what was done, and with each thing done, who did it and what it relied on. Whatever else can be computed from that record is computed, in place of being simply asserted: where it is out of line, how much would move if a given piece did, and what is sitting unplaced or unused, and for how long. At rest nothing is out of line, and what was knowingly left, and what lies open at the edge, are shown beside it.

## 1. What it is for

The purpose is a ratio: what is true and built-upon, over what a reader has to get through. The numerator alone is not enough. A verisectorium can be perfectly consistent and twice the size it needs to be, or consistent beside a thousand things brought in and never placed.

Two things about the ratio. Where the two pull apart, truer comes before shorter. And it is a ratio for some reader, starting where that reader starts: precision a mind has nothing yet to hang on arrives as noise, so the same sentence raises the ratio for one reader and lowers it for another. A body of work too large for any one mind therefore needs layers, a scaffold that fits in a mind and deeper pieces that hang on it, with each simpler telling tied to the fuller piece it tells. A simpler telling is honest when the fuller thing is held behind it and learning more enlarges it without reversing it.

The purpose is decided. No amount of computing over the record produces it. What the record can do is steer by it. A purpose with no feedback loop does not steer; and, in Joseph's words, "Feedback loops without purpose can't supply a purpose." Getting back to rest is the harbor. It is not what the ship is for.

From the side of the mind using it, three things the model and its tools should reach for, in Joseph's words:

1. "Allow the minds to quickly get caught up to the locus of action."
2. "Allow the minds as much as possible to think and work in the domain of the problem, not the tooling."
3. "Prevent easily preventable errors of [e.g., misattribution, incoherence, hyperbole, ...]."

The first is the ratio seen from the arriving mind. A distinction belongs in the model if one of these three needs it.

## 2. How the work moves

Work comes from two directions. An **audit** asks whether what is there is right as it stands. A **spike** is an open-ended attempt at something not yet known: a mind is given a question at the edge of what is established and pushes on it until something yields. In most verisectoria the spike is the main way the work moves forward. Outside material, a paper or a report, arrives already made. All three follow one pattern:

1. **Made**, off to the side, on its own merits. The maker draws on the whole as a reader would: to frame the question, to see what is already settled, and to say how it thinks the result should be placed. It suggests the placing and does not carry it out.
2. **Checked**, by a second mind, on the made thing's own premises, with latitude to hold it against the truth in any way it sees fit.
3. **Reflected into the whole**, by a third, who settles the whole to rest again on the whole's terms. What is superseded is replaced, not kept beside.

Three minds because there are three things to answer to (the thing's merits, its premises, the whole's terms) and each pulls against the others in one head. A finding that overturns a great deal is reflected in stages, with a resting state after each.

Because everything made has to be reflected in and not merely added, the denominator is forced: what comes in must displace or be placed.

## 3. What the whole is made of

**Pieces**, each small enough to be right or wrong on its own, gathered into larger pieces to any depth: steps into a result, results into a chapter, chapters into a work.

Each piece is of a **kind**, and the kind says what must be asked of it. This is where verisectoria differ, and the dozens of hand-kept bodies of work this model was drawn from already distinguish about eleven things a piece can exist to carry:

| A piece that carries | has to answer |
|---|---|
| a claim | does it follow; is it still so; who has reproduced it |
| a rule | who stands behind it; is it consistent with the others |
| a definition | is it used as defined; does it collide |
| a decision | who decided, on what grounds, what would reopen it |
| a passage or a saying | does it match its source; who said it, when |
| a question | who may close it; has it been closed |
| a finding | was it borne out; what does it open |
| an argument | does the piece built on it make that argument |
| a practice | does it come to mind when it should, and does it help |
| a proof or working | is each step right |
| an account of something that happened | whose account, from what record |

The model does not say what shape "how well it stands" takes. A level reached, a party behind it, a size with a spread, a plain match: each kind says its own.

Pieces point at one another, and what matters about a pointer is **whether anything relies along it, and for what**: for being true, for saying what another piece says, for where it came from, or for nothing at all, as a way in.

## 4. What counts as the whole

An **outline** is a piece that gathers others in an order: this is the work, and this is how it is arranged. Joseph: "canon is whatever the verisectorium says it is-- usually the main outline's non-provisional slots" (a slot is a place in an outline; provisional means on approval). A verisectorium marks one or more outlines as its roots. What they reach, through places that are filled and not marked as on approval, is the whole that readers build on and that has to be kept in line. There may be more than one root, each for its own purpose and its own reader, and what a piece has to have answered is what the roots reaching it ask.

Everything else is, to the whole, only the claim that someone suggested it, and nothing inside it is held to anything. That is deliberate. It gives a low-stakes way to hold a low-certainty idea, and without one a mind chooses between silence and overclaiming. The steps run from least committed to most: a note left beside a piece; a place in an outline where something is suggested; a place where someone in the work has promised something; a piece put in place on approval; a piece in the whole. Each is cheap to take.

An outline with most of its places empty is still a true statement of the work, and it is the first thing an arriving mind needs: what the work is, in what order, and which parts are settled.

## 5. Two kinds of answer

Every answer is **found out** or **decided**. They stand differently, and they change differently.

| | stands by | changes when |
|---|---|---|
| found out | evidence | what it relied on changes, or evidence shows it wrong |
| decided | whoever is behind it | they say so; and they look again when a ground they named fails |

Each rests on the other: a decision's grounds are things found out, and where "and what supports that?" stops is a decision.

The second column is why specs keep a column for derived against chosen. A derived rule can be changed only by going back to the decisions that forced it. A chosen one can be changed where it stands, and the cost depends on who chose it and how much rested on the choice when it was made. A choice an agent made a moment ago to close a hole, and a choice made deliberately by the steward (the person who answers for the verisectorium), are both decisions; only the record of who, and at what importance, tells them apart.

## 6. What is held as fact

Two things only.

- **What was written**: each piece at each of its versions, which never change.
- **What was done**: who checked or decided what, about which exact version, in what way, and what they relied on in doing it.

As much of the second as possible is captured from what a mind did and not typed by it. In the hand-kept verisectoria, who and when are typed by hand in dozens of kinds of piece.

Because a check is about an exact version, one thing follows without any rule being added: a piece that is rewritten has, for the moment, not been checked. A change lowers certainty by itself. One thing does not follow and is chosen: a check that found something wrong also puts a question to the piece, whether what was found is still there, and a question is about the piece and not a version, so it stays until someone answers it. With both, rewording is not a way out of a finding, and a repaired piece says honestly that it is unchecked until it has been.

## 7. What is computed, wherever it can be

Four readings of that one record. Each is computed in place of being simply asserted, wherever that is possible. Where something has to be asserted instead, because nothing can yet compute it or because it rests on someone's word, the assertion is itself a thing done: on record, with who made it.

1. **Where it is out of line.** Two things, the same for every kind: something relied on has since moved, or something that must be answered has no answer. The first covers a pointer whose target was rewritten, a check whose premises changed, a decision whose ground failed, and a whole-read of something since reordered. The second covers what a kind asks, what a root asks, and any question put to a piece.
2. **How each piece stands**, by the rule its kind states. Across the hand-kept verisectoria the commonest field of all is `status`, typed by hand. If the tool does not put a computed answer there, minds will type one.
3. **How much latitude there is.** The one question, *what relied on this?*, run both ways. Downstream: what would move. Upstream: which decisions a derived thing finally rests on, and for each, who made it and at what importance.
4. **What is accumulating.** Wherever things can pile up (material brought in and not yet placed, suggestions, pieces nothing relies on, open questions, things knowingly left) how many and how old. How much may sit is each verisectorium's to say.

## 8. Rest, and what stands at rest

The whole is **at rest** when nothing is out of line. What a reader builds on is always a resting state.

Shown beside it, always: what was knowingly left out of line, with the reason; and what is **open at the edge**, the questions about whether more could be said. The first list has to be cleared or knowingly left. The second may stand, and it is where the work goes next. An honest "not yet" belongs to the second and needs no excuse.

Work is done off to the side and rejoins as one step. An attempt can be abandoned without losing what it found out, since what was found out is about a version, and a version is the same thing wherever it appears.

## 9. Bringing in

Everything enters the same way, whether it was made outside or by a spike here. From the moment a source is known of, the verisectorium can say whether it has been read, by whom, and how far. It is cut into chunks, and the cut covers all of it, so that "every chunk dealt with" cannot mean "found something". Each chunk says what it bears on.

Where a chunk goes is a decision: into an existing piece, into a new one, already there, sent elsewhere, or left out with a reason. Leaving out has to be as respectable as putting in, or what cannot be absorbed has nowhere to go but the pile. A source is retired when every chunk has been placed, and if a later rewrite drops what a chunk delivered, the chunk is back.

Beside every piece is a place for what bears on it and has not been placed, and for any note a mind wants to leave. It is not part of the piece, so filling or emptying it never changes the piece.

This is where the failure that prompted the model happened. In late October 2026 hundreds of agents were set to checking and repairing a mathematical theory kept by hand as a verisectorium. Nothing brought in was ever retired, each pass left more than it removed, and nobody could tell "found everything" from "found something". A verisectorium with a thousand unplaced chunks has nothing out of line. It shows only in the fourth reading of section 7.

## 10. One small example

A formal spec. Rule R says nesting stops at two levels. It was derived from decision D ("keep the grammar regular"), which the steward made, on the ground G that nobody nests deeper than two.

| What happens | What the record then shows |
|---|---|
| A usage report shows people nesting to four. | G has evidence against it. D named G as a ground, so D is to be looked at again by whoever stands behind it. Nothing has been rewritten. |
| An agent asks how much latitude it has on R. | R is derived: changing it means reopening D. D is the steward's, made at high importance, and eleven rules rest on it. Not a thing to change in passing. |
| The agent finds a hole: what a tab means at depth three is undefined. It chooses, weakly, to settle it. | A decision by that agent, at low importance, a moment old, with nothing resting on it. Anyone may try another way. |
| Off to the side, someone works out what the grammar would be without D, and suggests how it would be placed. A second mind checks it. | Made and checked. The whole has not moved. Eleven rules still say what they said. |
| The steward decides. A third mind reflects it in, in two stages: first the rules that only needed rewording, with the rest marked as knowingly left; then the rest. | Two resting states. At the first, a reader sees which rules are still to come. At the second, nothing is out of line, the old R is gone, and the agent's weak choice about tabs is still shown as weak. |

## What this leaves out, and what it is not sure of

**Left out on purpose, as belonging to particular kinds of work and not to every verisectorium.** For a theory: the rule that a claim found to say too much is first strengthened, if it can be, before it is weakened, and that a proof it cannot be had is a result in its own right; levels of support, and how a premise's support limits what rests on it. For a specification: the full anatomy of a decision record. These matter most where the stakes are highest, and they are what a kind supplies.

**Left out as detail.** How pieces are named and versioned. The difference between a piece that lives inside another and one that is pointed at from several places. Dependence between the parts of one document, and the rule that a write either changes or expressly affirms each part that depends on what changed. Which bad states a tool should refuse outright and which it should allow and list. When two lines of work off to the side may both rejoin. Each has been worked out elsewhere; I think each is right or nearly, and that none is needed to hold the model.

**Not sure of:**

- **What content may say about its own standing.** "Computed wherever possible, in place of being simply asserted" is all this note has. Whether prose inside a piece ("we prove", "verified") needs a rule of its own has been answered three different ways and none has been shown to do any work.
- **Who, beyond being recorded.** Three minds are in the pattern, and who decided is part of the latitude. Whose evidence counts, and what makes two checks independent when nearly every checker is the same kind of mind, are set aside.
- **What answers a question put by evidence against a piece.** In practice, a mind other than the one who made the repair. This note does not say.
- **Reliance nobody wrote down.** Every computed reading in section 7 is as good as what was recorded as relied on. The practice that finds the rest is building a map of the neighbourhood before working, and writing down what it turns up.
- **A piece saying when it should come to mind.** Hand-kept verisectoria write this on practices and sayings. It bears on the first objective and has no place here.
- **Things outside that move with nobody watching**: a library version, a cited source, a date.
- **Bringing in.** It is the door for outside material and for the work's own discoveries, it is where the motivating failure was, and nobody has built or run it. Section 9 is the least tested section here.
