The acta entry for this (2026-11-10T16:01) says: "every piece of bad news in that record was a narrowing, which the model had let lapse on a rewrite."

**A hole the note's wording reopens.** Ada account, G4, "Found by the search, not predicted": "Evidence applies only while 'each of e's reads is as it was'. That is right for a pass. For a finding it means: reword any piece the finding read, and the finding stops applying to the piece it was about, which is untouched". The note's 3.3 has exactly this definition of *applies*, for good and bad alike. So under the note, a failing check of claim c that read derivation d stops applying when d alone is reworded. By my reasoning the piece then reads as unchecked and not as known wrong, which is the honest lowering the note wants; the question is whether "unchecked" is enough, and the next paragraph is why I doubt it.

**The model's one real failure, run through the note (my reasoning, not a test).** The stopped bound was passed by several minds who followed one written proof and was broken by a different means. Suppose that finding is on record under note 27, and the claim is then reworded without being repaired. The finding no longer applies. The root in 3.8 asks "by following the derivation and one other means". A check by following, plus one afresh that shares the proof's error, satisfies the root, and the state is stationary with the defect in it. The note's answer is that the old finding "stays on record against the earlier version, where the next checker is shown it", and that a verisectorium "can say in what its roots ask" whether the finding's means must be used. But a root asks per kind of piece. It has no way, in the note as written, to ask something of one piece because of that piece's history. So the protection rests on a rendering and on the checker's choice, in the one case the means were added to the model for.

**The note already has a persistence rule, for the weaker kind of bad news.** Part 5: "A claim in an old audit that something is wrong enters as a question about the piece." Clause 7: "Every question raised about it has been answered since." And 4.1: "a question, a promise or a waiver is unconditional and stands until withdrawn". A question is about a piece, not a version. So a relayed, unchecked claim of a defect outlasts every rewrite until answered, and a first-hand check that found the defect does not. Note 22a's last section asks nearly this ("if a found defect is a question that stays asked"). It may be the principled form of what note 22's rule was reaching for: failing evidence raises a question about the piece, which stands until answered, with no presumption about later versions. I offer it as something the note's own parts suggest. I have not tested it, and the builders' four holes were in the wording of when such a thing counts as answered (note 23 §8), which this would still have to say.

**The exit that grows under choice 1.** `instance-trial-2` §2: two integrators "replaced this segment's main claim twice in under four hours", and "recording a rewrite as 'retired, and a new piece minted' sheds everything owed on the old one. ## 4. Four things absent and not listed as left out

Note 27's "Against core" paragraph lists what it leaves out on purpose. These are not in that list.

**4.1 Levels of support along what a piece rests on.** The note has pieces standing "how well" (computed), roots asking "how well", decisions conditioned on "claims standing at least this well", and clause 8 as a yes or no: rests "only on pieces in canon that are themselves consistent". It does not say that how well a piece stands is limited by how well its premises stand. I searched the note for "cap", "least of" and "weight" and found none. What the unread side holds:

- Joseph, 11-05 15:53: things depend on a piece "*at* some ladder-level-- another nuance for dependencies we need to capture-- there are also *possible* / latent / implicit dependencies on a ladder-level even if it's not in the instance-- it's good for the system to say 'now that it's at *this* claim-support level-- which no dependent claim can rise above now-- can this other instance downstream here be a theorem anymore?'"
- The acta, 2026-11-09T16:35: "dimension's Definition states the cap as an equation (effective warrant is the least of own and the targets' effective warrants, a missing target the lowest)", with acyclicity tied to that equation having one solution.
- Ada account, G7: "what a root asks reaches *through the cap* to pieces the root does not reach. A root asking validity 2 of p2 is not satisfied until p1, which only a more lenient root reaches, has it too." Under the note, p1 is consistent by its own root and p2 rests on it without complaint. A paper's root resting on a theory's pieces is this case.
- `instance-trial-2` §7: a claim predicated on an imported lemma, where the import's kind opens a *source* dimension and not validity. "Core … gives no rule for the cap at such a target … A theory is full of claims that rest on borrowed results, so this is not a corner."
- Both builders: consistency is a greatest fixed point and "not the only fixed point" (pieces resting on each other in a ring). Clause 8 defines consistent in terms of consistent and the note does not say which solution is meant.

Note 22a ends unsure "whether levels of support and the cap along what a piece rests on fit inside 'grounded'". The note sorted the nine under the three ideas without carrying that doubt.

## 8. The note as a whole

I think its direction is his: purpose on the first page, the two atoms, events with forks, canon by placement, who left for later. Where it restores things (means, against, backing, importance, the halt), the unread side agrees they were lost. Three things about its shape.

**It is the next note in a line he has described.** 11-08 20:20, founding core-2: the aim is to "have the core model thoughtfully and carefully accumulate additional nuance and correctness every iteration, instead of the march of notes you currently see that is the 'other way' of trying to do something this complex, that doesn't work given constant turnover". He asked for this one, and a note is the right place for a proposal. The risk is in the step after: if Part 9 is carried out as "the note becomes the spine", core's content is replaced by one mind's synthesis from a partial read, which is the move the praxis exists to prevent. If instead each of the eight choices goes to core as a working note on the def it concerns, with the whole thing and not a pointer, the note has done its work and core has accumulated.

**The word count went the wrong way, and the direction is the thing he watches.** The note counts fifty-nine against note 22's fifty and calls nine a cost. 11-08 17:22: "look at how simple the notes have some of the earlier things--- 6 or 7 terms total … And this session has just been multiplying and multiplying terms again." **It is tested on less than what it replaces.** The note says so plainly. What I would add is where the tested and untested parts fall. The builders' frame result (what is owed about a piece depends only on what that piece reads) is about the function from a state to what it owes, and the Ada author calls it "the thing I would keep whatever else changes", because it depends "only on the frontier being computed instance by instance from what each instance reads".
