# The model in a nutshell, after four stores

*A self-contained statement of what a verisectorium is and how it works, meant to be held in the head. It assumes nothing from earlier notes. Written 2026-11-19 by a Fable 5.2 agent that arrived fresh, at the request of Joseph Wecker and of the session that wrote note 28. It is note 28 said again after that note was taken to four real bodies of work: a specification still in design, a philosophy paper built from sources, an application built by agents for one person, and a collection of sayings, practices and accounts that feeds a system prompt. In each, a mind imagined what the store wants to be and reported where the note helped, strained or was silent. This is a proposal. Nothing in it has been built or run. Sections 1 to 10 are the model. After them: how this differs from note 28, with what I left out that a pilot asked for and what I kept that a pilot argued against; what it is not sure of; and what it rests on.*

## The whole thing in one paragraph

A verisectorium is a body of work in which many minds, each arriving cold, raise the proportion of what is true and built-upon to what a reader has to get through. It is made of pieces small enough to be right or wrong on their own, each of a kind that says what can be asked of it, and of roots, each an outline of the work for a particular reader that says what that reader needs answered before building on a piece. Every answer is either found out, and stands by evidence, or decided, and stands by whoever is behind it, and most pieces have one of each. The work advances by one pattern: something is made off to the side on its own merits; it is checked on its own premises by something other than its maker; and it is reflected into the whole on the whole's terms, replacing what it supersedes. The verisectorium holds as fact only what was written and what was done: who did it, about exactly what, in what way, and relying on what. From that record, and in place of anyone's say-so wherever it can be, each arriving mind is shown what is waiting, for how long and on whom; how each piece stands; how much would move if a given piece did; and where a root is out of line. A root is at rest when nothing in it is out of line, and what was knowingly left, and what lies open at its edge, are shown beside it.

## 1. What it is for

*(This section is note 28's, as Joseph last edited it. Nothing in it is changed.)*

The purpose is generally to serve truth, which requires the presence and honesty of that truth with as much clarity as possible. It can be thought of as a ratio: what is known to be true by its degree, over what a reader has to get through to comprehend it;  that is, the truth to noise ratio. The numerator alone is not enough. A verisectorium can be perfectly consistent and twice the size it needs to be, therefore making much of the truth inaccessible, or consistent beside a thousand things brought in and never placed that also hold truth but unadjudicated and undistilled.

Two things about the ratio. Where the two pull apart, truer comes before shorter. And it is a ratio for some *specific reader* (an individual, not an abstraction), starting where that reader starts: precision a mind has nothing yet to hang on arrives as noise, so the same sentence raises the ratio for one reader and lowers it for another. A body of work too large for any one mind therefore needs layers, a scaffold that fits in a mind and deeper pieces that hang on it, with each simpler telling tied to the fuller piece it tells. A simpler telling is honest when the fuller thing is held behind it and learning more enlarges it without reversing it.

The purpose is decided. No amount of computing over the record produces it. What the record can do is steer by it. A purpose with no feedback loop does not steer; and, in Joseph's words, "Feedback loops without purpose can't supply a purpose." Getting back to rest is the harbor. It is not what the ship is for.

From the side of the mind using it, three things the model and its tools should reach for, in Joseph's words:

1. "Allow the minds to quickly get caught up to the locus of action."
2. "Allow the minds as much as possible to think and work in the domain of the problem, not the tooling."
3. "Prevent easily preventable errors of [e.g., misattribution, incoherence, hyperbole, ...]."

The first is the ratio seen from the arriving mind. A distinction belongs in the model if one of these three needs it.

## 2. How the work moves

Work comes from two directions. An **audit** asks whether what is there is right as it stands. A **spike** is an open-ended attempt at something not yet known: a mind is given a question at the edge of what is established and pushes on it until something yields. In most verisectoria the spike is the main way the work moves forward. Outside material, a paper or a report or one sentence from the steward (the person who answers for the verisectorium), arrives already made. All three follow one pattern:

1. **Made**, off to the side, on its own merits. The maker draws on the whole as a reader would: to frame the question, to see what is already settled, and to say how it thinks the result should be placed. It suggests the placing and does not carry it out.
2. **Checked**, on the made thing's own premises, by something other than its maker, with latitude to hold it against the truth in any way it sees fit.
3. **Reflected into the whole**, by a mind answering for the whole, which settles it to rest again on the whole's terms. What is superseded is replaced.

There are three things to answer to (the thing's merits, its premises, the whole's terms), and each pulls against the others in one head. Where the stakes ask for it they are three minds, and for a spike in a theory that is the practice. What is constant is that all three answerings happen and that the record says who or what did each. The rest varies with the work. The checking may be done by a program that can be run again; and content that was captured and not composed (a quotation lifted from its source by naming where it starts and stops) needs no second mind, though whatever was composed around it does. How much has to come before reflecting in depends on how cheaply the reflecting can be undone: for a paper about to be sent, nearly everything; for software behind a gate of tests, the cold read can come after. A finding that overturns a great deal is reflected in stages, with a resting state after each.

Sometimes what was made is a set of options with a recommendation, and the whole's terms include a decision only one party can give. Reflecting in then waits for them, or the thing goes in as what it is (a lean, a first guess) and is shown as that. What the decider is handed is a rendering for one reader at the most expensive point in the round.

Reflecting in is where the denominator is forced: what comes in displaces something or is placed, or the reflecting mind says why the whole may grow for the reader it is for. Replaced means replaced in what the whole now says. Every earlier version is still in the record (section 7), and that is where a reader goes for the trail. A piece that is itself a record, someone's words or an account of what happened, is not superseded by a better understanding, only corrected where it got the record wrong. How a kind is superseded is the kind's to say.

## 3. What an arriving mind is shown

Everything after this section is what has to be written, recorded and computed so that a screen like this one can be shown and be true. The store is invented: a small specification with two readers. No such tool exists.

```

  for people working on the spec    not at rest: 2 out of line
  for someone implementing it       at rest; went out as 0.3 nine days ago; 1 thing recorded against it since

  on anyone: made and checked, never reflected in            1     19 days   a grammar worked out without the depth limit
  brought in, not yet placed                                 7      9 days   a usage report: 3 of its 10 chunks placed
  weak choices nobody has looked at since                    3
```

Nothing on it is a status that anyone typed. Each of the four pilots drew its own version of this screen from its own store, and all four show whether the work is at rest, where it is out of line, and what has piled up, with counts and ages.

## 4. Pieces and kinds

**Pieces**, each small enough to be right or wrong on its own, gathered into larger pieces to any depth: steps into a result, results into a chapter, chapters into a work.

Each piece is of a **kind**, and the kind says what can be asked of it. This is where verisectoria differ. The hand-kept bodies of work this model was drawn from, and the four it has now been taken to, distinguish about a dozen things a piece can exist to carry. Nearly every one is asked something of each sort: something that someone decides, and something that is found out.

| A piece that carries | someone decides | is found out |
|---|---|---|
| a claim | will its author stand behind it | does it follow; is each step right; is it still so; who has reproduced it |
| a rule, or what a made thing is meant to do | who stands behind it, and by what act | is it consistent with the others; does the made thing do it |
| a definition | whose word it is, and who stands behind it | is it used as defined; does it collide |
| a decision | who decided, by what act, in whose words; what would reopen it | do its grounds still hold |
| someone's words, or a passage | | does it match its source; who said it, when, on what occasion |
| a telling of another piece (a gloss, a summary, an explanation) | which part is the other's and which is ours | is it faithful; does the fuller piece enlarge it without reversing it |
| a question | who may close it | has it been closed, and by what |
| a finding | where does it go | was it borne out; what does it open |
| a measurement | | under what conditions; does it reproduce |
| a practice | | does it come to mind when it should, and does it help |
| an account of something that happened | | whose account, from what record |
| a check that can be run again | | can it fail; did it run against what it claims; what can it not see |

The model does not say what shape "how well it stands" takes. A level reached, a party behind it, a size with a spread, a count of the times it worked, a plain match: each kind says its own. A kind is worth adding when something fails, and is repaired, differently from every kind already there.

Pieces point at one another, and what matters about a pointer is **whether anything relies along it, and for what**: for being true, for saying what another piece says, for where it came from, or for nothing at all, as a way in.

Not everything relied on is a piece. A sentence about what an application does relies on the build; a quotation on its source; a measurement on the machine and the versions of everything it ran under. These are held outside the pieces, have versions of their own where they have any, and are asked nothing. They are what answers are about.

## 5. Roots

An **outline** is a piece that gathers others in an order: this is the work, and this is how it is arranged. Each entry in it is a **place**. Joseph: "canon is whatever the verisectorium says it is-- usually the main outline's non-provisional slots" (a slot is a place; provisional means on approval).

A **root** is an outline the verisectorium answers for, and it is for a reader. It says who that reader is and where they start; which places it reaches; and what it asks of what it reaches: which of the answers a kind allows this reader needs before building on a piece, and for each, whether a missing answer puts the root out of line or is only shown, with its age. So a kind says what can be asked of a piece, and a root says what has to have been. A piece is held to what the roots reaching it ask, and to nothing else. A root can also ask things of itself as a whole: that it reads in order to someone who does not know it, with every term met before it is used; that it stays within a length; that nothing private reaches it.

Most bodies of work want more than one root over the same pieces, because they have more than one reader. The people working on a specification build on leans and need to know that no two rules contradict; someone implementing it needs every rule decided and every case settled. An argument is one body of work and each paper drawn from it is a root, so a claim is said once and a change to it shows in both papers. An agent in the middle of a task and a mind orienting on purpose want the same sayings in different orders, and are owed different things about each.

The ratio of section 1 is for a specific reader, and a reader belongs to a root. So the ratio, the denominator and rest (section 8) are reckoned root by root.

There has to be a low-stakes way to hold a low-certainty idea, or a mind chooses between silence and overclaiming. The steps run from least committed to most: a note left beside a piece; a place in an outline where something is suggested; a place where someone in the work has promised something; a piece put in a place on approval; a piece in place. Each is cheap to take. What no root reaches is, to every reader, only the claim that someone suggested it, and nothing inside it is held to anything. Being on approval is different. It says which roots reach a piece: usually the root for those working on it, and not yet the root others build on.

An outline with most of its places empty is still a true statement of the work, and it is the first thing an arriving mind needs: what the work is, in what order, and which parts are settled.

## 6. Two kinds of answer

Every answer is **found out** or **decided**. They stand differently, and they change differently.

| | stands by | changes when |
|---|---|---|
| found out | evidence | what it relied on changes, or evidence shows it wrong |
| decided | whoever is behind it | they say so; and they look again when a ground they named fails |

Each rests on the other: a decision's grounds are things found out, and where "and what supports that?" stops is a decision. And most pieces have one of each (section 4): who stands behind a rule, and whether it agrees with the others; whether a behaviour is wanted, and whether the built thing does it.

The second column is why specs keep a column for derived against chosen. A derived rule can be changed only by going back to the decisions that forced it. A chosen one can be changed where it stands, and what that costs depends on what was done in choosing it and on how much has come to rest on it since.

**What was done in deciding.** "Decided" is not one thing. The person with standing ruled after thought, or assented to something put to them, or leaned, or saw it and did not object; or a mind chose for them on what it took to be their intent; or a mind chose to close a hole; or nobody chose and it is in force by habit. Each is a different path for revisiting, from "change it freely" to "work out why it was decided, check that reading with them, and decide together". The steps and their names are each verisectorium's. The model asks that the record hold the act and not the word "decided", and that a weak choice be shown as weak for as long as it stays one. How much rests on a decision is not recorded with it. It is computed (section 8), and a choice made cheaply that a great deal has since come to rest on is the thing to show someone with standing.

**In whose words.** A decision recorded on someone else's behalf points at their words, located in a source like any other quotation. Without them it is shown as attributed and not located. Where nobody can say what act the words were, it is recorded as the least they support, and the person is asked. This is small, and it is the mechanism against the commonest error in the hand-kept stores: an agent's reading carried forward as the steward's ruling, until the steward reads it back as something already decided.

## 7. What is held as fact

Two things only.

- **What was written**: each piece at each of its versions, which never change. (Where pieces live under version control, that already is this.)
- **What was done**: who or what did it; about exactly what; in what way; and what it relied on.

*About exactly what* is a piece at a version, and where the thing done was narrower than the piece, which part or which words: a check that passed a quoted span says nothing of the gloss beside it. *In what way* is how the question was asked, and from where: by following the written derivation or by building a case against it; in the browser the tests drive or in the one the steward uses; by a program anyone can run again or by a look that happened once. The record shows how two checks differed, and whether the checker and the thing checked had the same author. It does not rule on whether they were independent. *What it relied on* is the pieces it read, at their versions, and the things outside the pieces that it was about, at theirs where they have any: a build, a source by its content and not its path, a toolchain, a result held in another verisectorium.

Things done are of more sorts than checking and deciding. Something was read, and how far. A search was run. A piece was made, a chunk placed, a result reflected in, a problem knowingly left. And a piece was used: a practice came to mind or did not, a reader got through or did not, the steward looked at the running thing.

As much of this as possible is captured from what a mind did and not typed by it: who and when, the version, what was opened, where off to the side a thing was made, the step that reflected it in, a run of the tests and exactly what it ran against. What is left to type is small and is all judgment: what a look was of, why a thing is left, what a decision rests on, where a chunk goes. If recording a check costs more than a sentence of prose, minds will write the prose.

Three things follow.

**A change lowers certainty by itself.** A check is about an exact version, so a piece that is rewritten has, for the moment, not been checked. No rule is added for this.

**A check that can be run again gives it back as fast as it runs.** Call a program that answers a question about a piece, or about the made thing, an **instrument**: a test, a script that compares a quotation with its source, a scan for what must not appear. Where an instrument covers a piece, what stands behind the piece is that the instrument exists and said yes about this version. A check that happened once is about the version it saw and no later one, and much of the honest work in a body of work that builds something is turning once-only checks into instruments. An instrument is itself a piece (section 4), and it can be the thing that is wrong.

**Evidence against a piece outlasts a rewrite.** This one is chosen. A check that found something wrong also puts a question to the piece, whether what was found is still there. A question is about the piece and not a version, so it stays until it is answered by something other than whoever made the repair. With both, rewording is not a way out of a finding, and a repaired piece says honestly that it is unchecked until it has been.

## 8. What is computed, and rest

Four readings of that one record. Each is computed in place of being simply asserted, wherever that is possible. Where something has to be asserted instead, because nothing can yet compute it or because it rests on someone's word, the assertion is itself a thing done: on record, with who made it.

What is waiting comes first, because it is the only one of the four that shows the failure this model was prompted by (section 9).

1. **What is waiting.** Wherever things can pile up, how many and how old, and where it is a person they wait on, who. Material brought in and not yet placed. Things made and checked and never reflected in. Decisions put to the steward, and looks only the steward can give. Weak choices nobody has looked at since. Pieces no root reaches, and pieces nothing relies on. Open questions. Things knowingly left. How much may sit is each verisectorium's to say. A pile can grow with nothing going out of line, which is how a body of work can be perfectly consistent and stalled.
2. **How each piece stands**, by the rule its kind states. Across the hand-kept verisectoria the commonest field of all is `status`, typed by hand. If the tool does not put a computed answer there, minds will type one. What the one typed word was hiding is several facts that move independently: what the piece says; who stands behind it, and by what act; where it is placed; what has been found out about it, and in what way; and which rests that went out the door include it. A design, a decision, a plan and a delivery are four of these, and not four values of one field.
3. **How much latitude there is.** The one question, *what relied on this?*, run both ways. Downstream: what would move. Upstream: which decisions a derived thing finally rests on, and for each, who made it and by what act.
4. **Where a root is out of line.** Two things, the same for every kind: something relied on has since moved, or something the root needs answered has no answer. The first covers a pointer whose target was rewritten, a telling whose original changed, a check whose premises changed, a decision one of whose grounds now has evidence against it, an order changed under something that relied on it, and an outside thing now at another version. The second covers what the root asks of each kind and of itself, and any question put to a piece by evidence against it.

A root is **at rest** when nothing in it is out of line. What its reader builds on is a resting state of that root. A root for people at work rests on easier terms than the root for those who will build on the result, and each says which it is.

Shown beside a rest, always: what was **knowingly left** out of line, with the reason and when it will be looked at again; and what is **open at the edge**. The edge holds the questions about whether more could be said, and the answers the root wants and does not wait for, some of which are never finished and only accrue (whether a practice comes to mind for someone who did not write it; whether the steward has yet seen a behaviour). The first list has to be cleared or knowingly left. The second may stand, and it is where the work goes next. An honest "not yet" belongs to the second and needs no excuse.

A rest can **go out the door**: sent to a journal, published as the place others start from, loaded into every session as a prompt. It is kept exactly as it went. What comes back about it is about that state, and is also new material, brought in like any other.

Work is done off to the side and rejoins as one step. An attempt can be abandoned without losing what it found out, since what was found out is about a version, and a version is the same thing wherever it appears.

## 9. Bringing in

Everything enters the same way, whether it was made outside or by a spike here. A **source** may be a document, one message in a stream that never ends, or a search over something too large to cut.

From the moment a source is known of, the verisectorium can say how far it has been read, and by whom. What is brought in is **accounted for in full**. For a document, every part is either cut into chunks or on record as not read: a book read for one page is honestly brought in when the rest is down as unread. For a search, what is on record is what was looked for, where, and how far the results were read. So "dealt with" cannot mean "found something", and a statement that nothing is there rests on a record of having looked. Each chunk says what it bears on.

Where a chunk goes is a decision: into an existing piece, into a new one, already there, sent elsewhere, or left out with a reason. Leaving out has to be as respectable as putting in, or what cannot be absorbed has nowhere to go but the pile. A chunk has been placed when, if its source disappeared, everything it carried would be either in a piece or truly disposable; a note about the remainder is not the remainder. Material keeps the standing its source gave it and gains none by being brought in. A source is retired when every chunk has been placed, and if a later rewrite drops what a chunk delivered, the chunk is back.

Beside every piece is a place for what bears on it and has not been placed, and for any note a mind wants to leave. It is not part of the piece, so filling or emptying it never changes the piece.

This is where the failure that prompted the model happened. In late October 2026 hundreds of agents were set to checking and repairing a mathematical theory kept by hand as a verisectorium. Nothing brought in was ever retired, each pass left more than it removed, and nobody could tell "found everything" from "found something". A verisectorium with a thousand unplaced chunks has nothing out of line. It shows only in the first reading of section 8. The four stores show the same failure at smaller scale, in two places: things made and never reflected in (two of them), and things waiting on the one person who can decide them or look at them (three).

## 10. Two small examples

Both are invented.

**A formal spec.** Rule R says nesting stops at two levels. It was derived from decision D ("keep the grammar regular"), which the steward made, on the ground G that nobody nests deeper than two. There are two roots: one for the people working on the spec, one for someone implementing it.

| What happens | What the record then shows |
|---|---|
| A usage report is brought in and cut. One chunk shows people nesting to four. | G has evidence against it. D named G as a ground, so the working root is out of line at D until whoever stands behind D has looked again. Nothing has been rewritten. |
| An agent asks how much latitude it has on R. | R is derived: changing it means reopening D. D is the steward's, a ruling, in the steward's own located words, and eleven rules rest on it. Not a thing to change in passing. |
| The agent finds a hole: what a tab means at depth three is undefined. It chooses, weakly, to settle it. | A decision by that agent, to close a hole, a moment old, with nothing resting on it. Anyone may try another way. It is counted among the weak choices until someone with standing has looked. |
| Off to the side, someone works out what the grammar would be without D, and suggests how it would be placed. A second mind checks it. | Made and checked. No root has moved. Eleven rules still say what they said. It is counted as made and not reflected in, and it waits on the steward. |
| The steward is handed the choice in plain words, with examples, and says: "I think we drop the limit, but show me the indentation cases first." | Recorded as a lean, in those words, which is the least they support. A question goes back to the steward. It may be reflected into the working root as a lean. The root for implementers does not reach it. |
| The steward rules. A third mind reflects it in, in two stages: first the rules that only needed rewording, with the rest marked as knowingly left; then the rest. | Two resting states of the working root. At the first, a reader sees which rules are still to come. At the second, nothing is out of line; the old R is gone from what the spec says and is still in the record; and the agent's weak choice about tabs is still shown as weak. |

**An application.** Behaviour B: search results highlight the words that matched.

| What happens | What the record then shows |
|---|---|
| The steward, using the application, says in passing that matches ought to be highlighted. | A piece holding those words, exactly, cut from the message. B is written, pointing at them as where it came from. Behind B: the steward, by a lean. |
| A builder makes it off to the side and writes a test. The tests pass and it is reflected in. | B is wanted (a lean) and the built thing does it (by an instrument, on this build). The cold read comes afterwards, because this reflecting can be undone. |
| The steward sees it working and says so. | A look, once, by the only one who can give it, about that build. |
| Three weeks later a restyle changes the colours. The test still passes: it checks that the matched words are marked, and not that the mark can be seen. | Nothing is out of line. B is still covered by its instrument. The steward's look is shown as being about an earlier build, with nothing having carried it forward. |
| The steward notices the highlights are gone. | Evidence against B, from use. And evidence against the instrument: it does not check what it is named for. A question is put to each, and neither is closed by whoever makes the repair. |

The second example ends on what the model does not do. For three weeks something was false and nothing was out of line. The record could say that the only look at B was about an older build, and that B's instrument had never been shown able to fail. It could not say that B was false. That took the steward.

- *A rule for what carries a once-only look across a change* (the application). Its author calls it the thing in that pilot it is least sure of. The second example shows the case without the rule.
- *A "subject" as a named part of the model* (the application). Section 4's last paragraph says the same in three sentences.
- *A cue as a kind, and a firing with its own tally* (the collection). "Was used" is among the things done, and answers that accrue sit at the edge. The cue itself, a moment stated so it can be told whether it occurred, held as a piece and used to order a root, is the one worked answer so far to "when should this come to mind", and the application's pilot reached a compatible one (the trigger is an event the tooling can see). I think it is a kind's and not the core's, on one store's evidence.
- *Parts of a piece with their own authors* (the collection), and *evidence that points at words inside a piece* (the paper). One clause in section 7: a thing done says exactly what it was about.
- *Witnessed as a third kind of answer* (the collection). Its own pilot folded it into found out, by a witness and a chain of custody, and I agree.
- *Who may see a piece* (the paper, the collection). A root can ask that nothing private reaches it, like anything else a root asks.
- *A guard* (the paper): something a piece must keep doing through every rewrite. It falls out of a question being about the piece and not a version.
- *Reliance written from the upstream end*, *pointers into other verisectoria*, and *a place for the account of how a piece came to be* (the specification). Detail, or a kind.
- *The reader at the moment of acting* (the collection): a reader who holds the right principle and still does not have it in front of them at the tenth hour. It is the thing that store exists to say. The model's first objective is about arriving, and I have not stretched it. See below.

**Kept, though a pilot argued against it.**

- *A change lowers certainty by itself.* The application's whole apparatus exists so that a change does not. Both are true, and the instrument is how.
- *Three minds.* In two stores the three were mostly fewer. Kept as what the stakes can ask for, with the three answerings as what is constant.
- *What a reader builds on is a resting state.* False of the specification as note 28 had it, since its readers build on leans. True once rest is a root's.
- *Replaced, not kept beside.* Three stores keep things beside on purpose. The one design document in the application that had been rewritten to say what is so now was, by its pilot's account, the only one that did not have to be replayed to be understood.
- *Every answer is found out or decided.*
- *Pieces small enough to be right or wrong on their own.* A source file is not one and a sentence of prose is the wrong size to write. Kept, with things relied on that are not pieces, and with a thing done able to name the words it is about.

**One place this departs from Joseph's own words.** He has said that importance is always importance for something, and that the agreement a decision needs rises with it. Note 28 recorded a decision's importance. This note records the act, and computes how much rests on a decision. I think his point survives in two places: as the reason the act matters (a ruling is what high importance asks for), and as the reading that shows a cheap choice now bearing more than it was made to bear. But only one of the four pilots argued for this, one kept importance as written, and he should see that the word is gone and decide whether it should be.

**Still left out, as in note 28.** What belongs to particular kinds of work: for a theory, that a claim found to say too much is first strengthened if it can be, and that a proof it cannot be had is a result in its own right; levels of support and how a premise limits what rests on it; for a specification, the full anatomy of a decision record. And as detail: how pieces are named and versioned; dependence between the parts of one document; which bad states a tool refuses and which it allows and lists; when two lines of work off to the side may both rejoin.

**Size.** This note is larger than note 28, and I would rather that were said than found. Words new here that are used in a fixed sense: *act* (what was done in deciding), *instrument*, and a rest that *went out*. Gone: *importance*. *Reader*, *place* and *way* were in note 28 and carry more here. In length, the paragraph and sections 1 to 10 come to about 5,400 words against note 28's 2,750. About 600 of the difference is the screen and the second example. The rest is mostly in four places where a pilot arriving cold had to guess what a sentence meant: what a root asks, what counts as a thing done, what "in what way" covers, and what deciding is. One cutting pass took out about 400 words. If it should be smaller, the second example and the screen are the first things I would give up, and the root, the act and the words, and what is waiting are the last.

## Not sure of

- **What content may say about its own standing.** Two pilots brought data, and it points one way. Words about *how* a thing was established ("measured", "inferred from the code", "his words", "my reading") did real work for a cold reader and are content. A piece arguing its own limits (what its sample cannot separate, under what conditions it holds) is content, and by one pilot's account is the most valuable prose in its part of that store. A word about how well *another* piece stands ("derived", "verified", "published") is where every recorded failure was, four of them in one store, each repaired by hand in the same way: the word was taken out of the prose and drawn from one place. So a candidate rule: a telling does not type how another piece stands; it is given that from the record. This sits beside what Joseph has called the heart of the whole thing (what an answer about a piece is, and how being sure of it relates to it). He has not taken up any rule about prose, and this is a lean with four cases behind it and no run.
- **Whether the way of a check can be afforded.** One pilot found it on nearly every line of its store's prose, and the others found it typed by hand in theirs. A running record of some 445 checks in one of the estate's stores keeps what was checked, by whom, the outcome and when, and has no field for how. Minds plainly need to say it. Whether they will say it in a form a tool can read depends on how much of it can be captured.
- **Who, beyond being recorded.** Whose evidence counts, and what makes two checks independent, are still set aside. For work judged only by reading, a paper's claim or an essay's argument, the part set aside is most of what evidence is. Showing how two checks differed is a start and no more.
- **What carries a once-only check across a change.**
- **Things outside with no version to name.** A build, a toolchain and a source by its content can be captured. A fact about the world that goes stale cannot. Joseph keeps two questions apart there (how likely is this to be stale by now; how long can staleness be afforded), and nothing here answers either.
- **Reliance nobody wrote down.** As good as it was in note 28, with one addition: where an instrument can ask the question, the reliance can be made into one, and the tool can show which sentences nothing watches.
- **Two roots, or something simpler.** Giving working material a root of its own is how the specification's pilot gave it standing, and that pilot was not sure it was the right way. I took it because three other stores wanted several roots for their own reasons.
- **The act, with more than one steward.** With a council the act becomes a vote, which is another shape.
- **The reader at the moment of acting.** Above.
- **Bringing in.** Imagined on four stores now, and still built on none.

**What would show this wrong.** A store whose real working material cannot be given a root with a nameable reader. A decision whose path for revisiting does not depend on what was done in deciding it. A steward who, shown the first reading for a real store, does not find it the most useful of the four. The count of words rising again at the next statement.

## What it rests on, and where it came from

Nothing here was built or run. It rests on note 28 and what that rested on; on four pilots of note 28, each a mind's imagined ontology, imagined commands with examples, and feedback, for one real store; and on the census of kinds and fields in the hand-kept verisectoria.

About the pilots. Two were forks of the session that wrote note 28 and two were fresh agents given note 28 alone, one of which reports that more reached it through its harness than was meant. All are the same family of model, as I am. None built or ran anything. Each read part of its store and says which part; the application's pilot read no product code. So where the four agree it is better than one mind agreeing with itself and well short of four independent witnesses. The agreement I lean on most is the kind that came from each store's own counts and history and not from the pilots' wording: where things had piled up, which typed fields had gone dead, where his words had been kept by hand.

What I read whole, myself: note 28 as Joseph last edited it; note 27 and its feedback; notes 13, 14 and 19; five of this project's memory files; the outline of `core/` and its working notes, and none of its definitions; every file of the four pilots, and the feedback file of a fifth that ran out of room; `slot-rollup.md`; about ten kind sections of `segment-kinds.md`; and, only after writing down my own reading of the pilots, the lead session's synthesis of them.
